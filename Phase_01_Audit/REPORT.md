## 1. Headline judgement

This is **not** a generic finite-difference application paper: it has an independent verification chain (3-D tensor
rotation cross-check, Chebyshev spectral 1-D reference, time-domain Crank–Nicolson reference, test-power cases that
must be able to fail), quantified numerical uncertainty with two grid families and Richardson extrapolation, a
closed-form local-response result that explains the main physical finding, and honest negative results
(the pre-registered collapse in Λ *fails* for the dynamic model, and the paper says so). That combination is
publishable at Q1 level.

The desk-rejection risk does **not** come from the science. It comes from five things a handling editor sees in the
first three minutes: (i) a table row that says the verification suite has **1 failed test** with a broken cell
(`n/a`) and no discussion in the text; (ii) live `[AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED]` and `[TEMPLATE — to be reviewed]`
placeholders, including in the AI-disclosure section; (iii) figure panels that compare "this work" against the
authors' own superseded buggy analysis, plus in-figure text "frozen criterion 10⁻⁸"; (iv) the words "handoff",
"frozen", "working copy", "internal analysis", "AI agent", "Arena.ai Agent Mode", "preliminary" inside the
manuscript body — 20+ occurrences; (v) a validity statement for the Laplace inversion that is broader than the
data support (see A5).
All five are fixable without touching a single result. **No scientific defect that changes a number was found in
Phase 1**; the two items that could change a headline number (A8, A10) are grid/labelling inconsistencies whose
correction requires re-running the ablations at production resolution, which Phase 2 does.
