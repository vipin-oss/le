## B. Facts by paper (section tag in brackets)
### s.5 / s.4.9
- **F30 Nowacki 1978, J. Thermal Stresses 1(2):171-182, 10.1080/01495737808926940.** Uniqueness theorem on the basis of the energy balance, generalized Hamilton principle and reciprocity theorem for thermopiezoelectricity. It builds on Mindlin's classical equations: motion, generalized heat equation T S = k_ij theta_,ij + W, quasistationary D_i,i = 0, with the Duhamel-Neumann relation generalised to piezoelectricity; general anisotropy has 55 material constants (21 elastic, 18 piezoelectric, 6+6, 3 pyroelectric-type, plus c_epsilon) as printed. CLASSICAL (Fourier) theory only. Equations (8)-(10),(16)-(18) [IMG].
- **F31 Chandrasekharaiah 1984, J. Thermal Stresses 7:293-306.** Temperature-rate-dependent (GL-type) thermopiezoelectricity. Entropy inequality p(...) with a nonclassical q_i term; G and phi depend on T, Tdot, T_,i, e_ij, E_i. Linear theory: stress (23) s_ij = c_ijkl e_kl + a_ij(theta + a thetadot) - p_ijk E_k; entropy (24); heat flux (25) **q_i = -theta0 (b_i thetadot + k_ij theta_,j) (Fourier form retained; extra constants a, s, b_i)**; D (26); five coupled field equations (30)-(32) in u, theta, psi. If s>0 (then a>0) the heat equation is wave-type with finite speed ("second sound"); a=s=b_i=0 returns Mindlin's theory; piezo effects off returns Green-Lindsay. Energy equation uses Biot's potential (35)-(36). Also: uniqueness, Hamilton-type variational principle, reciprocal theorem.
- **F32 Cowin-Nunziato 1983, J. Elasticity 13:125-147.** Linear theory of elastic materials with voids: volume fraction change phi is an independent kinematic variable; balance of equilibrated force; uniqueness and weak stability for the mixed problem; waves: transverse waves propagate with no attenuation and no porosity change; **longitudinal waves are attenuated and dispersed**, two distinct longitudinal types (one tied to matrix elasticity, one to voids).
- **F33 Nunziato-Cowin 1979, ARMA 72:175-201.** Nonlinear parent theory; solid-like porous materials that are non-conductors of heat; changes in volume fraction cause internal dissipation (relaxation, creep, hysteresis).
- **F34 Iesan 1986, Acta Mechanica 60:67-89.** Linear thermoelastic materials with voids: uniqueness, reciprocal and variational theorems; acceleration waves: three speeds c1 = [(lambda+2mu)/rho]^(1/2), c2 (porosity-related, expression partly garbled), c3 (printed as (mu/rho)^(1/2)); the longitudinal wave at c1 carries a third-order porosity disturbance and a second-order thermal discontinuity; transverse wave leaves temperature and porosity unaffected. Thermal-wave speed does NOT appear among the three speeds as printed (classical heat law implied; flux law display not read).
- **F35 Marchand-McDevitt-Triggiani 2012, Math. Methods Appl. Sci. (special issue), 10.1002/mma.1576.** Abstract MGT: tau psi_ttt + psi_tt - c^2 lap psi - b lap psi_t = 0 (1.2), b = delta + tau c^2 (delta = diffusivity of sound), RENDER-CHECKED on page 2; abstract model normalises tau=1 with alpha,c^2,b>0. Results: structural decomposition, group generation, spectral analysis (accumulation point in continuous spectrum), exponential decay with explicit sharp rate up to finitely many stable eigenvalues. Context sentences: Kuznetsov/Westervelt/KZK models use Fourier flux; replacing it by Maxwell-Cattaneo gives the Jordan-MGT equation (1.1). ACOUSTIC, not thermoelastic.
### s.8 (inversion and wave formalism)
- **F36 Durbin 1974, Comput. J. 17(4):371-376** (received June 1973; Honig-Hirdes write "1973"). Uses BOTH cosine and sine series; averaging (36) gives error bound independent of t, valid on the whole period (0,2T); for |f|<=C bound is C/(e^{2aT}-1) (37); aT=10 gives about 2e-9 C versus 1e-5 C for Dubner-Abate; Dubner-Abate formula "numerically valid only for t<=T/2" (Durbin); formally a trapezoidal rule with step pi/T. DOI not printed in the PDF (Crossref owed; memory: 10.1093/comjnl/17.4.371).
- **F37 Honig-Hirdes 1984, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 10(1):113-132 ("Algorithm 27", Fortran LAPIN).** Builds on Durbin; combines discretisation-error reduction ("Korrektur"), a convergence-acceleration for non-alternating series, and parameter selection; in one example Durbin's method needed about 60 times more F(s) evaluations and 30 times more CPU than LAPIN for comparable accuracy (problem-specific; do not generalise). Receipt dates: Feb 1980, rev. July 1983.
- **F38 Stroh 1958, Phil. Mag. 3:625-646 (10.1080/14786435808565804).** Anisotropic elasticity for states independent of one Cartesian coordinate (extending Eshelby-Read-Shockley 1953); dislocations, crack in non-uniform stress; most general anisotropy. **Stroh 1962, J. Math. Phys. 41:77-103.** Steady-state motion (subsonic or supersonic), stress functions, sextic equation for p with complex-conjugate roots, critical-speed discussion, surface waves. BOTH PURELY ELASTIC - no thermal or piezo coupling; do not cite them for thermoelastic Stroh formalism.
- **F39 Chadwick-Sneddon 1958, J. Mech. Phys. Solids 6:223-230.** Classical (Fourier) coupled thermoelasticity, harmonic plane waves, dimensionless frequency w/w*, w* = rho c v1^2/k, coupling constant epsilon (small at normal temperatures). Transverse (S) waves undergo no thermal modification; coupled dilatation-temperature gives a quadratic for p with two roots: modified P-wave and modified thermal wave, both dispersed and attenuated. Qualitatively: attenuation of the modified P-wave grows ~ w^2 at low frequency and tends to a constant at high frequency; numerical constants in the OCR text are UNRELIABLE (scan OCR) - do not quote numbers.
- **F40 Joseph-Preziosi 1989, Rev. Mod. Phys. 61:41-73.** Jeffreys-type heat flux with effective conductivity k1 + elastic conductivity k2; Cattaneo law; GK macroscopic equations (Sec. IV); Gurtin-Pipkin (Sec. V); canonical wave problems (VII); annotated chronology (VIII, pp. 51-70). History: infinite speed of diffusion first pointed out by Cattaneo 1948 (also Morse-Feshbach 1953, Vernotte 1958a); **Maxwell 1867 had the time-derivative term in his eq (143) but discarded it ("may be neglected")**; Vernotte 1958a wrote Cattaneo's law as the simplest generalisation; Landau 1941/Ward-Wilks 1951 for second sound as phonon-gas wave; the authors state the relaxation time is thought very small in practice. **Addendum 1990, Rev. Mod. Phys. 62:375-391:** adds missed literature; Mueller (1987) point that Cattaneo's original proposal is history dependence of flux on temperature gradient; mentions nonlocal heat transport, nonlinear thermal waves (Zel'dovich-Raizer), Wilhelm-Choi 1975, two-temperature femtosecond-laser models (Anisimov et al. 1974), Brorson et al. 1987 gold-film transit times.
- **F41 Hetnarski-Ignaczak 1999, J. Thermal Stresses 22:451-476.** Survey of five models: LS, GL, H-I (low-temperature nonlinear, 1996), GN (1993), "C-T" DPL (Chandrasekharaiah-Tzou 1998). Covers domain-of-influence, uniqueness, 1-D closed-form solutions. **Key quotable limitation: except for the rigid-conductor limit none of the five models had been verified by a combined thermoelastic experiment (as of 1999).** (A statement about 1999; later experiments not covered.)
- **F42 Ivanov-Savova 1991, J. Thermal Stresses 14:563-570, 10.1080/01495739108927085.** Authors now known: Tsolo P. Ivanov (Sofia) and Radiianka Savova (BAS Sofia). Classical isotropic thermoelastic surface (Rayleigh-type) waves as superposition of three dispersive inhomogeneous plane waves; stress-free surface with radiative thermal condition (coefficient h); **energy-flux vector (kinetic + internal) is NOT parallel to the surface, its direction varies with depth, magnitude decays with depth and exponentially along the line of intersection of constant-phase planes**; numerics for copper, thermally insulated. Classical (Fourier) only.
### s.11 / s.12
- **F43 Povstenko 2009, J. Math. Sci. 162(2):296-305.** Survey of nonlocal generalisations of Fourier's law. Defines RL integral/derivative and Caputo derivative with Laplace rules (Caputo: L{D^a f} = s^a F(s) - sum f^(k)(0+) s^(a-1-k)); exponential kernel = Cattaneo/Vernotte -> Kaliski and Lord-Shulman; constant kernel = Nigmatullin and Green-Naghdi (wave equation); power kernel -> Caputo time-fractional equation interpolating Fourier (a=1) and wave (a=2); Chen-Gurtin is the two-temperature thermoelasticity cited; Eringen's nonlocal thermoelasticity (exponential kernel in space); first book on generalized thermoelasticity Pidstryhach-Kolyano 1976 (as printed). See C4.
- **F44 Gorenflo-Mainardi 1997, CISM 378, pp. 223-276.** RL calculus via Laplace transforms; Caputo derivative "originally introduced by Caputo in the late sixties and adopted by Caputo and Mainardi in Linear Viscoelasticity"; Caputo derivative gives integer-order initial conditions; fractional relaxation (0<a<1) and oscillation (1<a<2) equations with Mittag-Leffler solutions; **E_a(z) = sum z^n / Gamma(a n + 1), a generalisation of exp by replacing n! with (a n)! = Gamma(a n + 1)** - supports the manuscript's "a! := Gamma(1+a)" convention remark. Appendix: ML functions.
- **F45 Gorenflo-Kilbas-Mainardi-Rogosin 2014, Springer Monographs in Mathematics (full book, 454 pp supplied).** Monograph on Mittag-Leffler functions; Ch. 2 historical, Ch. 3 classical ML function, Ch. 5 three-parameter ML, Ch. 6 mathematics, Ch. 8 role in fractional models. Used as reference-grade source for ML definitions; specific theorems not yet extracted.
- **F46 Wang-Li 2025 (IV), Fractal Fract. 9(12):794 (open access).** Defines the space-dependent derivative (SDD) as a new MDD-type operator to modify unsteady heat diffusion spatially; compares conventional, spatial-FD and SDD models; authors state SDD "has the most expressive force" (self-assessment). Argues FD and MDD reflect only temporal memory; MDD originally a weighted mean of the integer-order derivative over a sliding interval (Definition 1). Diffusion modelling paper, NOT thermoelastic.
- **F47 van der Merwe-van Rensburg-Sieberhagen 2021, Appl. Math. Comput. 396:125934.** 1-D DPL vs Cattaneo-Vernotte vs Fourier by modal analysis; modes overdamped/underdamped; DPL with tau_T < tau_q: first modes overdamped, then underdamped, then all higher modes overdamped again; CV (tau_T=0): higher modes all underdamped and damped at the common rate e^{-gamma t}; modal partial sums converge in an energy norm; heat-pulse model problem; authors note underdamped modes may conflict with the second law "intuitively" and prefer overdamped modes (their remark, not a proof).
- **F48 Chirita-D'Apice-Zampoli 2016, Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 102:226-232.** Time-differential three-phase-lag model recast via fading memory (flux depends on history of thermal-displacement gradient); thermodynamic restrictions on delay times; continuous dependence on initial data and supply; a second estimate with relaxed restrictions allows exponential growth (asymptotic instability). Delay-time inequalities themselves [not extracted] - read before quoting.
- **F49 Chen-Gurtin 1968, ZAMP 19:614-627.** Two-temperature theory for non-simple materials (energy, entropy, heat flux, thermodynamic temperature depend on conductive temperature phi and its first two spatial gradients). Linear relation **theta = phi - a lap phi (1.4)**, a >= 0 if k>0 (second law); linear energy equation (1.3) [garbled]; uniqueness, progressive waves, decay of discontinuities (e^{-kt/...} as printed), semi-infinite bar. Backward problem stable. **Gurtin-Williams 1966, ZAMP 17:626-633.** Second law with two temperatures (volume-relevant phi and surface-relevant theta); for a very large class of materials incl. most materials with memory the duality is inadmissible and reduces to Clausius-Duhem. (Chen-Gurtin cite this as "two temperatures coincide for simple materials".)
