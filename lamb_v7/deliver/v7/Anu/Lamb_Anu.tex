\title{\vspace{-1.2cm}Lamb waves in a magneto-electro-elastic plate:\\
pyroelectric and pyromagnetic coupling, size effect and a heat equation with one lag}
\author{Dr.\ Vipin \\ Gurugram University}
\date{30 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We set up and reduce the plane-strain Lamb problem in a plate of a magneto-electro-elastic composite of the
barium titanate and cobalt ferrite family, poled along the thickness, with free faces at $z=\pm h$. Five
fields are carried: the displacements $u_x,u_z$, the electric potential $\varphi$, the magnetic potential
$\psi$ and the temperature rise $\theta$. The mechanical stress follows the nonlocal strain gradient law on
its elastic part; the piezoelectric, piezomagnetic, dielectric, magnetic and magnetoelectric couplings are
local. The heat equation is of Moore--Gibson--Thompson type in its base form and carries the pyroelectric
and pyromagnetic work together with their electrocaloric and magnetocaloric partners. Effective constants of the composite are carried as a band from two homogenisation routes. The five field equations
are reduced to a $5\times5$ matrix in the thickness exponent, its characteristic polynomial is counted and
its parity decided, the amplitude ratios are given through the cofactors, the seven face conditions are
derived with open and closed electric and magnetic faces, and the dispersion matrix is assembled. Three
hand limits close the file.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} guided waves; magneto-electro-elastic plate; magnetoelectric coefficient;
pyroelectric and pyromagnetic coupling; nonlocal strain gradient elasticity.

\section{Introduction}

A magneto-electro-elastic material is one in which a strain produces both an electric polarisation and a
magnetisation, and in which an electric field can produce a magnetic response and the other way round. No
single phase does this well; the usual route is a composite of a piezoelectric phase, barium titanate in
our case, and a piezomagnetic one, cobalt ferrite, sintered together. The magnetoelectric coefficient of
the composite is a product property, present only because the two phases are in contact, and it is much
larger than the product of the two bulk effects would suggest.

Guided waves in magneto-electro-elastic plates have been computed, and the plate with a temperature field
has been computed too, though separately. Xiao, Han, Liu and Li solve guided waves in a functionally graded
magneto-electro-elastic plate with no thermal field at all \cite{Xiao2016}. Kondaiah, Shankar and Ganesan
keep both the pyroelectric and the pyromagnetic coefficients in a magneto-electro-elastic plate
\cite{Kondaiah2013}, and Tocci Monaco, Fantuzzi, Fabbrocino and Luciano keep both in a nonlocal strain
gradient nanoplate with hygro-thermal loads \cite{Tocci2021}; those two are free-vibration, buckling and
static problems, not complex-wavenumber dispersion. Matlam and Kedar put a Moore--Gibson--Thompson heat
equation with memory and nonlocality into an electro-magneto-thermoelastic medium \cite{Matlam2026}, and
Kakar and Kakar treat edge waves in an electromagnetic thermoelastic plate \cite{Kakar2015}; both were
available to us only as title and abstract.

The constitutive law of a linear magneto-electro-elastic medium is standard and is written in the layered
plate setting by Chen, Pan and Chen \cite{Chen2007}; the sign convention for the magnetoelectric
coefficient and the form of the plate conditions are taken from the way that literature writes them, and
the scholar must open the paper before quoting an equation number, because only the bibliographic data
came through for me.

What is claimed here is a plate problem in which the heat equation carries a relaxation time and both the
pyroelectric and the pyromagnetic couplings are present, with the mechanical size effect, and with the
uncertainty of the effective magnetoelectric coefficient carried openly as a band between two
homogenisation routes. The corner is narrow: the paper has to show, on one figure, that the attenuation it
predicts is larger than the spread between the two routes. If it is not, the honest outcome is a negative
result about the measurability of the effect, and that is a result.

\section{Setting and assumptions}\label{sec:setting}

The plate occupies $-h\le z\le h$, is unbounded in $x$ and $y$, and is poled along $z$, so the effective
medium is transversely isotropic with the axis along the thickness. Plane strain applies, $u_y=0$,
$\partial_y=0$. The electric and magnetic potentials give the fields by $E_i=-\varphi_{,i}$ and
$H_i=-\psi_{,i}$, and one plane wave travels along $x$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl(u_x,u_z,\varphi,\psi,\theta\bigr)
=\bigl(\hat u_x,\hat u_z,\hat\varphi,\hat\psi,\hat\theta\bigr)(z)\;
\exp\bigl[\ii(kx-\omega t)\bigr],
\qquad k=k_r+\ii k_i .
\end{equation}

The assumptions: linear magneto-electro-elasticity at the reference temperature with effective constants
of the composite, held fixed; a small temperature rise; no free charge and no free current in the bulk, so
both Gauss laws are sourceless; no pre-stress; and the two size lengths on the elastic part of the
mechanical stress only. The last point is a modelling choice with a reason: it keeps $D_i$ and $B_i$
second order in $z$, so the electric and magnetic problems remain Poisson-like and need no extra face
condition, and it keeps the size effect mechanical, so the extra conditions are mechanical too.

The magnetoelectric coefficient deserves its unit spelled out, because it is where sign and unit slips live. With $D_i=e_{ikl}\varepsilon_{kl}+\epsilon_{ik}E_k+\alpha_{ik}H_k+p_i\theta$, the term
$\alpha H$ must have the unit of $D$, which is C\,m$^{-2}$; $H$ is in A\,m$^{-1}$, so $\alpha$ is in
C\,A$^{-1}$m$^{-1}$, and since the ampere is a coulomb per second this is s\,m$^{-1}$. A positive
$\alpha_{33}$ means that a magnetic field along the axis adds electric displacement along the same axis.
The sign depends on which phase is taken as the matrix and on the poling direction, so both must be stated
with the number.

\section{Source equations}\label{sec:source}

Kinematics, as usual,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{ij}=\tfrac12(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i}),\qquad E_i=-\varphi_{,i},\qquad H_i=-\psi_{,i}.
\end{equation}

The constitutive relations of a linear magneto-electro-elastic medium with thermal terms,
\begin{align}
\bigl(1-l_{\rm nl}^2\nabla^2\bigr)t_{ij}&==\sigma_{ij}, \\
\sigma_{ij}&==C_{ijkl}\bigl(1-l_g^2\nabla^2\bigr)\varepsilon_{kl} \\
-e_{kij}E_k-h_{kij}H_k-\beta_{ij}\theta,\label{eq:C1} \\
D_i&==e_{ikl}\varepsilon_{kl}+\epsilon_{ik}E_k+\alpha_{ik}H_k \\
\quad +p_i\theta,\label{eq:C2} \\
B_i&==h_{ikl}\varepsilon_{kl}+\alpha_{ik}E_k+\mu_{ik}H_k \\
\quad +m_i\theta .\label{eq:C3}
\end{align}
The mechanical part without the size effect and without temperature is the standard law of the layered
plate literature \cite{Chen2007}; the pyroelectric and pyromagnetic terms are kept in plate work by
\cite{Kondaiah2013,Tocci2021}, and the fact that they are kept is worth stating because an older claim
that they are usually dropped is not true. The size-effect pair is from Lim, Zhang and Reddy through the
introduction of Barretta and Marotti de Sciarra \cite[Eqs.~(18)--(19)]{Barretta2018}; the Lim paper itself
was closed to us \cite{Lim2015}.

The field equations,
\begin{align}
\bigl(1-l_{\rm nl}^2\nabla^2\bigr)\rho\,\ddot u_i&=\sigma_{ij,j},\label{eq:mom}\\
D_{i,i}&=0,\qquad B_{i,i}=0,\label{eq:gauss}\\
\Bigl(1+\tau_q\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Bigr)
\Bigl[\rho c_p\dot\theta+T_0\beta_{ij}\dot\varepsilon_{ij}
+T_0p_i\dot E_i+T_0m_i\dot H_i\Bigr]&=k_{\rm th}\nabla^2\theta .\label{eq:heat}
\end{align}
The heat equation is the Moore--Gibson--Thompson base form with the conductivity rate put to zero, the same
operator used in this literature \cite{Matlam2026,Gupta2025}; I read the title and the abstract of both.
The electrocaloric and magnetocaloric terms are the reciprocal partners of the pyroelectric and
pyromagnetic terms: dropping them while keeping $p_i$ and $m_i$ would break reciprocity. That is a
textbook statement, not a discovery, and it belongs in one sentence of the model section.

\section{Calculation A: constitutive relations in components}\label{sec:calcA}

\begin{worked}
The effective medium is transversely isotropic with the axis along $z$, so the moduli are those of class
$6mm$: $C_{11}=C_{22}$, $C_{13}=C_{23}$, $C_{33}$, $C_{44}=C_{55}$, $C_{66}=(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$, and every
mixed element vanishes. The piezoelectric tensor has $e_{31}=e_{32}$, $e_{33}$, $e_{15}=e_{24}$; the
piezomagnetic tensor has the same shape with $h$ in place of $e$; the dielectric and magnetic tensors have
two entries each; the magnetoelectric tensor of this class is diagonal in the plane,
$\alpha_{11}=\alpha_{22}$ and $\alpha_{33}$. Plane strain removes $y$.
\end{worked}

\subsection*{The three stresses and the four inductions, one at a time}

\begin{worked}
$\sigma_{xx}=\Gcal(C_{11}\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{13}\varepsilon_{zz})-e_{31}E_z-h_{31}H_z-\beta_1\theta$.
Plane strain kills the $C_{12}$ term. Substitution: nothing to substitute yet. Collect: one stiffness pair
carrying the gradient factor, one electric term, one magnetic term, one thermal term. Result: the first
line below. The gradient factor sits only on the elastic stiffness, which is the modelling choice of
Section~\ref{sec:setting}: the electric and magnetic problems stay second order and need no extra face
condition.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
$\sigma_{zz}=\Gcal(C_{13}\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{33}\varepsilon_{zz})-e_{33}E_z-h_{33}H_z-\beta_3\theta$, the
same shape with the axial coefficients. Result: the second line.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
$\sigma_{xz}=2C_{44}\Gcal\varepsilon_{xz}-e_{15}E_x-h_{15}H_x$. The factor $2$ belongs to the engineering
shear, because the two-index notation carries $\sigma_{xz}=C_{55}\,2\varepsilon_{xz}$ and $C_{55}=C_{44}$.
Result: the third line.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
$D_x=e_{15}\,2\varepsilon_{xz}+\epsilon_{11}E_x+\alpha_{11}H_x$ and
$D_z=e_{31}\varepsilon_{xx}+e_{33}\varepsilon_{zz}+\epsilon_{33}E_z+\alpha_{33}H_z+p_3\theta$. The
magnetoelectric terms are $\alpha_{11}H_x$ and $\alpha_{33}H_z$: a magnetic field produces an electric
displacement, which is the direct magnetoelectric effect, and $\alpha$ is symmetric in the energy, so the
same coefficient appears in the magnetic induction below. Result: the fourth line.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
$B_x=h_{15}\,2\varepsilon_{xz}+\alpha_{11}E_x+\mu_{11}H_x$ and
$B_z=h_{31}\varepsilon_{xx}+h_{33}\varepsilon_{zz}+\alpha_{33}E_z+\mu_{33}H_z+m_3\theta$. The same
$\alpha$ appears, and that is the Onsager symmetry of the coupled energy rather than a second measurement;
it is also the reason one symbol serves both rows and why the parity discussion of
Section~\ref{sec:calcD} sees the same entry twice. Result: the fifth line.
\end{worked}

\begin{align}
\sigma_{xx}&==\Gcal\bigl(C_{11}\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{13}\varepsilon_{zz}\bigr) \\
\quad -e_{31}E_z-h_{31}H_z-\beta_1\theta, \\
\sigma_{zz}&==\Gcal\bigl(C_{13}\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{33}\varepsilon_{zz}\bigr) \\
\quad -e_{33}E_z-h_{33}H_z-\beta_3\theta, \\
\sigma_{xz}&==2C_{44}\Gcal\,\varepsilon_{xz}-e_{15}E_x-h_{15}H_x, \\
D_x&==e_{15}\,2\varepsilon_{xz}+\epsilon_{11}E_x+\alpha_{11}H_x, \\
D_z&==e_{31}\varepsilon_{xx}+e_{33}\varepsilon_{zz}+\epsilon_{33}E_z \\
\quad +\alpha_{33}H_z+p_3\theta, \\
B_x&==h_{15}\,2\varepsilon_{xz}+\alpha_{11}E_x+\mu_{11}H_x, \\
B_z&==h_{31}\varepsilon_{xx}+h_{33}\varepsilon_{zz}+\alpha_{33}E_z \\
\quad +\mu_{33}H_z+m_3\theta .
\end{align}

\section{Calculations B and C: the five equations and the matrix}\label{sec:calcBC}

With $\nabla^2\to L=\eta^2-k^2$,
\begin{equation}
\Ncal(\eta)=1+l_{\rm nl}^2(k^2-\eta^2),\qquad \Gcal(\eta)=1+l_g^2(k^2-\eta^2),
\end{equation}
and amplitudes $\mathbf a=(\hat u_x,\hat u_z,\hat\varphi,\hat\psi,\hat\theta)^T$, the five equations are
$M(\eta)\mathbf a=\mathbf 0$.

\subsection*{Row 1: momentum in $x$}

\begin{worked}
The balance is $\Ncal\rho\ddot u_x=\partial_x\sigma_{xx}+\partial_z\sigma_{xz}$, written as
$-\Ncal\rho\omega^2X-\partial_x\hat\sigma_{xx}-\partial_z\hat\sigma_{xz}=0$.
Substitution: $\varepsilon_{xx}=\ii kX$, $\varepsilon_{zz}=\eta Z$, $2\varepsilon_{xz}=\eta X+\ii kZ$,
$E_x=-\ii k\Phi$, $E_z=-\eta\Phi$, $H_x=-\ii k\Psi$, $H_z=-\eta\Psi$.
First divergence: $-\partial_x\hat\sigma_{xx}
=-\ii k\bigl[\Gcal(C_{11}\ii kX+C_{13}\eta Z)+\eta e_{31}\Phi+\eta h_{31}\Psi-\beta_1\Theta\bigr]
=\Gcal C_{11}k^2X-\ii k\eta\Gcal C_{13}Z-\ii k\eta e_{31}\Phi-\ii k\eta h_{31}\Psi+\ii k\beta_1\Theta$,
where the sign of the two potential terms comes from $-e_{31}E_z$ with $E_z=-\eta\Phi$.
Second divergence: $-\partial_z\hat\sigma_{xz}
=-\eta\bigl[\Gcal C_{44}(\eta X+\ii kZ)+\ii ke_{15}\Phi+\ii kh_{15}\Psi\bigr]
=-\Gcal C_{44}\eta^2X-\ii k\eta\Gcal C_{44}Z+\eta ke_{15}\Phi+\eta kh_{15}\Psi$.
Collect $X$: $-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal C_{11}k^2-\Gcal C_{44}\eta^2
=-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal(C_{11}k^2-C_{44}\eta^2)$. That is $M_{11}$.
Collect $Z$: $-\ii k\eta\Gcal(C_{13}+C_{44})$. That is $M_{12}$.
Collect $\Phi$: $-\ii k\eta(e_{31}+e_{15})$. That is $M_{13}$.
Collect $\Psi$: $-\ii k\eta(h_{31}+h_{15})$. That is $M_{14}$.
Collect $\Theta$: $+\ii k\beta_1$. That is $M_{15}$.
Result: row 1. The electric and magnetic entries have exactly the same shape, which is what makes the
magnetoelectric bookkeeping symmetric in the rows below.
\end{worked}

\begin{align}
M_{11}&==-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal\bigl(C_{11}k^2-C_{44}\eta^2\bigr), \\
M_{12}&==-\ii k\eta\Gcal(C_{13}+C_{44}), \\
M_{13}&==-\ii k\eta(e_{31}+e_{15}), \\
M_{14}&==-\ii k\eta(h_{31}+h_{15}), \\
M_{15}&==\ii k\beta_1 .
\end{align}

\subsection*{Row 2: momentum in $z$}

\begin{worked}
The balance is $-\Ncal\rho\omega^2Z-\partial_x\hat\sigma_{xz}-\partial_z\hat\sigma_{zz}=0$.
First divergence: $-\partial_x\hat\sigma_{xz}
=-\ii k\bigl[\Gcal C_{44}(\eta X+\ii kZ)+\ii ke_{15}\Phi+\ii kh_{15}\Psi\bigr]
=-\ii k\eta\Gcal C_{44}X+\Gcal C_{44}k^2Z+e_{15}k^2\Phi+h_{15}k^2\Psi$,
using $-\ii k\cdot\ii k=+k^2$.
Second divergence: $-\partial_z\hat\sigma_{zz}
=-\eta\bigl[\Gcal(C_{13}\ii kX+C_{33}\eta Z)+\eta e_{33}\Phi+\eta h_{33}\Psi-\beta_3\Theta\bigr]
=-\ii k\eta\Gcal C_{13}X-\Gcal C_{33}\eta^2Z-e_{33}\eta^2\Phi-h_{33}\eta^2\Psi+\eta\beta_3\Theta$.
Collect $X$: $-\ii k\eta\Gcal(C_{44}+C_{13})$. That is $M_{21}$, equal to $M_{12}$.
Collect $Z$: $-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal C_{44}k^2-\Gcal C_{33}\eta^2
=-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal(C_{44}k^2-C_{33}\eta^2)$. That is $M_{22}$.
Collect $\Phi$: $e_{15}k^2-e_{33}\eta^2$. That is $M_{23}=-M_{32}$.
Collect $\Psi$: $h_{15}k^2-h_{33}\eta^2$. That is $M_{24}=-M_{42}$.
Collect $\Theta$: $+\eta\beta_3$. That is $M_{25}$, odd, the partner of $M_{52}$.
Result: row 2.
\end{worked}

\begin{align}
M_{21}&==-\ii k\eta\Gcal(C_{13}+C_{44}), \\
M_{22}&==-\Ncal\rho\omega^2+\Gcal\bigl(C_{44}k^2-C_{33}\eta^2\bigr), \\
M_{23}&==e_{15}k^2-e_{33}\eta^2, \\
M_{24}&==h_{15}k^2-h_{33}\eta^2, \\
M_{25}&==\eta\beta_3 .
\end{align}

\subsection*{Row 3: electric Gauss law}

\begin{worked}
Gauss' law is $\partial_xD_x+\partial_zD_z=0$; there is no free charge in a dielectric.
Substitution: $D_x=e_{15}(\eta X+\ii kZ)-\ii k\epsilon_{11}\Phi-\ii k\alpha_{11}\Psi$ and
$D_z=\ii ke_{31}X+\eta e_{33}Z-\eta\epsilon_{33}\Phi-\eta\alpha_{33}\Psi+p_3\Theta$.
First term: $\partial_x\hat D_x=\ii k\bigl[e_{15}(\eta X+\ii kZ)-\ii k\epsilon_{11}\Phi
-\ii k\alpha_{11}\Psi\bigr]=\ii k\eta e_{15}X-e_{15}k^2Z+\epsilon_{11}k^2\Phi+\alpha_{11}k^2\Psi$.
Second term: $\partial_z\hat D_z=\eta\bigl[\ii ke_{31}X+\eta e_{33}Z-\eta\epsilon_{33}\Phi
-\eta\alpha_{33}\Psi+p_3\Theta\bigr]
=\ii k\eta e_{31}X+e_{33}\eta^2Z-\epsilon_{33}\eta^2\Phi-\alpha_{33}\eta^2\Psi+\eta p_3\Theta$.
Collect $X$: $\ii k\eta(e_{15}+e_{31})$. That is $M_{31}=-M_{13}$.
Collect $Z$: $e_{33}\eta^2-e_{15}k^2$. That is $M_{32}=-M_{23}$.
Collect $\Phi$: $\epsilon_{33}\eta^2-\epsilon_{11}k^2$. That is $M_{33}$, even in $\eta$.
Collect $\Psi$: $\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2$. That is $M_{34}$, the magnetoelectric entry, even in
$\eta$, and identical to $M_{43}$ below.
Collect $\Theta$: $\eta p_3$. That is $M_{35}$, odd.
Result: row 3.
\end{worked}

\begin{equation}
M_{31}=\ii k\eta(e_{15}+e_{31}),\quad
M_{32}=e_{33}\eta^2-e_{15}k^2,\quad
M_{33}=\epsilon_{33}\eta^2-\epsilon_{11}k^2,\quad
M_{34}=\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2,\quad
M_{35}=\eta p_3 .
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Row 4: magnetic Gauss law}

\begin{worked}
The magnetic law is $\partial_xB_x+\partial_zB_z=0$, and there is no magnetic monopole. The steps are
identical to row 3 under the replacements $D\to B$, $e\to h$, $\epsilon\to\mu$ and $p_3\to m_3$, with
$\alpha$ staying where it is. Substitution:
$B_x=h_{15}(\eta X+\ii kZ)-\ii k\alpha_{11}\Phi-\ii k\mu_{11}\Psi$ and
$B_z=\ii kh_{31}X+\eta h_{33}Z-\eta\alpha_{33}\Phi-\eta\mu_{33}\Psi+m_3\Theta$.
Collect $X$: $\ii k\eta(h_{15}+h_{31})$. That is $M_{41}=-M_{14}$.
Collect $Z$: $h_{33}\eta^2-h_{15}k^2$. That is $M_{42}=-M_{24}$.
Collect $\Phi$: $\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2$. That is $M_{43}$, and it equals $M_{34}$; the equality
is the Onsager symmetry of the magnetoelectric energy, not a separate assumption, and it is what keeps
the matrix from becoming non-conservative.
Collect $\Psi$: $\mu_{33}\eta^2-\mu_{11}k^2$. That is $M_{44}$.
Collect $\Theta$: $\eta m_3$. That is $M_{45}$, the pyromagnetic entry.
Result: row 4.
\end{worked}

\begin{equation}
M_{41}=\ii k\eta(h_{15}+h_{31}),\quad
M_{42}=h_{33}\eta^2-h_{15}k^2,\quad
M_{43}=\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2,\quad
M_{44}=\mu_{33}\eta^2-\mu_{11}k^2,\quad
M_{45}=\eta m_3 .
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Row 5: the heat equation}

\begin{worked}
Put $f=1-\ii\omega\tau_q$. The heat equation is of Moore--Gibson--Thompson type, so the sources carry $f$
and the conduction term does not. Write it with the sources on the left:
$f\bigl[T_0\beta_1\dot\varepsilon_{xx}+T_0\beta_3\dot\varepsilon_{zz}+T_0p_3\dot E_z
+T_0m_3\dot H_z\bigr]+k_{\rm th}L\,\Theta+\ii\omega f\rho c_p\,\Theta=0$.
Substitution, one source at a time.
$\dot\varepsilon_{xx}=-\ii\omega\,\ii kX=\omega kX$, since $(-\ii)(\ii)=+1$, giving
$M_{51}=fT_0\omega k\beta_1$.
$\dot\varepsilon_{zz}=-\ii\omega\,\eta Z$, giving $M_{52}=-\ii\omega fT_0\beta_3\eta$.
$\dot E_z=-\ii\omega(-\eta\Phi)=+\ii\omega\eta\Phi$, giving $M_{53}=\ii\omega fT_0p_3\eta$.
$\dot H_z=-\ii\omega(-\eta\Psi)=+\ii\omega\eta\Psi$, giving $M_{54}=\ii\omega fT_0m_3\eta$.
The $\Theta$ terms collect to $-\ii\omega f\rho c_p-k_{\rm th}L$, which is $M_{55}$.
Result: row 5. The electrocaloric and magnetocaloric entries have the same shape and both are odd in
$\eta$, so both fit the parity matching.
\end{worked}

\begin{equation}
M_{51}=fT_0\omega k\beta_1,\quad
M_{52}=-\ii\omega fT_0\beta_3\eta,\quad
M_{53}=\ii\omega fT_0p_3\eta,\quad
M_{54}=\ii\omega fT_0m_3\eta,\quad
M_{55}=-\ii\omega f\rho c_p-k_{\rm th}L .
\end{equation}

\section{Calculation D: degree and parity}\label{sec:calcD}

\begin{worked}
Degrees by entry: row 1 is four, three, three, three, one; row 2 is three, four, two, two, one; row 3 is
one, two, two, two, one; row 4 is one, two, two, two, one; row 5 is zero, one, one, one, two. The diagonal
sum is $4+4+2+2+2=14$ and no permutation improves on it, so the characteristic polynomial has degree $14$
when $l_g\ne0$ and $10$ when $l_g=0$.
For the parity, write the parity of every entry first. Each entry is a constant times a power of $\eta$, so
the power alone decides it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & \hat u_x & \hat u_z & \hat\varphi & \hat\psi & \hat\theta\\\hline
\text{row 1} & + & - & - & - & +\\
\text{row 2} & - & + & + & + & -\\
\text{row 3} & - & + & + & + & -\\
\text{row 4} & - & + & + & + & -\\
\text{row 5} & + & - & - & - & +
\end{array}
\end{equation}
with $+$ even and $-$ odd; there are no zero entries here. Now look for signs $\sigma_i,\tau_j$ with
$M_{ij}(-\eta)=\sigma_i\tau_jM_{ij}(\eta)$.
Set $\tau_1=+1$: the odd entries $M_{12},M_{13},M_{14}$ give $\sigma_1\tau_2=\sigma_1\tau_3=\sigma_1\tau_4=-1$
and the even $M_{15}$ gives $\sigma_1\tau_5=+1$. Take $\sigma_1=+1$, so $\tau_2=\tau_3=\tau_4=-1$ and
$\tau_5=+1$. Row 2 then fixes $\sigma_2=-1$, and its even entries $M_{23},M_{24}$ need
$\sigma_2\tau_3=\sigma_2\tau_4=+1$, true; its odd $M_{25}$ needs $\sigma_2\tau_5=-1$, true. Row 3 fixes
$\sigma_3=-1$ from the odd $M_{31}$; the odd $M_{35}$ needs $\sigma_3\tau_5=-1$, true; the even
$M_{33},M_{34}$ are satisfied. Row 4 gives $\sigma_4=-1$ the same way. Row 5: the odd
$M_{52},M_{53},M_{54}$ need $\sigma_5\tau_j=-1$, which holds with $\sigma_5=+1$; the even $M_{51},M_{55}$
are fine. The matching closes.
\end{worked}

So the determinant is even in $\eta$ for all settings, the roots come in pairs, and the plate modes split
into a symmetric and an antisymmetric family. Each family can be posed with seven roots and seven rows, and
the two routes must agree. The magnetoelectric coupling does not break the split, because it enters as
$\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2$, even, and in the same form in both the electric and the magnetic Gauss
rows, so $M_{34}$ and $M_{43}$ carry the same parity as the diagonal entries beside them. The pyromagnetic
and pyroelectric constants enter as $\eta\,m_3$ and $\eta\,p_3$, both odd, and fit the matching.

This too is not a claim about one setting. The five couplings $e$, $h$, $\alpha$, $p_3$ and $m_3$ were
switched on and off independently, all thirty-two combinations, and in every one of them the determinant
comes out even in $\eta$ and the sign matching closes. Nothing in this plate breaks the mirror about the
mid-plane, whatever the coupling, and the two seven-by-seven family matrices can always be written.

\section{Calculation E: amplitude ratios and the solution}\label{sec:calcE}

\begin{worked}
For each root, take a column of the cofactor matrix $C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\mathcal M_{ji}$, which is a null
vector of $M(\eta_m)$ because $M\,C=(\det M)I=0$ at the root. The safest column is the one belonging to the
row with the largest norm at that root, and the arbitrary scale is absorbed in the amplitude $A_m$.
\end{worked}

\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}u_x\\u_z\\\varphi\\\psi\\\theta\end{pmatrix}
=\sum_{m=1}^{14}A_m
\begin{pmatrix}X_m\\Z_m\\\Phi_m\\\Psi_m\\\Theta_m\end{pmatrix}
e^{\eta_mz}e^{\ii(kx-\omega t)} .

\section{Calculation F: the seven face conditions}\label{sec:calcF}

\begin{worked}
Seven conditions per face: the normal and shear tractions with the surface-gradient term and the nonlocal
operator inverted root by root, the two double tractions, the normal electric displacement, the normal
magnetic induction, and the temperature condition. The traction and double-traction construction is the
one of \cite[Sec.~2, Eq.~(14)]{Charal2019}.
Two switches must be stated in the paper, not buried: an electrically open face uses $D_z=0$, a closed one
uses $\Phi=0$; a magnetically open face uses $B_z=0$, a closed one uses $\Psi=0$. The four combinations are
all defensible and they give different attenuation, so the paper must name the one it uses and show one
figure for the extreme pair.
\end{worked}

\begin{align}
b_1&==\frac{1}{\Ncal_m}\Bigl[\hat\sigma_{zz}-l_g^2\ii k\eta\,C_{44}(\eta X_m \\
\quad +\ii kZ_m)\Bigr], \\
b_2&==\frac{1}{\Ncal_m}\Bigl[\hat\sigma_{xz}-l_g^2\ii k\eta\,(\ii kC_{11}X_m \\
\quad +\eta C_{13}Z_m)\Bigr], \\
b_3&==\frac{l_g^2}{\Ncal_m}\eta\,C_{44}(\eta X_m+\ii kZ_m), \\
b_4&==\frac{l_g^2}{\Ncal_m}\eta\,(\ii kC_{13}X_m+\eta C_{33}Z_m), \\
b_5&==\ii ke_{31}X_m+\eta e_{33}Z_m-\eta\epsilon_{33}\Phi_m \\
\quad -\eta\alpha_{33}\Psi_m+p_3\Theta_m, \\
b_6&==\ii kh_{31}X_m+\eta h_{33}Z_m-\eta\alpha_{33}\Phi_m-\eta\mu_{33}\Psi_m \\
\quad +m_3\Theta_m, \\
b_7&==\eta\Theta_m .
\end{align}

\subsection*{The seven rows, one at a time}

\begin{worked}
Row $b_1$. On a face of a strain-gradient solid the normal traction is not $\sigma_{zz}$ alone; the
surface-gradient term is the tangential divergence of the double traction. Substitution: insert
$\hat\sigma_{zz}=\Gcal(C_{13}\ii kX_m+C_{33}\eta Z_m)+\eta e_{33}\Phi_m+\eta h_{33}\Psi_m-\beta_3\Theta_m$
and the surface-gradient term $-l_g^2\ii k\eta C_{44}(\eta X_m+\ii kZ_m)$, then divide by $\Ncal_m$, the
nonlocal operator being inverted root by root. Collect: five amplitudes. Result: the first row function.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row $b_2$. The same construction for the shear traction, with
$\hat\sigma_{xz}=\Gcal C_{44}(\eta X_m+\ii kZ_m)+\ii ke_{15}\Phi_m+\ii kh_{15}\Psi_m$ and the
surface-gradient term $-l_g^2\ii k\eta(\ii kC_{11}X_m+\eta C_{13}Z_m)$. Collect and divide by $\Ncal_m$.
Result: the second row function.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Rows $b_3$ and $b_4$ are the double tractions, $l_g^2\eta C_{44}(\eta X_m+\ii kZ_m)$ and
$l_g^2\eta(\ii kC_{13}X_m+\eta C_{33}Z_m)$, both divided by $\Ncal_m$. Both vanish with $l_g$, which is
why five rows suffice when $l_g=0$. Result: the third and fourth row functions.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row $b_5$ is $\hat D_z$ at the face. Substitution:
$\hat D_z=\ii ke_{31}X_m+\eta e_{33}Z_m-\eta\epsilon_{33}\Phi_m-\eta\alpha_{33}\Psi_m+p_3\Theta_m$.
Collect: five terms, the magnetoelectric one being $-\eta\alpha_{33}\Psi_m$. Setting the row to zero is
the electrically open condition; the closed alternative replaces it by $\Phi_m=0$. Result: the fifth row
function.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row $b_6$ is $\hat B_z$ at the face. Substitution:
$\hat B_z=\ii kh_{31}X_m+\eta h_{33}Z_m-\eta\alpha_{33}\Phi_m-\eta\mu_{33}\Psi_m+m_3\Theta_m$. Collect:
five terms, with $-\eta\alpha_{33}\Phi_m$ carrying the same $\alpha$ as row $b_5$, which is the Onsager
symmetry and not a second measurement. Setting the row to zero is the magnetically open condition; the
closed alternative replaces it by $\Psi_m=0$. Result: the sixth row function. The two switches, electric
and magnetic, are independent, so there are four face combinations and not two; the paper must name the
one it uses and show a figure for the extreme pair, because the attenuation differs between them.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row $b_7$ is $\partial_z\theta=0$, an adiabatic face. Substitution: $\eta\Theta_m$. An isothermal face
replaces it by $\Theta_m$. Result: the seventh row function.
\end{worked}

\section{Calculation G: the dispersion matrix}\label{sec:calcG}

\begin{equation}
\mathbf B(k,\omega)=
\begin{pmatrix}
b_r(\eta_m)\,e^{\eta_mh}\\
b_r(\eta_m)\,e^{-\eta_mh}
\end{pmatrix}_{r=1\dots7,\;m=1\dots14},
\qquad \det\mathbf B(k,\omega)=0 ,
\end{equation}
solved for complex $k$ at real $\omega$, with $k_i$ the attenuation per unit length. When $l_g=0$ the rows

\section{Calculation H: three hand limits}\label{sec:calcH}

\subsection*{The magnetoelectric coupling off}

\begin{worked}
Set $\alpha_{11}=\alpha_{33}=0$ and look at what the matrix becomes. Substitution: $M_{34}$ and $M_{43}$
vanish, so the electric and the magnetic Gauss rows no longer see each other; $M_{33}$ keeps
$\epsilon_{33}\eta^2-\epsilon_{11}k^2$ and $M_{44}$ keeps $\mu_{33}\eta^2-\mu_{11}k^2$. Collect: the
electric row still couples $u_x,u_z$ to $\Phi$ through $e_{31},e_{33},e_{15}$, and the magnetic row does
the same through $h_{31},h_{33},h_{15}$; the mechanical rows carry both. Result: two independent
stiffening mechanisms acting on the same elastic block.
The stiffening is worth writing out, because it is the number a measurement returns. Take the bulk shear
wave at $\eta=0$, where the surviving system in $(Z,\Phi,\Psi)$ is
$(-\rho\omega^2+C_{44}k^2)Z+e_{15}k^2\Phi+h_{15}k^2\Psi=0$,
$-e_{15}k^2Z+\epsilon_{11}k^2\Phi=0$ and $-h_{15}k^2Z+\mu_{11}k^2\Psi=0$.
Substitution: eliminate $\Phi=e_{15}Z/\epsilon_{11}$ and $\Psi=h_{15}Z/\mu_{11}$. Collect:
$\rho\omega^2=k^2(C_{44}+e_{15}^2/\epsilon_{11}+h_{15}^2/\mu_{11})$. Result: with $\alpha=0$ the two
stiffenings simply add, each with its own coupling factor $e_{15}^2/(C_{44}\epsilon_{11})$ and
$h_{15}^2/(C_{44}\mu_{11})$. That is the piezoelectric plus piezomagnetic plate, and its dispersion must
be what a piezoelectric plate with one extra stiffening gives.
\end{worked}

\subsection*{Piezoelectric and piezomagnetic coupling both off}

\begin{worked}
With $e=h=\alpha=p=m=0$ the two Gauss rows reduce to $\epsilon_{ii}$ and $\mu_{ii}$ terms and force the
potentials to vanish; the mechanical rows reduce to the thermoelastic plate with the size effect. The
dispersion must then be the thermoelastic Rayleigh--Lamb problem.
\end{worked}

\subsection*{The thin-plate low-frequency limit and the effective magnetoelectric coefficient}

\begin{worked}
For a thin plate at low frequency the fields through the thickness are nearly uniform, so $D_z=0$ and
$B_z=0$ hold through the thickness and not only at the faces. Substitution: write the two conditions, at
zero strain for clarity, as a $2\times2$ system for the two potentials,
$\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{33}&\alpha_{33}\\ \alpha_{33}&\mu_{33}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}E_z\\ H_z\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}p_3\\ m_3\end{pmatrix}\theta$.
Its determinant is $\Delta_m=\epsilon_{33}\mu_{33}-\alpha_{33}^2$.
Collect: solving, $E_z=-\theta(p_3\mu_{33}-m_3\alpha_{33})/\Delta_m$ and
$H_z=-\theta(m_3\epsilon_{33}-p_3\alpha_{33})/\Delta_m$, so a temperature change drives both fields, and
each is driven by both pyro-coefficients once $\alpha\ne0$.
Now restore the strain. The full system is
$\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{33}&\alpha_{33}\\ \alpha_{33}&\mu_{33}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}E_z\\ H_z\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}e_{31}\\ h_{31}\end{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{xx}
-\begin{pmatrix}e_{33}\\ h_{33}\end{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{zz}
-\begin{pmatrix}p_3\\ m_3\end{pmatrix}\theta$, and inverting it gives both potentials as linear functions
of the two strains and the temperature. Substituting back into $\sigma_{xx}$ and $\sigma_{zz}$ gives the
effective stiffnesses, each corrected by a term of the form
$(e,\ h)\,\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&\alpha\\ \alpha&\mu\end{pmatrix}^{-1}(e,\ h)^{T}$, and it is that
matrix inverse, not $\alpha$ on its own, that sets the size of the effect in the plate.
Result: the effective magnetoelectric coefficient of the plate, defined as the magnetic induction produced
per unit applied electric field at zero strain and zero stress, is
$\alpha_{\rm eff}=\alpha_{33}+\dfrac{(h_{33}\epsilon_{33}-e_{33}\alpha_{33})(e_{33}\mu_{33}-h_{33}\alpha_{33})}
{\epsilon_{33}\mu_{33}-\alpha_{33}^2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\ }$ evaluated at the strain-free state, which is
$\alpha_{33}$ itself; the strain-mediated part appears only when the mechanical boundary conditions are
imposed, and then the full expression above applies. The denominator is the one to watch: it is positive
only for $\alpha_{33}^2<\epsilon_{33}\mu_{33}$, so the magnetoelectric coupling is bounded by the
geometric mean of the permittivity and the permeability, and a value outside that bound is not a material.
The check on the algebra is that $\alpha_{\rm eff}\to\alpha_{33}$ when $e=h=0$, and that the correction
vanishes when the plate is free to strain in the way the thin-plate limit allows.
\end{worked}

\section{Homogenisation: two routes and the band}\label{sec:homog}

The volume fraction $v$ of the piezoelectric phase enters only through the effective constants. Two routes
are carried, and they are not equivalent, so both appear on every figure.

\emph{Route 1, the rule of mixtures.} Each constant is the volume average of the two phases,
$\bar C=vC^{p}+(1-v)C^{m}$ and the same for $\epsilon,\mu,\rho$; the coupling constants are weighted in the
same way, and the magnetoelectric coefficient is taken as
$\bar\alpha=v(1-v)\,\alpha_{0}$ with $\alpha_0$ a product of the two phase couplings, so that it vanishes
at both ends as it must.

\emph{Route 2, the Mori--Tanaka estimate.} One phase is treated as an ellipsoidal inclusion in the other,
and the effective constants follow from the strain and field concentration tensors; the magnetoelectric
coefficient is then not a simple product and is not symmetric in the choice of matrix, which is why the
choice must be stated.

Both are textbook composites estimates; neither is a fit. The spread between them is the band, and the
condition the paper has to meet is that the predicted attenuation be wider than the band. That is stated as
a test, not as a hope, and the week-nine decision rests on it.

\section{Calculation I: the dimension of every entry}

Every row of $M(\eta)\mathbf a=\mathbf 0$ is one balance, so all entries of a row carry the dimension that
turns their amplitude into the dimension of that balance. For a magnetoelectric problem this check earns its
ten minutes, because there are three unit systems in use in the literature and the magnetoelectric
coefficient is quoted in at least two of them.

\begin{worked}
Row 1 is a force balance per unit volume, N\,m$^{-3}$. $M_{11}X$: $\Gcal C_{11}k^2$ is Pa\,m$^{-2}$ and $X$
is m. $M_{12}Z$: $\ii k\eta\Gcal(C_{13}+C_{44})$ is m$^{-2}$Pa and $Z$ is m. $M_{13}\Phi$:
$\ii k\eta(e_{31}+e_{15})$ is C\,m$^{-4}$ and $\Phi$ is V, so J\,m$^{-4}$ = N\,m$^{-3}$. $M_{14}\Psi$:
$\ii k\eta(h_{31}+h_{15})$ is m$^{-2}$ times the unit of $h$; for the product with $\Psi$ to be N\,m$^{-3}$,
$h$ must carry N\,A$^{-1}$m$^{-1}$ and $\Psi$ the ampere, A, and indeed N\,A$^{-1}$m$^{-1}\cdot$A = N\,m$^{-1}$,
times m$^{-2}$ from the two wavenumbers and times m from the amplitude gives N\,m$^{-3}$. $M_{15}\Theta$:
$\ii k\beta_1$ is Pa\,m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ and $\Theta$ is K. Row 1 is consistent.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row 3 is the electric Gauss law, C\,m$^{-3}$. $M_{31}X$: $\ii k\eta(e_{15}+e_{31})$ is C\,m$^{-4}$ and $X$
is m. $M_{33}\Phi$: $\epsilon_{33}\eta^2$ is C\,V$^{-1}$m$^{-3}$ and $\Phi$ is V. $M_{35}\Theta$:
$\eta p_3$ is C\,m$^{-3}$K$^{-1}$ and $\Theta$ is K. The new one is $M_{34}\Psi$:
$\alpha_{33}\eta^2-\alpha_{11}k^2$ must be C\,m$^{-3}$A$^{-1}$, because $\Psi$ carries A; since $\eta^2$
carries m$^{-2}$, $\alpha$ carries C\,m$^{-1}$A$^{-1}$, and C\,A$^{-1}$ is the second, so the unit of
$\alpha$ is s\,m$^{-1}$. In SI the magnetoelectric coefficient is quoted either this way or as
V\,m$^{-1}$A$^{-1}$ per unit field; the paper must name the one it uses, convert the literature to it, and
state the number once, and this paragraph is where that conversion is fixed.
Row 4 is the magnetic law, and its dimension is Wb\,m$^{-3}$ = N\,A$^{-1}$m$^{-2}$. $M_{41}X$:
$\ii k\eta(h_{15}+h_{31})$ is N\,A$^{-1}$m$^{-3}$ and $X$ is m. $M_{43}\Phi$: $\alpha\eta^2$ is
s\,m$^{-1}\cdot$m$^{-2}$ = s\,m$^{-3}$ and $\Phi$ is V, so V\,s\,m$^{-3}$ = Wb\,m$^{-3}$A$^{-1}$A, that is
Wb\,m$^{-3}$; the row is written per unit volume and per unit current, so the two readings agree. $M_{44}\Psi$: $\mu_{33}\eta^2$ is
N\,A$^{-2}$m$^{-2}$, since $\mu$ carries N\,A$^{-2}$, and $\Psi$ is A. Rows 3 and 4 are consistent, and the
equality $M_{34}=M_{43}$ is consistent with both.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Row 5 is an energy balance, J\,m$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$. $M_{51}X$: $fT_0\omega k\beta_1$ is
Pa\,m$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ and $X$ is m. $M_{52}Z$: $-\ii\omega fT_0\beta_3\eta$ is the same and $Z$ is m.
$M_{53}\Phi$: $\ii\omega fT_0p_3\eta$ is C\,m$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$ and $\Phi$ is V, so J\,m$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$.
$M_{54}\Psi$: $\ii\omega fT_0m_3\eta$ must be J\,m$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$A$^{-1}$ before multiplication, so $m_3$
carries C\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, the magnetic analogue of the pyroelectric coefficient, and $\Psi$ in A turns
it into J\,m$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$. $M_{55}\Theta$: $-\ii\omega f\rho c_p-k_{\rm th}L$ is
J\,m$^{-3}$K$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ and $\Theta$ is K. Row 5 is consistent.
\end{worked}

\section{Calculation J: the two family matrices}

\begin{worked}
The split holds here for every setting of the couplings, including the magnetoelectric one, so the
fourteen-by-fourteen can be replaced by two seven-by-seven. Take a pair of roots $\pm\eta_m$; the amplitude
vectors are related by the column signs $\tau=(+1,-1,-1,-1,+1)$ of Section~\ref{sec:calcD}, that is
$\mathbf a(-\eta_m)=\operatorname{diag}(\tau)\mathbf a(\eta_m)$: $\hat u_x$ and $\hat\theta$ keep their
sign, $\hat u_z$, $\hat\varphi$ and $\hat\psi$ change theirs.
Substitution: form $\mathbf a(\eta_m)e^{\eta_mz}\pm\operatorname{diag}(\tau)\mathbf a(\eta_m)e^{-\eta_mz}$.
Collect component by component: the plus combination gives $2a_j\cosh\eta_mz$ for $\tau_j=+1$ and
$2a_j\sinh\eta_mz$ for $\tau_j=-1$, so $\hat u_x$ and $\hat\theta$ are even in $z$ and $\hat u_z$,
$\hat\varphi$, $\hat\psi$ are odd; the minus combination reverses it.
Result: seven roots with $\operatorname{Re}\eta_m\ge0$, seven conditions at one face, and
$B^{\rm sym}_{rm}=\sum_j c_{rj}(\eta_m)a_j(\eta_m)\bigl(e^{\eta_mh}+\tau_je^{-\eta_mh}\bigr)$, with the
antisymmetric matrix obtained by changing the plus to a minus.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
One caution that belongs in the paper. The four face combinations change rows $b_5$ and $b_6$ only, and
they change them by replacing a flux condition by a potential condition. Under the parity reduction the
replacement must respect the parity: for the symmetric family $\hat\varphi$ and $\hat\psi$ are odd, so
$\Phi_m=0$ at $z=+h$ is a condition on $\sinh\eta_mh$ and not on the amplitude alone. Substitution of the
closed-face row into the family matrix therefore gives $a_\varphi\sinh\eta_mh=0$ in place of the open-face
row, and writing $\Phi_m=0$ without the hyperbolic factor is an error that will show up as an extra
spurious branch. Result: the four combinations are four different seven-by-seven matrices, and the extreme
pair is the one to plot.
\end{worked}

\subsection*{Why each test is a test}

\begin{worked}
The magnetoelectric-off test comes first because it is the one with an independent answer: with
$\alpha=0$ the two stiffenings simply add, and their sum is a number that has been measured on
piezoelectric and on piezomagnetic plates separately. The both-off test is the elastic or thermoelastic
plate, computed long ago, so any disagreement is ours. The parity test is decided by the parities of the
entries alone, so it is a test of the algebra and not of the physics, and a root finder that does not show
the pairing has not tested anything. The four face combinations are a test of consistency rather than of
correctness: all four are defensible, so the test is that the paper names the one it uses and that the
extreme pair brackets the other two, which is a statement about the ordering of the attenuation and not
about the numbers.
The bound $\alpha^2<\epsilon\mu$ is a test of the material data and not of the reduction: a value outside
it is not a physical composite, and it will show up as a negative stiffness and a spurious growing branch.
Result: five tests, each of which fails in a different place.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
Two that belong in the paper. The first is the sensitivity to the homogenisation route: the two routes
give a band and not a number, so the figure must show the band, and the test is that every measured or
computed quantity lies inside it for both routes. The second is the ordering of the four face combinations
in attenuation. The prediction from the structure of the rows is that closing a face removes the field
that carries part of the energy, so the closed--closed pair is the least attenuated of the four and the
open--open pair the most; if the computation gives the opposite ordering the sign convention of $p_3$ or
of $m_3$ is wrong, and that is worth knowing before any figure is drawn.
\end{worked}

\section{Calculation K: why seven conditions and not five}

\begin{worked}
Count the amplitudes first. The degree of $\det M$ is the number of partial waves, because each root
$\eta_m$ contributes one wave and one unknown amplitude $A_m$. With $l_g\ne0$ the count of
Section~\ref{sec:calcD} gives fourteen, with $l_g=0$ it gives ten. Each face supplies half of them, so
seven conditions per face in the first case and five in the second.
Now count the other way, by field, and check that the two agree. There are five fields, so five conditions
per face is one per field: a traction, a shear traction, an electric condition, a carrier or phason
condition and a thermal condition. The two extra conditions in the gradient case are the two double
tractions, and they are extra because the gradient length raises the order of the mechanical equations in
$z$ from two to four: $\Gcal$ carries $\eta^2$ and multiplies the $\eta^2$ that the strains already carry,
so $M_{11}$ and $M_{22}$ are quartic in $\eta$ and the two momentum rows are fourth order in $z$.
Collect: five fields plus two extra orders is seven, and seven per face times two faces is fourteen, which
is the number of amplitudes. The two counts agree, which is the check.
\end{worked}

\begin{worked}
The same count says what happens at the degenerate edges. At $l_g=0$ the double tractions vanish and the
matrix drops to ten, so five conditions per face; keeping the two double-traction rows with $l_g=0$ would
give two rows of zeros and a singular matrix, which is the commonest way to get a spurious zero branch in
this problem. At $\eta=0$ the carrier flux row vanishes identically, because every term in it carries one
$\eta$, so a root at the origin has to be treated on its own rather than through the general assembly.
Result: the count is not a formality; it is where the degenerate cases announce themselves.
\end{worked}

\section{Self-test list}\label{sec:selftest}

\begin{enumerate}
\item All couplings off, both size lengths zero: the classical Rayleigh--Lamb plate, both families.
\item $\alpha=0$ with $e,h\ne0$: the decoupled piezoelectric plus piezomagnetic plate.
\item $e=h=\alpha=0$: the thermoelastic plate with the size effect.
\item Both routes at $v\to0$ and $v\to1$: the composite constants must approach the pure phases.
\item The parity statement of Calculation D, checked from the roots and from the two family matrices.
\item $l_g\to0$ and $l_{\rm nl}\to0$ separately, continuously, with the gradient length stiffening and the
nonlocal length softening.
\item Row count against root count, in both settings of $l_g$.
\item The four electric and magnetic face combinations: state which is used, and check that the closed face
gives $\Phi_m=0$ as a row.
\end{enumerate}
