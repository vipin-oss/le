\section*{2. Parity, the one substantive result that changed}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{2.2cm}p{7.0cm}p{4.6cm}}
\toprule
paper & statement in the master & how it was checked\\
\midrule
A & the polynomial is even, and the families split, if and only if $\beta_{\rm th}=0$, $\Pi_{\rm th}=0$
and $E_g=0$ together; with any one of them present the roots do not pair and the determinant is taken over
all fourteen roots & (i) exact parity of every entry plus a row-and-column sign matching, which is
independent of every constant; (ii) the determinant expanded over the 120 permutations in exact rational
arithmetic. Both agree on all sixteen combinations of $p_3,\beta_{\rm th},\Pi_{\rm th},E_g$; the two
entries that refuse the matching are $M_{45}$ and $M_{54}$\\
B & even for every setting; two seven-row family matrices may be built and must agree & both tests on all

C & even for every setting; the magnetoelectric entries are even in $\eta$ in both Gauss rows & both tests
on all 32 combinations of $e,h,\alpha,p_3,m_3$; no entry fails the sign matching\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The hand sign argument and the exact test agree. An earlier statement of mine, that the break came from the
pyroelectric constant, was wrong and is withdrawn: $p_3$ enters through two odd entries, $M_{35}$ and
$M_{53}$, both of which fit the matching, and switching it off does not restore the pairing. The break
comes from the thermodiffusion term in the carrier equation and from the Peltier and recombination terms in
the heat equation, all three of which are even in $\eta$ where the matching needs them odd.

The first test I ran on this question was worthless and it is worth saying why, because the same trap is
open in the other two papers. It evaluated $\det M(\eta)$ and $\det M(-\eta)$ in floating point and
compared them. The entries of these matrices span about forty decades, so the odd part of the determinant
disappears in cancellation and the answer follows the size of the coupling constants --- which is nonsense,
because the parity of a polynomial cannot depend on how large its coefficients are. Any future check of
this kind has to be either structural or exact.
