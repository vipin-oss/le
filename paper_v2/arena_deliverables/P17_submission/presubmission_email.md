I should like to ask whether *Applied Mechanics Reviews* would consider a review manuscript of the following kind before I submit it formally.

**Title.** *Waves in Thermoelastic and Multiphysics Continua: Generalized Heat Conduction, Coupled Fields and Size Effects.*

**Abstract (150 words).**

> A solid that conducts heat, deforms and carries further fields is described by one member of a family of coupled continuum models, which the literature presents one member at a time. This review organises the family around a single heat-flux template with three lags and shows how the classical, Lord–Shulman, Green–Naghdi, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag and Moore–Gibson–Thompson theories arise from it as parameter choices, separating regular from singular limits. For linear homogeneous media a dispersion polynomial in the squared wavenumber is derived, verified symbolically, and used to compare phase speed, attenuation and thermoelastic damping across the theories. Waves in bounded and unbounded media, interfaces and energy partition, multiphysics couplings, size effects, solution methods, applications and a comparative synthesis are treated in turn. Comparison of truncated lag laws with the exact delay shows that the dual-phase-lag stability condition depends on the truncation order.

**Length.** The manuscript runs to 157 pages including 12 appendices and 414 references. Appendices E (classified catalogue of the cited literature), J (equation-level verification register) and K (atlas of problems) — 24 pages in all — can move to supplementary material, which brings the main text to about 100 pages without loss of any argument; a condensed key-works table would remain in the main text so that it stays self-contained. I would be glad to receive the editor's preference on this before submission.

**Fit to AMR.** The manuscript organises a field that is usually presented one model at a time: 414 works, 330 of them after 1990 and 133 after 2020, are placed on one set of coordinates, with the reductions between the named theories stated exactly and the singular limits identified. It carries a page-level provenance table that records, for each attribution, which printed page and equation was checked and what the check does not cover, and it closes with a comparative synthesis, a seventeen-item pitfalls checklist and a decision guide. Its purpose is the one AMR states for itself: to organise, clarify and summarise existing work, and to make the literature usable rather than merely cited. It also contains a modest amount of new synthesis — the comparative dispersion polynomial and the truncation analysis of the dual-phase-lag stability condition — and the manuscript states plainly which parts are review and which are new, so that the editor can judge the balance.

**Declarations.** No funding; no competing interests; the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published in any form. Generative tools were used in preparing the manuscript and in writing the verification scripts, and this is disclosed in the manuscript's Declarations section together with the limits of the checking that was performed. The scripts that produce every computed figure accompany the manuscript.
