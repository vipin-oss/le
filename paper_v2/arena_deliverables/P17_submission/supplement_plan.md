**Companion to** `referee_report.md` (finding R1, R2 and Section 4 of that report).
**Manuscript read.** Compiled paper of 2 November 2026, 157 pages.
**Rule applied.** Whatever moves out, the main text must stay self-contained: a reader of the printed article must be able to follow every argument, every figure and every citation without the supplement, and must be able to tell what has been moved (one sentence per move, in the place where the material used to be).

Tier 1 takes the paper from 157 to about 145 pages. It is not enough, and it is not meant to be: its purpose is to remove the three things a referee would otherwise have to report.

**Tier 3 — if the editor still asks for less.** The honest options are a two-part publication (Part I: the template, the reductions, the dispersion relation, Sections 3–5; Part II: waves, interfaces, multiphysics, Sections 6–11, with the reference list printed once at the end of Part II) or acceptance of a longer main text with Appendices E, F, G, J and K all supplementary. The first is a much better outcome for the reader than cutting Section 6, which is the section the review exists for.

## 4. The reference-renumbering consequence, stated numerically

Citation markers were extracted from every page of the compiled paper and grouped by location:

* 414 printed references; **every one is cited at least once** (no orphan entries — worth keeping that property through the revision);
* **339** are cited somewhere in the body (p. 1–94);
* **329** are cited somewhere in the appendices (p. 95–126);
* **72** are cited **only** in the appendices: 66 only in Appendix E (the catalogue), 5 only in Appendix K ([410]–[414]) and 1 only in Appendices F–I;
* a further **3** are cited only in Tables 19–21, which are themselves misplaced on p. 155–157 — so **75** references, 18 % of the list, never appear in the body;
* **82** are cited only in the body.

So: moving Appendix E as it stands detaches 66 references (15.9 % of the list) from the main text, and moving Appendix K detaches a further 5. Because the reference list is in order of first citation (checked: [403]–[406] are first cited in Appendix E, p. 108–109, and [411] in Appendix K, p. 121 — so the ordering is correct as printed), a move of the appendices renumbers everything after the first appendix-only reference. The condensed key-works table proposed in Tier 2 is what prevents this: any work a reader of the main text needs must be cited in the main text, and only those references keep their place.

**Order of operations, so that the numbering is touched once:**

1. settle the Tier 1 and Tier 2 moves;
2. write the replacement key-works table for Section 2.5;
3. recompile and re-run the citation check (every printed reference cited at least once in the new main text, no orphan);
4. then, and only then, fix page numbers in the captions and in Tables 19–21, because every move changes them.

## 5. What must not move, whatever else is decided

* **Table 6 (p. 29), page-level provenance.** It is the evidence behind the declarations, and a reader of the main text must be able to discount the main text's own claims in the main text.
* **Table 10 (p. 66), computed values against printed values.** Same reason.
* **Section 4.3.1 and Fig. 10.** The contribution most likely to be cited.
* **Section 10.1.4, the 17-item pitfalls checklist, and Fig. 40, the decision sequence.** This is the "organises and clarifies" half of AMR's remit in two pages.
* **Appendix D with Table 19.** Code and figure reproducibility is the manuscript's distinguishing claim; a pointer to a supplement is much weaker than the table itself.

## 6. What the submission should say about length

One sentence in the pre-submission inquiry and in the cover letter, in this form:

> The manuscript runs to 157 pages including 12 appendices; Appendices E, J and K (24 pages) can move to supplementary material, which brings the main text to about 100 pages without loss of any argument.

The number must be recomputed once Tier 1 and Tier 2 are actually done — an editor who is promised 100 pages and receives 118 will remember it.
