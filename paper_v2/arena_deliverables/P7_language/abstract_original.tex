\begin{summarybox}{Highlights}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A single heat-flux template with three lags and one memory term organises the classical, Lord--Shulman, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag, Moore--Gibson--Thompson, fractional and memory-dependent theories (and the Green--Naghdi types as limits) as parameter choices; Green--Lindsay and Guyer--Krumhansl lie outside it and are treated separately.
\item One coupled dispersion polynomial, verified symbolically, gives comparative phase speed, attenuation and thermoelastic damping across all template models.
\item The widely repeated dual-phase-lag stability condition $\tau_T\ge\tau_q/2$ is shown to belong to a specific Taylor truncation, not to the first-order template.
\item Finite thermal fronts, Rayleigh-wave damping, piezoelectric renormalisation and interface energy-partition rules are derived and checked numerically.
\item Open problems, a pitfalls checklist, a decision guide and reporting standards are provided for journal submission.
\end{itemize}
\end{summarybox}

\begin{abstract}
Wave propagation in solids that conduct heat, deform, and carry further fields such as electric polarization, magnetic induction, diffusing species or microstructural degrees of freedom is governed by a family of coupled models that is usually presented one member at a time. This review organises that family around a single heat-flux template with three lags and one memory term, shows how the classical, Lord--Shulman, Green--Lindsay, Green--Naghdi, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag, Moore--Gibson--Thompson, fractional and memory-dependent theories arise (or fail to arise) as parameter choices, and records exactly which limits are regular and which are singular, including a correction to a widely repeated stability statement for the dual-phase-lag model. For the coupled problem in a linear homogeneous medium we derive and symbolically verify a dispersion polynomial in $K=k^2$ for the template models with rational heat laws, state where exact delays, fractional powers and memory kernels make the relation transcendental or multivalued, and use the polynomial to compare phase speed, attenuation and thermoelastic damping across theories; we then treat transient problems (heat pulse, thermal shock), Rayleigh waves, reflection and interface energy partition, piezoelectric, magneto-thermoelastic, diffusive and microstructural couplings, and nonlocal and gradient size effects against lattice dispersion. We discuss analytical and numerical solution techniques with a benchmark, applications from laser ultrasonics to micro-resonators and tissue heating, and close with a comparative synthesis, a pitfalls checklist, a decision guide and a research agenda. We also provide flexural and plate thermoelastic damping validated against exact solutions, anisotropic and free-surface reflection results, a finite-difference benchmark for hyperbolic heat conduction, worked examples and reporting standards. All quantitative figures are generated by accompanying scripts and the key identities are checked symbolically or numerically.
\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generalized thermoelasticity; second sound; dual-phase-lag; Moore--Gibson--Thompson; fractional heat conduction; memory-dependent derivative; nonlocal elasticity; piezoelectricity; Rayleigh waves; thermoelastic damping; interface energy partition.
\end{abstract}
