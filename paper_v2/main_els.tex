\title{Waves in Thermoelastic and Multiphysics Continua:\\
 Generalized Heat Conduction, Coupled Fields and Size Effects}

\author{Vipin Gupta}
\address{Gurugram University}
\ead{vipin@gurugramuniversity.ac.in}

\begin{abstract}
Wave propagation in solids that conduct heat, deform, and carry further fields such as electric polarization, magnetic induction, diffusing species or microstructural degrees of freedom is governed by a family of coupled models that is usually presented one member at a time. This review organises that family around a single heat-flux template with three lags and one memory term, shows how the classical, Lord--Shulman, Green--Lindsay, Green--Naghdi, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag, Moore--Gibson--Thompson, fractional and memory-dependent theories arise (or fail to arise) as parameter choices, and records exactly which limits are regular and which are singular, including a correction to a widely repeated stability statement for the dual-phase-lag model. For the coupled problem we derive and symbolically verify a single dispersion polynomial, and use it to compare phase speed, attenuation and thermoelastic damping across theories; we then treat transient problems (heat pulse, thermal shock), Rayleigh waves, reflection and interface energy partition, piezoelectric, magneto-thermoelastic, diffusive and microstructural couplings, and nonlocal and gradient size effects against lattice dispersion. We discuss analytical and numerical solution techniques with a benchmark, applications from laser ultrasonics to micro-resonators and tissue heating, and close with a comparative synthesis, a pitfalls checklist, a decision guide and a research agenda. We also provide flexural and plate thermoelastic damping validated against exact solutions, anisotropic and free-surface reflection results, a finite-difference benchmark for hyperbolic heat conduction, worked examples and reporting standards. All quantitative figures are generated by accompanying scripts and the key identities are checked symbolically or numerically.
\end{abstract}

\item A single heat-flux template with three lags and one memory term organises classical, Lord--Shulman, Green--Lindsay, Green--Naghdi, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag, Moore--Gibson--Thompson, fractional and memory-dependent theories as parameter choices.

\item The widely repeated dual-phase-lag stability condition $\tau_T\ge\tau_q/2$ is shown to belong to a specific Taylor truncation, not to the first-order template.
\item Finite thermal fronts, Rayleigh-wave damping, piezoelectric renormalisation and interface energy-partition rules are derived and checked numerically.
\item Open problems, a pitfalls checklist, a decision guide and reporting standards are provided for journal submission.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Nomenclature.} The following symbols are used throughout. Indices follow the Einstein summation convention; a comma denotes a spatial derivative and a dot a time derivative.
\begin{table}[ht]\centering\small
\caption{Principal notation.}\label{tab:nomenclature}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{4.1cm}lp{4.3cm}@{}}\toprule
$u_i,\ \varepsilon_{ij}$ & displacement, strain & $\sigma_{ij}$ & stress\\\tabularnewline
$\theta=T-T_0$ & temperature increment & $T_0$ & reference temperature\\\tabularnewline
$q_i$ & heat flux & $v$ & thermal displacement, $\dot v=\theta$\\\tabularnewline
$\rho$ & mass density & $c$ & specific heat at constant strain\\\tabularnewline
$\lambda,\mu$ & Lam\'e constants & $\alpha_T$ & linear expansion coefficient\\\tabularnewline
$\beta=(3\lambda+2\mu)\alpha_T$ & thermal-stress coefficient & $k,\ k^*$ & conductivities (Fourier, GN)\\\tabularnewline
$\tau_q,\tau_T,\tau_v$ & relaxation time and lags & $\alpha=k/\rho c$ & thermal diffusivity\\\tabularnewline
$c_1=\sqrt{(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho}$ & isothermal P-wave speed & $c_2=\sqrt{\mu/\rho}$ & shear wave speed\\\tabularnewline
$\varepsilon=\beta^2T_0/(\rho^2cc_1^2)$ & thermoelastic coupling & $\omega_*=\rho c c_1^2/k$ & thermal characteristic frequency\\\tabularnewline
$G(s)$ & heat-flux function & $s=-\ii\omega$ & Laplace variable / complex frequency\\\tabularnewline
$c_T$ & thermal wave speed & $K^2$ & electromechanical coupling\\\tabularnewline
$\xi,\ell$ & nonlocal and gradient lengths & $Z=\rho c$ & mechanical impedance\\\tabularnewline
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The strain tensor is always written with two indices ($\varepsilon_{ij}$) so that it is not confused with the scalar coupling $\varepsilon$. Plane waves are $\exp[\ii(kx-\omega t)]$ with $\Imag k\ge0$ for spatial decay in the propagation direction, and a ``branch'' of the dispersion relation is a root $k(\omega)$ followed by continuity in $\omega$.
