**Re: submission of a review manuscript, "Waves in Thermoelastic and Multiphysics Continua: Generalized Heat Conduction, Coupled Fields and Size Effects"**

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript named above for consideration as a review article in *Applied Mechanics Reviews*.

**1. What the manuscript is.** A review of linear wave propagation in thermoelastic and multiphysics continua, organised around one heat-flux template with three lags (Eq. (14)). The classical, Lord–Shulman, Green–Naghdi, dual-phase-lag, three-phase-lag and Moore–Gibson–Thompson theories follow from it as parameter choices, with the regular and singular limits identified; Green–Lindsay and Guyer–Krumhansl theories do not pass through the template and are treated separately. The manuscript cites 425 works and treats waves in three dimensions, at free surfaces and interfaces, in plates and layered media, in piezoelectric and other coupled continua and in materials with nonlocal and gradient enrichments. It then covers solution methods with a benchmark, the experimental evidence base, a comparative synthesis, a pitfalls checklist and a research agenda.

**2. What is new compared with earlier reviews.** The field has been reviewed before (Chandrasekharaiah 1986, 1998; Hetnarski and Ignaczak 1999, 2000; Ignaczak and Ostoja-Starzewski 2010; Tzou 2015), and the manuscript cites these. The contributions claimed here are specific and checkable:

* the reduction of the named theories to one template, with the reduction table (Table 4) and the singular limit τ_q → 0 at fixed τ_T identified as such (Sec. 3.2.4);
* a dispersion polynomial in K = k² valid for every rational heat law, derived and checked symbolically, so that speed, attenuation and damping can be compared across models that are usually tabulated separately (Eq. (33));
* a demonstration that the dual-phase-lag stability threshold depends on the Taylor truncation: τ_T = τ_q/2 for the variant second order in τ_q, τ_T = τ_q for the first-order variant, and τ_T = (2−√3)τ_q when both are expanded to second order (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 10);
* a quantified statement of when a truncated lag law stops representing the exact delay (Sec. 4.4, Fig. 12);
* a taxonomy, a pitfalls checklist and a decision guide, intended so that a new model can be placed without argument and two studies can be judged comparable or not (Sec. 10).

**3. Review and new material.** Section 1 states which contributions are review (the organisation, the comparison tables, the decision guide) and which are new to the literature (the comparative dispersion polynomial and the truncation analysis).

**4. Length and supplementary material.** The manuscript is 156 pages in the submission layout, of which the appendices take 32 pages and the reference list 29. The classified catalogue of the cited literature, the equation verification register and the atlas of problems can move to supplementary material without loss of argument, and a condensed key-works table would stay in the main text. I will follow the editor's preference on this and on whether the longest body sections should be shortened.

**5. Originality.** The manuscript is original, has not been published in whole or in part, and is not under consideration elsewhere. 46 of the 425 references (10.8 %) are co-authored by me; the appendix on problems separates studies from my own group from studies by others.

**6. Generative-tool disclosure.** Generative AI tools were used for drafting and language editing, for writing and running the verification scripts, for checking notation and cross-references, and for literature and claim checks. This is disclosed in the Declarations section together with its limits: not every cited work was read page by page, and the claim register records which attributions were checked against the printed source and which were not. I reviewed the content and am responsible for it; the tools are not authors. Every figure was produced by a script or drawn as a schematic from the governing equations. No figure was generated by an image model, and no figure is reproduced from another publication.

**7. Data and code availability.** The Python scripts that produce every computed figure, the benchmark cases and the parameter registers accompany the manuscript. No new experimental data were generated; every measured value quoted is attributed to its source.

**8. Declarations.** No funding was received; there is no competing interest. I am the sole author.

**9. References without DOI.** Fourteen reference entries have no DOI. Six are journal articles that predate DOI assignment or appeared in journals that do not register DOIs (Born and von Kármán 1912, Duhamel 1837, Cattaneo 1948, Danilovskaya 1950, Vernotte 1958, Gulyaev 1969), one is in a journal that is not a Crossref member (Kumar, Devi and Sharma 2017), one is a chapter in a multi-author book (Chadwick 1960), and four are monographs without a registered DOI (Auld 1973, Boley and Weiner 1960, Love 1911, Scruby and Drain 1990). For two further books a DOI exists only for another edition: Nowacki's *Thermoelasticity* is cited in the 1986 Pergamon edition, and Tzou's 1997 CRC edition has no DOI, the registered work being the 2014 second edition. Each entry carries the author, title, journal or publisher, volume, pages and year needed to locate it.

I hope the manuscript is suitable for *Applied Mechanics Reviews* and look forward to the referees' comments.
