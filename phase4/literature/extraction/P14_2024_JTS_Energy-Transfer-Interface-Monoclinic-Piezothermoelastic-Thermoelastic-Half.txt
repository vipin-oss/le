1. Introduction
The reflection problem in piezothermoelastic materials has practical applications in designing
devices, advanced materials, and structures across various industries. An improved understanding
of wave behavior and energy transfer at material interfaces can enhance smart composites’ and
multilayered plates’ performance and durability.
Actuation, dynamic sensing, intelligent structures, and mechanical systems have shown signifi-
cant interest in piezoelectric materials. Curie brothers [1] first uncovered piezoelectricity. The
ability of a crystal to generate a voltage when subjected to mechanical stress is known as piezo-
electricity. The piezothermoelectricity theory was invented by Mindlin [2]. Nowacki [3] and
Chandrasekharaiah [4] investigated the piezothermoelastic material’s adherence to the universe’s
physical laws. Extensive effort was made in the subsequent century, particularly in the past few
decades, to create piezoelectric materials and make their possible usage in commercial applica-
tions a reality. Kumar and his team [5–9] conducted several vital investigations on the wave
reflection phenomena in intelligent and composite structures.
The Biot [10] classical coupled thermoelasticity (CCT) theory and the two-temperature ther-
moelasticity (C2T) theory developed by Chen and his coworkers [11–13] are plagued by the para-
dox of heat conduction. These theories predicted that the heat equations are of a mixed
parabolic–hyperbolic type, which indicates infinite propagation speeds of heat waves even though
these speeds are considered finite in the real world. Several researchers have tried modified CCT
and C2T theories to construct a hyperbolic equation for heat conduction to avoid the paradoxical

unlimited speed of thermal waves. Lord and Shulman [14] generalized the CCT theory by adding
a relaxation time to Fourier’s law of heat conduction and making the equation hyperbolic. Green
and Naghdi (GN-I,-II,-III) [15–17] presented the three novel generalized thermoelastic models.
GN I’s linearized form is the same as Fourier’s thermoelasticity. GN-II,-III linear types have a
slow thermal signal transfer, and the entropy flow vector is a stress-affecting potential. Unlike
GN-III, GN-II does not dissipate thermal energy.
In 1995, Tzou [18] proposed modifying the CCT model by adding the two delay time parame-
ters for temperature gradient and microstructural interaction and developed the dual-phase-lag
(DPL) model. Tzou’s framework illustrates tiny electron–phonon interactions as retarding causes
causing a macroscopic delay. Using the DPL model for the macroscopic formulation, it would be
possible to analyze micro-structural heat transport behavior. Later, the three-phase lag (TPL)
theory was developed by Roy Choudhuri [19] by extending the DPL theory to account for an
additional delay time for the thermal displacement gradient. Recently, Youssef and Bary [20]
overcame the conundrum of thermal wave propagation at infinite speed in C2T theory by invent-
ing the novel hyperbolic two-temperature (H2T) model.

This idea will more clearly illustrate the “memory effect” (change rate depends on prior state)
than the fractional calculus. MDD may be easily described purely physically, and the memory-
dependent differential equation can be seen as equating to greater verbal fluency. Therefore, it
may be used in more situations than partial derivatives can, where the parameter of the fractional

Initial stresses are essential in studying wave propagation, reflection, and transmission because
they affect material mechanical responses. Initial stresses are relentless due to non-uniform mater-
ial qualities, sluggish creeping, changes in thermal expansion coefficients, differential external
forces, gravity fluctuations, material shrinkage, growth, etc., throughout the processing at various
temperatures. Piezoelectric materials are pre-stressed during manufacture to avoid brittle fractures
caused by inconsistent material characteristics. Several problems with the transmission and reflec-
tion of plane waves have been explored by various researchers [23–27].
Biswas [28] comprehensively examined Stroh’s analysis of the Rayleigh waves in an anisotropic
medium. Moreover, Biswas and Abo-Dahab [29] scrutinized the influence of initial stresses on sur-
face waves within orthotropic-thermoelastic mediums. Utilizing the TPL theory with MDD, Biswas
[30] discussed a thermoelastic problem in an orthotropic medium. Furthermore, Biswas [31]

explored the propagation of Rayleigh surface waves in a piezothermoelastic transversely isotropic
layer positioned above a piezothermoelastic transversely isotropic half-space, focusing specifically on
the Green–Naghdi III model. In a recent study, in their noteworthy research, Gupta et al. [32–36]
have made a significant advancement in the field by examining wave phenomena at the interface of
orthotropic piezothermoelastic materials. They utilized the 2PL and 3PL MDD models, which
effectively capture the intricate material behavior, and incorporated various temperature theories to
investigate the impact of hyperbolic temperature on the wave behavior of the interface.
We conclude from the above literature work that much work has been done on transversely
isotropic and orthotropic piezothemoelastic material. Still, few researchers study the wave phe-
nomenon at monoclinic piezothemoelastic medium due to its complexity. In this research work,
the distribution of energy ratios of transmitted and reflected waves at the interface of initially
stressed monoclinic piezothermoelastic half-space and thermoelastic half-space have been studied
under the H2T and TPL theory of MDD.

9. Conclusion
In this work, we investigate the plane wave phenomena at the interface of thermoelastic and ini-
tially stressed monoclinic piezothermoelastic medium under the 3PL equation of MDDs with
H2T. The energy ratios of various reflected (in TS medium) and transmitted waves (in MPS
medium) are depicted graphically for the incident of two types of waves, P or SV. The results are
obtained for different kernel functions and temperature models. The study’s findings are summar-
ized as follows:
i. For incidence P waves, MDD reduces the size of the energy ratios of reflected P and SV
waves while increasing the reflected thermal waves. The size of each reflected energy ratio is
somewhat reduced when initial stresses are present.
ii. For incidence SV waves, in all reflected waves, the energy ratios are dominated by the K0,0
kernel function followed by the K and K kernel functions of MDD. Initial stresses
0,1/2 1,1
increase the magnitude of reflected P and thermal waves and decrease the magnitude of SV
waves.
iii. Energy ratios are influenced by the material’s two-temperature properties, initial stresses,
MDD, and angle of incidence. This dependency manifests differently for various waves.
iv. Graphically the sum of the energy ratios at each angle of incidence is almost equal to one
for all considered models. Thus, every model concurs with the law of energy balance.
This research’s applications span materials science, geophysics, mechanical engineering, applied
mathematics, renewable energy, and materials characterization, offering valuable insights into the
behavior of stressed piezothermoelastic materials and wave propagation dynamics.
