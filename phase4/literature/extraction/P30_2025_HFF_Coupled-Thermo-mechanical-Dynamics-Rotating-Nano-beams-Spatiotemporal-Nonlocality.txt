Abstract
Purpose – This study aims to advance the thermo-electro-mechanical modeling of rotating nanobeam
resonators. The primary objective is to develop a comprehensive theoretical framework that accurately
predicts the nanobeam’s dynamic response under combined rotational, thermal and magnetic influences,
considering crucial size-dependent and memory effects. This study seeks to offer deeper insights for the
optimal design of advanced micro-electromechanical systems and nano-electromechanical systems (MEMS/
NEMS).
Design/methodology/approach – The study uses the Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, integrating a novel
spatiotemporal nonlocal elasticity theory based on the Klein–Gordon (KG) model, which incorporates both
internal length and time scales. For heat conduction, the Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) theory with a
higher-order memory-dependent derivative is used. Governing equations are derived using Laplace
transformation, with the inverse transform performed through Zakian’s algorithm for enhanced accuracy.
Findings – Numerical simulations reveal the significant roles of rotation and size-dependent effects on the
nanobeam’s dynamic behavior. Key findings highlight the substantial influence of delayed time parameters,
various kernel types and applied magnetic fields on the coupled fields (displacement, temperature, bending
moment). The study also demonstrates that increasing angular velocity suppresses deformation, while
magnetic fields enhance displacement but reduce bending moment.
Research limitations/implications – This study is based on the Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, which
inherently neglects shear deformation and rotational inertia effects, limiting its applicability to slender beams.
Practical implications – The findings are crucial for the design and optimization of advanced MEMS/
NEMS. This includes applications, such as micro-gyroscopes, high-frequency sensors, actuators and
resonators, where accurate thermal, magnetic and mechanical coupling is essential at the nanoscale. The International Journal of Numerical

1. Introduction
In general, nonlocal theories may encompass both spatial and temporal characteristics,
leading to integral-type formulations for the constitutive laws that span space and time
domains (Brillouin, 1960). Within the fields of electrodynamics and plasma dynamics
(Broese et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2004; Demiray, 1977), the role of space-time nonlocality
becomes crucial for accurately modeling material behavior. At this point, only the spatial or
temporal nonlocality for memory effect modeling has been considered in the literature
survey for nonlocal elasticity (Eringen, 1983). To establish a systematic formulation of
nonlocal elasticity that accounts for both spatial and temporal nonlocal effects, we derive an
isotropic constitutive model characterized by a single internal time scale and a single internal
length scale. This model is obtained by simplifying a general anisotropic constitutive
equation, which includes a tensor-valued kernel function, for a three-dimensional solid. The
resulting formulation leads to the isotropic nonlocal elasticity theory of the “Klein-Gordon”
type. Several scientists have found micro-electrical, chemical and biological technologies to
be appealing throughout the past few decades. This technology describes tiny electrical and
mechanical parts made with precision manufacturing methods. Micro-electromechanical
systems (MEMS) technology is widely used in atomic force microscopes, micro-sensors,
safety devices for microscopes, energy harvesting, optical metamaterials, micro-activators
and micro-resonators (Babaei and Arabghahestani, 2021). To create superior prototypes for
the industrial society, the idealized models should incorporate both static and dynamic
analysis. It demonstrates that such small-scale systems cannot be adequately described by
classical mechanics. However, non-classical theories can capture size-dependent
phenomena, which could result in more accurate analysis and outcomes (Babaei et al., 2025).
The classical Fourier law becomes inadequate when dealing with extremely small structures
and rapid thermal processes. Under such conditions, thermal conduction must be modeled using
more advanced and physically consistent approaches. With advancements in nanotechnology, it
is now possible to manufacture materials and devices with characteristic sizes in the nanometer
range. Examples of such nanostructures include quantum dots, nanowires and superlattices. At
these miniature scales, the Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) theory (Thompson, 1972)
replaces the conventional continuum Fourier law for modeling heat transfer (Hobiny et al.,
2022; Zeeshan et al., 2023; Abouelregal et al., 2023). Using MGT theory, Das and his
coworkers (Sur et al., 2025; Das et al., 2025; Das et al., 2025; Gupta et al., 2024a; Gupta et al.,
2024c; Gupta et al., 2024b) have documented the memory-dependent heat transport
phenomenon in wave propagation and thermo-mechanical interaction problems.
The scientific contribution of this manuscript lies in the formulation of a unified thermo-
electro-mechanical framework for rotating nanobeam resonators, which rigorously
incorporates size-dependent effects, memory-based heat transfer and coupled nonlocal
interactions. Unlike conventional approaches that address these phenomena in isolation, the

The primary contribution of this research involves the extension of classical Helmholtz
nonlocal elasticity to a Klein–Gordon (KG) paradigm, creating a unified theoretical
framework for spatiotemporal nonlocality in elastic continua. The developed theoretical
framework was applied to examine how thermoelastic waves propagate through a rotating
nanobeam experiencing sinusoidal thermal influences. This study investigates the
thermoelastic vibration behavior of a rotating nanobeam subjected to an externally induced
magnetic field and sinusoidal thermal pulse heating. The constitutive relations are
formulated by incorporating spatiotemporal nonlocality modeled via a KG-type framework.
The heat transport equation is developed based on an advanced MGT thermoelasticity
theory, which includes MDD within a finite slipping interval. The nanobeam is assumed to
have simply supported boundary conditions. A sinusoidal thermal pulse is applied at the left
end, while the right end is maintained at zero temperature. The Laplace integral transform is
applied to the governing equations, reformulating the problem into a system of differential
equations in vector–matrix form. The system is then solved using the eigenvalue method. For
the evaluation of the physical field variables in the real space-time domain, numerical
inversion of the Laplace transform is carried out using the Zakian method, implemented in
the Mathematica software package (Halsted and Brown, 1972). Graphical representations of
the numerical results for displacement components, temperature and bending moment have
been presented to highlight the unique aspects of the current study, particularly under the
influence of various kernel functions in the heat conduction law. The findings further
illustrate how nonlocal effects and size dependency influence wave propagation in the
semiconductor, emphasizing the role of the time-delay parameter. Additionally, the impact of
rotational motion and the presence of a magnetic field on the nanobeam’s behavior is
examined. This advanced nanobeam model has several promising applications in the design
and optimization of nanoscale devices. It is particularly relevant for MEMS/NEMS
technologies, high-precision sensors, actuators, gyroscopes and resonators, where thermal,
magnetic and mechanical coupling must be accurately captured at the nanoscale. The
insights from this work pave the way for enhanced structural stability, thermal control and
stress management in smart nanoengineering systems.

6. Inverse Laplace transform using Zakian’s algorithm
In this section, our attention turns to transforming the solutions derived in the Laplace domain
back into the time domain. Directly inverting complex Laplace transform expressions analytically
is frequently challenging and sometimes even impossible. To overcome this, we utilize a
numerical approximation technique. This method, rooted in the concept of the Zakian’s (Halsted
and Brown, 1972) algorithm, provides a practical and efficient alternative for approximating the
inverse Laplace transform. The detailed scheme has been outlined in the section Appendix.

9. Concluding remarks
In this paper, a novel thermoelastic model of a rotating nanobeam has been developed by
integrating spatiotemporal nonlocal elasticity of the KG type with a refined MGT heat
conduction law incorporating memory-dependent derivatives. The model accounts for
characteristic internal length and time scales, electromagnetic field effects and rotational
dynamics, offering a robust framework for the study of nanoresonator systems under
sinusoidal thermal loading. The conclusions are as follows:
• The incorporation of spatiotemporal nonlocality leads to a more accurate
representation of size-dependent and delayed responses in the material under thermal
and mechanical loading.
• Memory effects embedded through different kernel functions significantly influence
the damping behavior, reducing stress concentrations and displacement magnitudes.
• The characteristic length scale parameter ℓ controls the spatial diffusion of
mechanical and thermal responses, enhancing the effective stiffness of the beam and
minimizing deformation.
• The characteristic time scale parameter τmodulates the temporal evolution of the fields,
introducing stronger memory effects that amplify displacement and temperature fields.
• Application of a magnetic field H 0 enhances the beam’s axial and transverse
displacements due to Lorentz force interaction, while simultaneously reducing the
internal bending moment, revealing a stress-relieving behavior.
• Increasing angular velocity Ω introduces centrifugal stiffening, which significantly
suppresses both deformation and thermal responses, making the structure more
stable under high-speed rotation.
• Among different thermoelastic theories, the MGT theory with higher-order memory-
dependent derivative yields the most flexible and sensitive response, whereas the
GN-III theory offers a conservative and damped response profile.
• The Laplace transform-based semi-analytical solution technique, along with
Zakian’s inversion method, proves effective for analyzing complex spatiotemporal
behaviors in nanostructures.
This advanced nanobeam model has several promising applications in the design and optimization
of nanoscale devices. It is particularly relevant for MEMS/NEMS technologies, high-precision
sensors, actuators, gyroscopes and resonators, where thermal, magnetic and mechanical coupling
must be accurately captured at the nanoscale. The insights from this work pave the way for enhanced
structural stability, thermal control and stress management in smart nanoengineering systems.
