characteristics of seismic waves through the Earth’s lithosphere. Different geometrical features of geological structures, inhomo-
geneities, irregularities, and anisotropies are the main factors that determine the change in frequency, amplitude, and direction of
seismic waves, resulting in wave scattering and coupling between modes of P, SV and SH-waves. Zhou and Chen [1] put forward an
in-depth practical approach that can directly determine the sampling points on irregular surfaces and significantly decrease the cal-

propagation, influencing phase velocity and dispersion. This finding emphasizes the need to consider surface irregularities when

unique propagation properties across different media and interfaces.
The study of surface waves in electro-magneto-elastic materials has garnered significant attention due to their theoretical im-
portance and practical applications in electro-active technologies. Recent studies have focused on layered structures incorporating
advanced materials with unique properties. These include examinations of Love waves in systems featuring piezoelectric [3–5] and
piezomagnetic [6–8] components, further enhancing our understanding of wave propagation in complex, multi-functional materi-
als.
The emerging field of straintronics, situated at the intersection of micro- and nano-electronics and materials science, investi-
gates the potential of strain-induced physical effects in solids for developing cutting-edge electronic devices [9,10]. This innovative
approach has led to the exploration of various phenomena, including direct flexoelectricity, where polarization is induced in non-
piezoelectric materials through strain gradients [11–13]. Complementing this effect is converse flexoelectricity, which describes the
linear relationship between stress and applied electric field gradients. Flexomagnetism, a phenomenon that links strain gradients to
induced magnetization, remains a largely unexplored frontier in materials science. Its complexity surpasses that of flexoelectricity,
primarily due to the indirect nature of its mechanism. While flexoelectricity involves a straightforward relationship between elec-
tric polarization and atomic displacement, flexomagnetism relies on the intricate reorientation of atomic spins following structural
deformations. The significance of flexomagnetism becomes particularly pronounced in nanostructures, where strain gradients can
be substantial. In contrast, macro-scale structures often exhibit negligible strain gradients, rendering flexomagnetic effects largely
inconsequential. Lukashev and Sabirianov [14] proposed a phenomenological approach to describe magnetomechanical coupling by
incorporating higher-gradient terms into the thermodynamic potential, drawing parallels with flexoelectric effect calculations. While
the piezomagnetic effect is limited by crystal symmetry constraints, Eliseev [15] has conducted extensive theoretical calculations
using symmetry theory to determine the form of the flexomagnetic tensor across various magnetic classes. This work suggests that
flexomagnetic coupling could potentially induce magnetic fields in a wider range of materials, opening up possibilities for developing
piezomagnetic-like structures beyond traditional piezomagnetic materials. Recent years have seen a surge in research exploring the
behavior of plane and surface waves in flexoelectric and flexomagnetic structures [16–18]. These studies are paving the way for a
deeper understanding of wave propagation in these novel materials, potentially leading to innovative applications in fields ranging
from sensors to energy harvesting devices.
Poroelasticity is crucial in soil dynamics, reservoir engineering, and various geological processes. The double porosity concept
distinguishes between two pore types: matrix pores, which provide substantial storage but limited fluid flow, and fracture pores, which
facilitate fluid movement but offer minimal storage. In order to refine fluid flow analysis and derive a symmetric dual-permeability
Darcy’s Law, Pride and Berryman [19] incorporated mesoscopic structure for understanding the complex nature of fluid movement
within hydrocarbon reservoirs by using volume averaging technique. This work stimulated further research on fractured poroelastic
models [20–22]. More recently, Kumar et al. [23] examined plane wave reflection in dual-porous thermoelastic materials. Das et al.
[24] investigated size-dependent micropolar media with dual porosity, focusing on thermal influences. Recently, Dutta et al. [25]
studied the impact of matrix and fracture pores on horizontally polarized wave behavior.
Nonlocal theory fundamentally challenges classical elasticity by proposing that a material’s behavior is not determined solely
by local conditions, but by the interconnected state of its entire system, i.e., the stress state is influenced by the strain conditions
throughout the entire body of the material. Researchers have extensively explored wave propagation characteristics using nonlocal
elasticity theory [26–28] laying critical groundwork for understanding these complex mechanical behaviors. Recent investigations
have further expanded our understanding of wave dynamics in nonlocal systems. Kaur et al. [29,30] conducted an in-depth analysis
of wave behavior in complex media, focusing on Love and Lamb waves traversing nonlocal porous elastic structures. Complementing
this research, Manna et al. [31] conducted an in-depth analysis of Love wave dispersion characteristics within finite layers. Dutta
et al. [32] and Said et al. [33] have continued to push the boundaries, investigating wave propagation through layered nonlocal
structures and exploring the impacts of memory-dependent derivatives and gravitational effects. Marin and his co-workers [34–36]
investigated wave behavior in different types of materials subjected to size-dependent elasticity theories.
Kumar et al. [37] conducted a significant study on Love wave propagation in a anisotropic fluid-filled porous layer with a rigid
boundary overlying an irregular, inhomogeneous half-space. Hrytsyna et al. [38] explored the combined influence of flexo-effects and
microinertia on Love wave propagation in a nano-sized layer bonded to piezomagnetic /piezoelectric substrates. Further advancing
the field, Gupta et al. [39] investigated the response of flexoelectricity and interfacial irregularity on SH-wave propagation in a func-
tionally graded, fissured porous layer. Despite these advancements, there remains a gap in our understanding of SH-wave propagation
in systems that combine size-dependent elasticity, flexomagnetism, and fluid-saturated fissured porous layers. The current research
work addresses these gaps by integrating several advanced structural formation which are given as follows;
• This study aims to investigate SH-wave propagation in a nonlocal fissured porous layer overlying a nonlocal flexomagnetic
substrate with a parabolic interfacial irregularity.
• The primary objective is to derive the complex frequency equation that governs the dispersive and attenuative behavior of
SH-waves in this composite structure.

The study’s findings have broad practical applications which are presented as below;
• The model can be used to monitor the integrity of critical structures made of these materials, such as aerospace components,
civil infrastructure (e.g., concrete with embedded sensors), or biomedical implants.
• Flexomagnetic materials exhibit a coupling between strain gradient and magnetic polarization. This unique property, combined
with SH-wave propagation, could lead to the development of highly sensitive sensors for various applications, including detection
of minute deformations or stress gradients in materials and creation of sensors that respond to localized magnetic fields induced
by flexomagnetic effects.
• The interaction of SH waves with fluid-saturated porous media can be used to infer properties of aquifers and groundwater flow.
• Poroelastic materials are fundamental to understanding fluid-saturated rocks. This model could improve the understanding of
seismic wave propagation in complex geological formations, particularly those with fissured or fractured rock, aiding in oil and
gas exploration and geothermal energy assessment.
• The interfacial irregularity aspect could be particularly useful for modeling fault zones.
• The model enhances seismic imaging for complex formations, aiding fractured reservoir and sedimentary basin characterization.
It improves flaw detection in composites, benefiting aerospace and civil engineering. The insights also advance acoustic wave
devices, particularly surface acoustic wave sensors for bio-sensing and chemical detection.
• Additionally, the results contribute to earthquake engineering by refining seismic risk assessments and earthquake-resistant
design.
Being motivated by the aforementioned facts, authors have considered to analyze the behavior of SH-waves propagating through
a stratum of nonlocal fluid-saturated fissured porous material overlying a substrate comprising nonlocal flexomagnetic material
under the purview of parabolic irregularity at the interface. By employing the Fourier transform and perturbation technique, a
complex frequency relation is determined to describe SH-wave propagation across the irregular interface. The dispersion and damping
equations are determined from the real and imaginary components of the frequency relation. The impact of various parameters, such
as the flexomagnetic coefficient, irregularity parameter, depth of source, porosity, piezomagnetic properties, and nonlocality are
visualized through graphical execution. Validation of the proposed model is represented through several cases.

8. Conclusions
This study investigates the propagation characteristics of Shear Horizontal (SH) waves in a fluid-saturated fissured porous material
layer overlying a flexomagnetic substrate with a parabolic interfacial irregularity. The model incorporates nonlocal elasticity theory,
flexomagnetic effects, and various material parameters to analyze wave dispersion and attenuation behavior. By applying Fourier
transform and perturbation techniques, complex frequency relations are derived to describe SH-wave propagation across the irregular
interface. Based on the comprehensive analysis of wave propagation characteristics in this complex layered system, several key
conclusions can be drawn.
• Complex wave behavior: The study reveals intricate dispersion and attenuation patterns across multiple wave modes, indicating
complex wave-material interactions in the layered system. This complexity is particularly evident in the frequency-dependent
velocity changes and mode-specific attenuation characteristics.
• Impact of structural irregularities: The irregularity parameter (𝐻 ∕𝐻) significantly influences wave propagation. Higher
0
irregularity values lead to more pronounced velocity variations and stronger attenuation, especially in the first mode. This
suggests that structural heterogeneities play a crucial role in modulating wave behavior.
• Source depth effects: Deeper wave sources result in more stable propagation characteristics, with delayed onset of dispersion
effects and reduced attenuation at lower frequencies. This finding has important implications for seismic exploration and wave
propagation studies in layered media.
• Porosity influence: Increased porosity generally leads to lower wave velocities and stronger attenuation across all modes.
This behavior is attributed to the reduced rigidity and increased energy dissipation in more porous materials, highlighting the
importance of porosity in wave propagation through fissured porous media.
• Flexomagnetic effects: The inclusion of flexomagnetic coupling introduces additional wave modulation and energy dissipation
mechanisms, particularly noticeable at higher frequencies. This effect modifies both dispersion and attenuation characteristics,
underscoring the importance of considering flexomagnetic properties in advanced material designs.
• Nonlocality parameters: The nonlocality parameters (𝜉 and 𝜉 ) significantly influence wave propagation, especially in higher
1 2
modes. Their presence consistently results in modified dispersion curves and enhanced attenuation, indicating the importance
of considering nonlocal effects in accurate wave modeling.
• Mode-specific behavior: Different wave modes exhibit distinct responses to various parameters, with the first mode typically
showing the highest sensitivity to changes in material properties and structural characteristics.
These findings have significant implications for various fields, including geophysics, materials science, and structural engineering.
They provide valuable insights into wave behavior in complex media, which can be applied to improve seismic analysis techniques,
design advanced materials with specific wave propagation properties, and enhance our understanding of wave dynamics in heteroge-
neous and layered systems. The study also highlights the importance of considering multiple factors, such as structural irregularities,
material properties, and nonlocal effects, in accurately predicting and analyzing wave propagation phenomena in complex media.
