Abstract
Purpose – This study aims to focus on a comparative analysis of three prominent models within the realm of
Rayleigh wave dynamics: the piezo-thermoelastic with voids, the piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor and the
composite piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor with voids.
Design/methodology/approach – An innovative mathematical framework has been meticulously
developed, incorporating the influential Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) theory. The investigation
commences with the formulation of governing equations that smoothly combine concepts from the voids,
semiconductor and piezoelectric theories. The governing equations are established under the purview of
Eringen’s nonlocal elasticity theory. These complex equations are expertly solved using an eigenmode
technique. The surface wave decay condition is then used to refine these root solutions.
Findings – This study delivers valuable insights into key parameters, encompassing surface particle motion,
attenuation coefficients and phase velocities. These outcomes are thoroughly compared across the three
models, offering a distinctive perspective on their individual characteristics. To enhance the understanding,

1. Introduction
Semiconductors are materials that sit on the line between conductors and insulators. They
exhibit unique electrical characteristics that are crucial for solar energy technology and other
renewable energy sources (Neamen, 1992). The domains of technology and science are given
new dimensions when integrated with piezoelectric capabilities. Piezoelectric semiconductors
have attracted a lot of attention from researchers. These materials exhibit the electro-thermo-
mechanical phenomena, in which they generate electricity through polarization in response to
mechanical stress or thermal loads (Smith, 2005). Recent advancements in high-performance
power electronic devices and distributed generation systems, such as power electronic
transformers and adaptive current controllers (Zhang et al., 2023; Cheng et al., 2024), reflect the
growing need for precise control of energy transfer in such coupled systems.
The investigation of thermoelastic characteristics in certain materials has facilitated the
advancement of traditional heat conduction theory. The theory under consideration suggests that
the propagation of heat waves occurs at an infinite velocity, a concept that clearly contradicts
scientific evidence. The classical coupled theory of thermoelasticity (CT), as highlighted by Biot
(1956), is primarily based on the classical heat conduction model. Consequently, it inherits the
aforementioned constraint. To surpass this fundamental limitation, a multitude of research
endeavors has been performed. Two notable competitors have emerged as significant theories of
thermoelasticity. One of the theories put forth by Lord and Shulman (1967)involves the inclusion
of a solitary thermal relaxation parameter. Expanding on the foundational Lord-Shulman
framework, Dhaliwal and Sherief (1980)further advanced the field by formulating equations for
generalized thermoelasticity. This significant addition incorporates the consideration of anisotropic
media, hence enhancing the applicability and relevance of the framework. Green and Naghdi
(1991, 1992, 1993) are prominent scholars in the field of thermoelasticity. The researchers’
ground-breaking efforts focus on the advancement of diverse thermoelastic theories that are
carefully designed to investigate the complex mechanisms of energy dissipation present in elastic
materials. The theories known as Green-Naghdi type II (GN-II) and Green-Naghdi type III (GN-
III) are considered to be ground-breaking in the field. These ideas have significantly advanced our
understanding concerning the way elastic materials behave when it comes to energy dissipation.
In 1974, Mindlin (1974) established the fundamental basis by developing a series of
governing equations that revealed the complex interplay between piezoelectric materials and
temperature. Building upon the groundwork laid by Nowacki (1978, 1979) in 1978 and 1979,
substantial contributions were made in the field of coupled piezo-thermoelastic diffusion
equations. Drawing inspiration from Mindlin’s revolutionary study, Chandrasekharaiah (1988)
undertook a comprehensive investigation whereby he introduced the pioneering notion of finite
wave signal within the domain of piezo-thermoelasticity. This invention represents a notable
advancement in our comprehension of the fundamental relationship between temperature and
piezoelectricity. An important turning point in the development of this discipline took place
when Aouadi (2006)made significant contributions by presenting a comprehensive theory of
thermoelasticity specifically designed to tackle the distinct challenges posed by piezoelectric
materials, which experience changes in their material constants as temperatures vary.
The pioneering contributions of Edelen and Laws (1971)have been credited with significant
progress in the domain of nonlocal interaction theory. Furthermore, Edelen et al. (1971)
effectively derived the constitutive equations that are fundamental to the theory of nonlocal

Advancements in nanofabrication, such as stitchless atomic force microscopy and large-area

advances in high-coupling symmetric transformers and neutron-irradiated thyristors (Deng et al.,
2024; Tong et al., 2025) emphasizing the synergy between device physics and material behavior
under extreme or dynamic conditions.
The analysis of Rayleigh waves within different types of generalized thermoelastic media
(Kumar et al., 2023; Kumar, 2025; Sharma, 2023; Gupta et al., 2024a) has increasingly
considered nonlocal elasticity and time-dependent memory effects as critical factors
influencing wave behavior. In recent times, there has been a significant increase in scholarly
research focusing on the Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) equation, as evidenced by studies
(Thompson and Beavers, 1972; Marchand et al., 2012; Lasiecka and Wang, 2015; Pellicer and
Solà-Morales, 2019). This equation, which originates from a third-order dynamic equation,
has been of significant importance in elucidating diverse facets of fluid dynamics. Quintanilla
(2019) has made noteworthy advancements in the field by discovering an intriguing
thermoelastic model, which originates from the MGT model. Quintanilla made improvements
to the existing model by introducing the relaxation parameter in the GN-III framework, which
improved the heat model that previously existed. The MGT theory has sparked a significant
increase in academic research endeavors focused on comprehending its complex ramifications
(Abouelregal et al., 2021; Gupta et al., 2022; Abouelregal et al., 2022).
Piezoelectric semiconductors with voids are a ground-breaking class of materials that have
appeared in the constantly changing semiconductor environment. These complex nanoscale-
engineered structures have the potential to revolutionize sensor and energy-harvesting
technologies. Thermal stresses can be produced, and mechanical changes can be caused when
photo-thermal techniques are used to activate an elastic semiconductor media. This
phenomenon is very important for studying the piezoelectric effect, especially in a variety of
disciplines like laser drilling. In a thorough investigation, (Lotfy and El-Bary, 2022; Aldwoah et
al., 2022; Alshehri and Lotfy, 2023) conducted a comprehensive examination of semiconductor
materials, examining several parameters, including pyroelectricity, the magnetic fields, electron-
hole effect and thermal conductivity. Studies on reverse recovery, asymmetric flux modulation
and hybrid excited machines (Shen et al., 2022, 2024; Huo, 2024) further enrich the landscape
of electromechanical interaction analysis – strengthening the motivation for examining the
wave propagation behavior in composite piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor systems.
Numerous scholars (Marin et al., 2013; Pathania et al., 2022; Abouelregal et al., 2022; Pathania
et al., 2023) have focused their research on exploring various facets of plane waves, with a
notable emphasis on fractional order, nonlocality and two-temperature models. Barak et al.
(Barak et al., 2023a; Gupta et al., 2023; Barak et al., 2023b; Gupta and Barak, 2023) examined
the energy distribution in a number of piezo-thermoelastic contact models. Lewis and his team
(Lewis et al., 2004a; Lewis et al., 1997; Lewis et al., 1991; Lewis and Garner, 1972; Lewis
et al., 2004b) offers a systematic approach to solving heat transfer and fluid dynamics problems
using finite element methods, integrating conduction, convection and porous media flow
analyses.

8. Validation
• Disregarding the influence of carrier density, nonlocality and voids the current piezo-
thermoelastic model aligns with a specific case discussed by Barak and Gupta
(2023).
• When we set ξ ! 0, omit the impact of voids and simplify the problem into one
dimension, the present model corresponds to the results elucidated by Khamis et al.
(2021).
• Without piezoelectric effect, voids and transformation into a one-dimensional
model, the current model coincides with a particular scenario elucidated in the work
of Lotfy and Sarkar (2017). This correspondence is observed where the thermal
temperature equals the conductive temperature.
• In the absence of temperature, carrier density, voids and nonlocality, the present
study conforms to a particular scenario discussed by Sharma et al. (2005).
• Disregarding the response of carrier density, nonlocality and voids the present model
agrees with the research conducted by Sharma et al. (2008).
• By disregarding the influences of voids, temperature, carrier density and nonlocal
effects, the present model reduces to a specific case previously examined by Sharma
(2023).

10. Numerical analysis and discussion
Expanding on the foundation laid in the previous section, this section explores the
propagation characteristics of a generalized Rayleigh wave in a piezo-thermoelastic
semiconductor half-space with voids. Given the complexity of the mathematical expressions
involved, interpreting them in an abstract manner can be challenging. To provide clearer
insights, we present a numerical example that examines the influence of several key
parameters on the wave propagation behavior. The selected material for the computational
study is Cadmium Selenide (CdSe), which is chosen based on its unique material
characteristics as described in previous studies conducted by Gupta et al. (2024a, 2024b)
which are further consolidated in Table 1.
The thermal relaxation time is linked to the finite speed of heat propagation, as described
in the MGT framework, and is typically derived from experimental studies or cited literature
for materials like CdSe. Similarly, the nonlocal parameter is associated with the internal
characteristic length of the medium and reflects the scale at which long-range interactions
become significant. This clarification aims to assist readers in replicating or adapting the
model for different material systems.
10.1 Impact of nonlocal parameter on attenuation coefficient and phase velocity
Figure 2(a)and (b), illustrates the impact of the nonlocal parameter (ξ) on Rayleigh wave
propagation in a piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor material with voids. In Figure 2(a), the
variation of phase velocity with respect to the wave number is depicted for four different
values of ξ(= 0, 0.003, 0.006, 0.009). It is observed that as the wave number increases, the
phase velocity also increases. The influence of the nonlocal parameter is significant, as
higher values of ξ lead to a noticeable increase in phase velocity. This indicates that nonlocal
effects enhance wave speed by accounting for long-range interactions within the material,
effectively reducing stiffness constraints at the local scale. When ξ=0, the material behaves
as a classical continuum, yielding the lowest phase velocity. However, as ξ increases, the
wave experiences a stronger nonlocal influence, leading to a steeper rise in velocity.
Figure 2(b) illustrates the variation of attenuation with respect to the wave number for
different values of ξ. The attenuation decreases as the wave number increases, but its rate of
decrease is influenced by the nonlocal parameter. Higher values of ξ result in a more rapid
decline in attenuation, suggesting that nonlocal effects contribute to reduced energy
dissipation. When ξ=0, the classical model predicts relatively higher attenuation, whereas
increasing ξ introduces a nonlocal softening effect, leading to reduced wave damping. This
behavior suggests that in highly nonlocal regimes, wave energy is sustained over longer
distances, which is crucial for applications requiring low-loss wave propagation.
The nonlocal parameter plays a pivotal role in altering both phase velocity and attenuation
in piezo-semiconductor materials with voids. As this parameter increases, it enhances wave
speed while concurrently reducing attenuation. This underscores the significance of nonlocal
elasticity in wave dynamics. These findings hold substantial implications for the design of
cutting-edge piezoelectric and semiconductor-based devices, such as high-frequency
acoustic sensors and waveguides. By leveraging nonlocal effects, it is possible to optimize
wave propagation characteristics, which is crucial for achieving high-performance signal
transmission and detection in these devices.

11. Conclusions
This study presents a comparative analysis of three significant models in Rayleigh wave
dynamics: piezo-thermoelastic with voids, piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor and the
composite piezo-thermoelastic semiconductor with voids medium. Utilizing an advanced
mathematical framework based on the MGT theory, the research systematically formulates
governing equations and solves them using the eigenmode technique. The impact of critical
parameters such as the nonlocal effect, material configuration and depth variations on phase
velocity, attenuation coefficient and particle motion is extensively analyzed. The findings
