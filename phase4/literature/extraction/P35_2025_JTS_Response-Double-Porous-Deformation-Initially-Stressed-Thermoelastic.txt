1. Introduction
Virtually all solid substances, naturally or artificially, possess varying degrees of porosity and
exhibit elastic behavior. An elastic porous solid may exhibit either interconnectivity or non-
interconnectivity among its pores. A porous elastic material whose pores are not linked in any
way is known as an elastic material having voids, and if porous are linked, the material is known
as a porous elastic material. Furthermore, the pores may exhibit non-uniformity in size and can
exist in diverse forms and dimensions. Multiple porosities are the property of an elastic solid,
including different sizes of pores. Multiple porosities are vital in many areas, such as geophysics,
petroleum sciences, chemical engineering, and biomechanics. It is also known that when a mater-
ial undergoes heating, the voids within the material undergo expansion, while during the cooling
process, the voids contract due to the thermal impact.
Lord and Shulman [1] modified the coupled thermoelasticity (CT) theory by incorporating a
time derivative, a heat flow vector, and a wave-type heat conduction equation was introduced.
For porous elastic materials, a nonlinear theory (NT) is introduced by Nunziato and Cowin [2].
According to NT theory, an extra degree of freedom, the fractional elementary volume, is incor-
porated into every material particle. For thermoelastic materials with pores, a linear theory is

longitudinal waves, are present in the medium. Using the linear theory of elasto-thermo dual type

et al. [19,20] investigated the transmission phenomena of wave transpiring at the boundary
between a thermomechanical solid medium with dual porosity and an incompressible liquid

fluid, elastic, piezo thermoelastic, and thermoelastic interfaces. This investigation was carried out
within the context of multiple heat transfer models.
In the medium, the initial stresses are due to many causes, including temperature differences,
gravity variations, quenching process, etc. Initial pressures on the earth are expected to be signifi-
cant. The influence of these pressures on thermal and mechanical conditions is of great interest.
Montanaro [31] investigated the linear isotropic thermoelastic theory using the body’s basic con-
figuration in a state of initial hydrostatic stress. By using the concept of initial stress and the LS
theory, El-Naggar et al. [32] discussed the propagation of progressive waves in a homogenous iso-
tropic elastic material. Othman et al. [33] investigated the plane strain problem of an anisotropic
homogeneous thermal elastic porous material that is initially stressed. Khalil et al. [34] discussed

using the LS and DPL theory. Kumar et al. [35–37] investigated various problems within the con-
text of different thermoelasticity theories. The work conducted by Lotfy and his coworkers
[38–43] focused on the propagation of waves in elastic, thermal elastic, semiconductor mediums
under the context of most recent theory of thermoelasticity.
This manuscript introduces an innovative mathematical model to investigate the interactions
within an isotropic initially stressed, homogeneous, double porous thermo elastic half-space, uti-
lizing the DPL theory of thermoelasticity as the foundational framework. Despite the existence of
multiple research challenges within such a medium, characterized by homogeneity, isotropy, and
double porosity, no prior study has been undertaken to explore the spatial distributions of various
physical fields. These fields encompass quantities like normal stress, shear stresses, normal dis-
placement, temperature distribution field, and equilibrated stresses resulting from both types of
voids. By utilizing eigenmode analysis, exact analytical solutions of different field variables are
derived. The analytical results obtained are then translated into numerical computations and visu-
ally depicted, with a specific emphasis on a material resembling a magnesium crystal. The study
encompasses the impact of phase lags, initial stress, and time within the analyzed medium. The
implications of these findings extend across diverse engineering sub-domains, including soil
dynamics, seismology, geophysics, and earthquake engineering, among others.

8. Conclusions
This article presents an innovative mathematical model within the domain of generalized thermo-
elasticity, with a specific emphasis on a homogeneous double porous initially stressed isotropic
thermoelastic solid half-space. The study is conducted within the framework of the DPL theory.
The methodology involves utilizing normal mode analysis to derive accurate solutions for the
physical field variables described by the governing equations of the proposed model. By incorpo-
rating appropriate boundary conditions, the analysis leads to the following noteworthy
conclusions:
1. The numerical and graphical findings reveal that the phase lag, time, and initial stress consid-
erably impact the physical field quantities.
2. The equilibrium stress due to type-I voids has a positive impact due to initial stress in the
initial part of the domain, but it exhibits a negative impact in the later part of the domain.
However, the influence of initial stress on the equilibrated stress due to type-II voids gets
reversed to its effect on equilibrated stress due to type-I voids.
3. Initial stress has a positive impact on the temperature distribution field but a negative effect
on normal stresses, shear stress, and normal displacement.
4. When compared to the LS model of thermoelasticity, both the equilibrated stresses (due to
type-I, II voids) and temperature field distribution have higher values in the DPL model;
however, the generalized theories exhibit a contrary impact on normal stress, shear stress,
and normal displacement.
5. The time parameter exhibits a substantial amplifying influence on the equilibrated stress due
to type-I, and II voids, shear stress, normal stress, normal displacement, and the temperature
field distribution.
The inclusion of initial stress in a thermoelastic medium makes the considered model more
realistic. The present study is helpful in various engineering subfields such as soil dynamics, seis-
mology, geophysics, and earthquake engineering.
