relation was then separated into dispersion and attenuation equations to represent the dispersive and damping
characteristics of SH-waves in the LoL model. The study presents the classical case as a particular instance along
with various other cases obtained by relaxing certain assumptions from the present model. To visualize the
impact of key parameters such as viscosity, NVL thickness, permittivity, piezoelectricity, nonlocality parameters
of NVL and NFL, imperfectness, fractional-order derivative, and flexoelectricity on dispersive and damping

and attenuation curves. This was achieved by deriving the lower and upper bounds for SH-wave velocity.
Additionally, the influence of key parameters on the surface response of nonlocal shear stresses and particle
displacement within the LoL structure is graphically depicted as a function of depth. The findings reveal
that SH-wave characteristics are significantly more diverse in the size-dependent LoL model compared to
the classical LoL model. The findings of this study hold significant promise for advancing the design and
functionality of various technological applications. By enhancing our understanding of surface wave dynamics
in size-dependent structures combining flexoelectric and viscoelastic materials, this research paves the way for
innovations in sensor technology, energy harvesting systems, and devices capable of manipulating waves.

The imperfect interface introduces a discontinuity that can affect wave
transmission and reflection, potentially leading to energy dissipation
at the interface. In contrast, the perfect interface allows for unimpeded
wave propagation between layers. Understanding these effects is crucial
for accurately predicting the behavior of layered structures in various
applications, such as seismic isolation systems or acoustic waveguides.
9. Conclusion
This study investigated the propagation of shear horizontal (SH)
waves in a size-dependent thin-walled layer over layer (LoL) structure
with an imperfect interface. The model consists of a nonlocal viscoelas-
tic layer (NVL) resting on a nonlocal flexoelectric layer (NFL). Through
analytical solutions and numerical simulations, we examined the effects
of various parameters on wave dispersion and attenuation. Key findings
include
• Viscosity significantly influences wave attenuation more than dis-
persion, highlighting its crucial role in energy dissipation mecha-
nisms. This has important implications for modeling wave prop-
Fig. 15. Surface response of displacement component with respect to depth (x) in the agation in Earth materials with viscous properties.
presence of imperfect interface between NVL and NFL. • Layer thickness affects both dispersion and attenuation, with a
more pronounced impact on dispersion characteristics. This em-
phasizes the importance of considering layer geometry in seismic
wave analysis.
• Permittivity and piezoelectric constants predominantly influence
phase velocities, underscoring the importance of electrical prop-
erties in wave propagation through piezoelectric materials.
• Nonlocal effects in the viscoelastic layer reduce phase veloci-
ties but increase damping velocities, revealing complex material
behavior at small scales. This highlights the need to consider
long-range interactions in nanoscale structures.
• The nonlocal flexoelectric layer exhibits higher phase and damp-
ing velocities compared to local models, emphasizing the signifi-
cance of flexoelectric effects in nanoscale wave propagation.
• Interface imperfections decrease phase velocities but increase
damping velocities, demonstrating the critical role of interfacial
conditions in wave transmission. This is particularly relevant for
modeling wave behavior in layered geological structures.
These findings enhance our understanding of wave propagation
in advanced layered structures, with potential applications in seismic
wave analysis, nanoelectromechanical systems (NEMS), and energy
