This manuscript presents a detailed analytical study on the propagation behavior of circumferential
shear horizontal (SH) waves in a tri-layered cylindrical composite structure, comprising an inner
functionally graded (FG) layer, a viscoelastic (VE) middle layer, and an initially stressed piezomagnetic
(PM) outer layer. Unlike prior studies that primarily focus on phase velocity analysis, this work offers
a novel and in-depth exploration of stress distribution characteristics across the multilayered system.
The model incorporates both mechanical and magneto-mechanical interface imperfections and
examines the effects of geometric dimensions, material gradation, angular wave modes, and initial
stresses on wave dynamics. A robust mathematical framework is developed using the equations of
motion and constitutive relations specific to each material, leading to a dispersion relation formulated
under suitable boundary conditions. Numerical simulations demonstrate that the phase velocity and
surface wave responses are notably influenced by gradation parameters, layer radii, and imperfection
factors. Most significantly, detailed stress profiles are constructed to reveal how material damping,
gradation, and interface conditions interact to shape the internal stress landscape. This dual-phase
velocity and stress analysis offers enhanced insight into wave–material interactions, providing a
valuable design reference for next-generation piezomagnetic sensors, actuators, and composite
structural components.

Piezomagnetic material, Composite cylinder
SH wave propagation has emerged as a critical research area due to its applicability in structural health
monitoring, seismic analysis, and advanced device design1–3. Unlike longitudinal or Rayleigh waves, SH-
waves propagate with particle motion perpendicular to the direction of propagation and are less influenced by
surface conditions, making them ideal for characterizing subsurface or interfacial features4,5. Several studies
have extensively analyzed the dispersive and damping behaviors of SH-waves6–8. In parallel, researchers have
investigated a broad range of structural dynamics issues, such as vortex-induced vibrations in risers9, vibration
characteristics of damped systems10, and discrete wave dynamics in mechanical chains11. Despite the technical
sophistication of these models, most assume perfectly bonded interfaces. However, in realistic engineering and
geological settings, interfaces often exhibit imperfections, such as voids, cracks, or partial bonding that can

in modulating both the phase velocity and the stress distribution of SH-waves, thereby necessitating a deeper
investigation into their coupled effects. Recent developments in smart sensing materials and structural health
monitoring systems have highlighted the importance of coupled stress–wave behavior and multifunctional

SH waves have been extensively investigated in cylindrical structures due to their unique polarization
perpendicular to the propagation direction and circumferential symmetry, which make them highly suitable for
applications in sensing, non-destructive evaluation, and wave guiding devices18. In recent years, smart material
composite materials have attracted significant attention for their superior mechanical performance, tunable
wave propagation characteristics, and multifunctional capabilities. Tong et al.19 expanded this framework to
include multiphase cylindrical models examining thermal, magnetic, and mechanical interactions in fiber
composites. In underground infrastructure and geomechanical engineering, data-driven prediction techniques
and advanced diagnostic methods have been widely employed to analyze tunnel stability, pipeline settlement,
and rock damage evolution under dynamic loading conditions20–22. Furthermore, modern studies on wave
propagation, structural optimization, vibration characteristics, and electro-mechanical interactions in composite
and piezoelectric media demonstrate the growing relevance of multiphysics wave analysis in complex structural
configurations23–27.
Recent studies have extensively investigated the dynamic behavior and mechanical response of advanced shell
and curved structures under various loading conditions. Analytical and computational models for cylindrical,
conical, and spherical shells have demonstrated significant applications in vibration analysis, structural
stability, and multifunctional composite systems28–32. These contributions highlight the growing importance
of shell-based configurations in modern engineering and motivate further investigation of wave propagation
phenomena in multilayer cylindrical structures. The study of wave dynamics in cylindrical geometries is critical
across diverse domains, including structural engineering, materials science, geophysics, biomedical engineering,
nanotechnology, acoustics, and applied physics33–35. Viscoelastic materials have emerged as an essential
component in controlling dynamic behavior in composite cylindrical structures due to their inherent damping
capabilities and time-dependent mechanical response36,37. Akbarov and Kepceler38 analyzed torsional wave
characteristics in hollow viscoelastic cylinders, while Zhang et al.39 studied guided wave behavior in Kelvin-
Voigt (KV) viscoelastic cylinders featuring material gradation.
Material gradation has been widely investigated as a means to tailor mechanical and electro-mechanical,
and magneto-mechanical behavior in cylindrical structures. Notably, Khoshgoftar et al.40 analyzed the impact
of graded material properties on thermoelastic responses in piezoelectric cylinders, while Othman and Abbas41
discussed how non-uniform material characteristics influence the response of isotropic cylinders subjected to
thermal shock using finite element methods. Guo et al.42 probed into the influence exerted by functionally
graded intermediate layers on the governing SH waves within layered piezoelectric/piezomagnetic cylinders.
Mahanty et al.43 probed into shear acoustic wave conveyance through layered cylindrical configurations
exhibiting imperfect bonding between functionally graded piezoelectric material (FGPM) strata. Gupta and his
team study various problem related to wave dynamics in piezo-thermoelastic media44–48. Recently, Kumawat and
Vishwakarma49, Gupta et al.50 scrutinized SH-wave propagation across multi-layered cylinders.
Moreover, although several previous studies have analyzed SH-wave dispersion, their focus has predominantly
been limited to calculating the phase velocity. The specific influence of the gradation parameter on wave
dynamics and surface responses remains insufficiently explored, particularly in configurations incorporating
viscoelastic damping and interfacial imperfections. Motivated by these gaps, the present study investigates
SH-wave propagation in a tri-layered cylindrical composite comprising an inner functionally graded layer, a
viscoelastic middle layer, and an initially stressed piezomagnetic outer layer. A mathematical model is developed
to derive dispersion relations while rigorously incorporating mechanical and magneto-mechanical interface
imperfections.
Modeling of the structural geometry
The physical setup is a tri-layered cylindrical construct featuring concentric arrangements: an internal
functionally graded (FG) layer, an orthotropic viscoelastic (VE) material middle layer, and an externally
positioned, initially stressed piezomagnetic (PM) layer. The FG layer has a thickness of (r 1− r 0 ), enclosed by
the VE layer having a thickness of (r 2− r 1 ). The outermost PM layer has a thickness of (r 3− r 2 ), as shown in
Fig. 1. Due to the significantly smaller cross-sectional dimensions compared to their axial lengths, the cylinders
are idealized as infinitely extended along the longitudinal direction. The concentric layers are assumed to be
imperfectly bonded at their interfaces, giving rise to discontinuities in both mechanical and magneto-mechanical
interactions. The entire system is analyzed within a cylindrical coordinate framework (r, θ, z), where the origin
O is positioned along the central axis of the structure. In this study, the SH-wave is considered to propagate
along the circumferential (θ) direction with particle motion in the axial (z) direction, perpendicular to the plane
of propagation, satisfying the condition of shear horizontal motion in a cylindrical structure. As a result, wave
displacement is confined to the z direction. Thus, the displacement components given as51

Numerical simulations and analyses
To understand the complex behavior of SH-wave propagation in a tri-layered cylindrical structure, this section
undertakes a comprehensive computational study focused on key elastic and material parameters. A schematic
overview of the system’s geometry, including precise dimensions and material distributions for each layer, is
provided in Fig. 2. These values are based on established literature, ensuring consistency with prior theoretical
and experimental studies49,52,55,56. Wave motion within each cylindrical layer is characterized by analytically
solving the governing equations under the appropriate boundary conditions, as established in “Wave dispersion
analysis” section. This process leads to the formulation of a dispersion relation that captures the essential wave
mechanics. A comparative evaluation is carried out through dispersion plots, where normalized phase velocity

Influence of layer radii on phase velocity
This subsection investigates how the radii of individual layers within the cylindrical composite influence the
propagation of circumferential SH-waves. The system consists of three concentric layers: a functionally graded
(FG) orthotropic inner core, a viscoelastic (VE) damping layer, and an outer piezomagnetic (PM) shell. The
variation of normalized phase velocity (c/c SH) is plotted as a function of the dimensionless wavenumber (kr 0)
for varying values of the respective radii: r 1 (FG), r 2 (VE), and r 3 (PM), as shown in Fig. 3a–c.
Figure 3a shows how the dispersion characteristics change with different values of the inner radius
r 1 =2.0, 2.4, 2.8. Interestingly, the response is non-monotonic—the red curve (r 1 =2.4) lies above both
the blue (r 1 =2.0) and yellow (r 1 =2.8) curves across a large portion of the wavenumber spectrum. This
indicates that an intermediate radius leads to a higher phase velocity compared to both smaller and larger radii.
Such behavior arises from the radial variation of material properties in the FG layer, where wave propagation
is influenced by the spatial stiffness and density distribution. An optimal radius appears to align wave energy
with stiffer regions of the FG material, enhancing wave speed, whereas too small or too large a radius reduces
this alignment.
Similar non-monotonic behavior is observed in Fig. 3b, which displays the effect of the viscoelastic layer radius
r 2 =3.0, 3.4, 3.8. The red curve (r 2 =3.4) lies above both the blue (r 2 =3.0) and yellow (r 2 =3.8) curves,
particularly for low and moderate wavenumbers. This suggests that there exists an intermediate VE thickness
that optimally balances stiffness and damping. A thinner VE layer contributes less to damping, while a thicker
one may overly suppress wave speed due to enhanced energy dissipation. The peak phase velocity at r 2 =3.4
reflects this trade-off between wave support and damping within the VE layer.
In contrast, Fig. 3c shows a monotonic trend with respect to the outer radius r 3 =4.0, 4.4, 4.8. As r 3
increases, the phase velocity uniformly increases across the wavenumber range, with no curve crossing.
However, the differences between curves are subtle, as highlighted in the magnified inset. This indicates that the
piezomagnetic layer’s effect on SH wave speed is stable and predictable, though relatively minor in the absence
of active magnetic field interaction. The smooth monotonic increase suggests that increasing the PM layer
thickness slightly enhances the effective stiffness of the boundary, leading to a gentle rise in phase velocity.
The analysis highlights the significant impact of each layer’s geometric characteristics on wave propagation
within the multilayered cylindrical system. Notably, the inner functionally graded (FG) and middle viscoelastic
(VE) layers demonstrate non-linear behavior, suggesting the presence of specific radius values that optimize
wave transmission. In comparison, the outer piezomagnetic (PM) layer influences phase velocity in a more
consistent and linear manner. These observations underscore the potential for tuning layer thicknesses to
effectively modulate the structure’s dynamic performance.

Influence of angular mode on phase velocity
Figure 5 illustrates the variation of normalized phase velocity (c/c SH) with respect to the dimensionless wave
number (kr 0) for different angular mode numbers (n=1, 4, 8). The angular mode number (n) characterizes
the number of circumferential wave cycles around the cylindrical geometry and directly influences the wave’s
angular structure.
Across all modes, a consistent trend is observed wherein the normalized phase velocity decreases as the
dimensionless wavenumber increases, indicative of dispersive SH-wave behavior. However, the variation in
phase velocity with increasing n is distinctly nonlinear. The lowest angular mode (n=1) exhibits the highest
phase velocity across the entire range of wavenumbers shown. As the angular mode number increases from
n=1 to n=4, a pronounced and rapid decline in phase velocity is observed. The n=4 mode (red curve)
shows considerably lower velocities compared to the n=1 mode, especially at lower wavenumbers. Further
increasing the angular mode number from n=4 to n=8 results in a smaller, less pronounced decrease in
phase velocity. The n=8 mode (yellow curve) is closer to the n=1 curve than the n=4 curve, particularly
at higher wave numbers.
This behavior suggests that the influence of the angular mode number on phase velocity does not scale
linearly. The transition from lower to moderate modes introduces a stronger shift in wave dynamics than
the jump from moderate to higher modes. Physically, higher n values correspond to more tightly confined
waveforms around the circumference, which increases the effective inertia and reduces the propagation speed.
However, after a certain point (as seen between n=1 and n=8), this effect saturates, and the curves tend to
converge, especially at larger wavenumbers.
In conclusion, the angular mode number n plays a crucial and nonlinearly scaling role in SH wave propagation.
Lower modes are associated with faster and more stable wave transmission, while higher modes introduce sharp
velocity drops that eventually level off, reflecting a saturation effect in the modal influence on dispersive behavior.
This trend emphasizes the critical role of the mode number in analyzing wave behavior within cylindrical
structures, especially when precise control over wave velocity is required for targeted applications.

Conclusion
This study presents a comprehensive investigation into the propagation of circumferential SH-waves in a three-
layered cylindrical composite system incorporating FG, VE, and PM materials. Emphasizing the effects of
mechanical imperfections, material gradation, and initial stresses, the analysis has yielded valuable insights into
the wave dynamics and stress behavior crucial for magneto-mechanical applications. Based on these findings,
the following conclusions can be drawn:
• The inner FG layer and middle VM layer exhibit non-monotonic influence on phase velocity, pointing to the
presence of optimal radii where wave propagation is most efficient.
• The phase velocity exhibits a nonlinear and asymmetric response to the gradation parameter l 1. Positive
gradation (l 1 =0.5) yields the highest velocity, inverse gradation (l 1 = − 1) gives a moderate increase, while
the homogeneous case (l 1 =0) results in the lowest velocity—highlighting the strong influence of outward
stiffness distribution on wave propagation.
• The phase velocity exhibits a nonlinear dependence on the angular mode number n; a sharp drop is observed
from low to moderate modes, while higher modes show diminishing variation, indicating a saturation effect
in wave speed reduction at large n.
• The material gradation parameter significantly influences stress distribution across the layered structure, with
higher values promoting more uniform stress profiles and minimizing concentrations, thereby allowing pre-
cise tailoring of material properties for optimized load-bearing capacity and functional performance.
