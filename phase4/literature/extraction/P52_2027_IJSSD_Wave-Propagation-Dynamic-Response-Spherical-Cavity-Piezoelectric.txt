This study investigates the transient dynamic response and thermomechanical stabil-
ity of a transversely isotropic piezoelectric medium featuring a spherical cavity. To
accurately model the small-scale effects inherent in advanced structural components,
a spatiotemporal nonlocal elasticity framework of the Klein-Gordon type is employed,
incorporating both internal length and intrinsic time scale parameters. The governing
equationsoftheMoore-Gibson-Thompson(MGT)thermoelasticmodelarereformulated
using a nonsingular Rabotnov-type fractional exponential kernel, providing a robust
mathematicalformulationtocapturememory-dependentinteractionswithoutthepara-
doxofinfinitepropagationspeeds.Thestructuralboundaryofthecavityissubjectedtoa
ramp-typethermalloadingandelectricalgrounding,simulatingrealisticoperationalcon-
ditionsforsensorsandactuators.UsingtheLaplacetransformtechniqueandtheZakian
numerical inversion method, the transient distributions of temperature, displacement,
stress, and electric potential are derived. The results highlight the significant influence

1. Introduction
The utilization of piezoelectric solids has grown remarkably over the past few
decadesduetotheiradaptablenatureinsmartmaterialsandmultifunctionalstruc-
tures.Thesesolidspossessanintrinsicabilitytointerconvertelectricalandmechan-
ical forms of energy, making them essential for precision engineering and advanced
electromechanical applications. This electro-mechanical coupling facilitates their
widespread adoption in ultra-sensitive actuators, micro-positioning systems, and
precision sensing devices, thereby enhancing their value across modern functional
material technologies.1
A defining feature of piezoelectric materials is their intrinsic multiscale het-
erogeneity. Even within a single crystal, the presence of polarization domains with
distinctcrystallographicorientationsleadstospatialvariationsinelastic,dielectric,
andpiezoelectricproperties.Thisheterogeneitybecomesmorepronouncedinpoly-
crystalline ceramics, where each grain exhibits its own domain configuration and
interacts anisotropically with neighboring grains, resulting in highly non-uniform
electroelastic fields. Processing-induced elastic, electrical, and thermal anisotropies
generateresidualstressesandinternalmicrofields,whichmayrelaxthroughdomain
switching or microcrack formation, thereby modifying the macroscopic response.
Furthermore, porosity can either enhance or degrade the overall electromechani-
calefficiency.2–4 Atlargerscales,piezoelectricfibersembeddedinpolymermatrices
formhybridcompositeswhoseeffectivebehaviorisgovernedbytheelectromechani-
calpropertiesoftheceramicinclusionsandthefiber–matrixinterfacialinteractions.
Theresultingspatialdistributionoflocalelectroelasticfieldsultimatelycontrolsthe
global response under coupled thermo-electro-mechanical loading.
Fractional-order thermoelasticity extends classical thermoelastic theory by in-
corporatingfractionalcalculustomodelelasticmaterialssubjectedtothermalloads
withgreaterfidelity.Theintroductionofafractional-orderparameterαenablesthe
representation of memory effects and nonlocal interactions, allowing heat and me-
chanical disturbances to propagate with finite speeds. This framework provides
a more realistic description of wave propagation and coupled thermal-mechanical
responsesthanconventionalinteger-ordermodels,withapplicationsspanningengi-
neering systems and biomechanical materials.

The principal advantage of the MGT model lies in its ability to overcome the

tional relaxation parameters, the MGT framework ensures finite-speed thermal

equations and may pose challenges in parameter identification from experimental
data.9,10 Furthermore, the applicability of the MGT framework is most relevant at
short time scales or high-frequency thermal processes; for slow thermal evolutions,
themodelnaturallyreducestoclassicalthermoelasticformulations,whereitsadded
complexity may not yield significant advantages. These aspects have now been ex-
plicitly acknowledged in the manuscript to provide a balanced and transparent
discussion of the scope and applicability of the model.
The use of fractional differential equations in modeling real-world phenomena
hasattractedsignificantattentioninrecentyears.Owingtotheirinherentmemory
and hereditary properties,11–13 fractional derivatives provide a more accurate and
comprehensive framework for describing complex physical processes, including dif-
fusion and transport, electrical circuits, fluid dynamics, viscoelastic materials, and
financial systems. In the past decade, fractional-order operators have also been in-
creasingly adopted in epidemiological modeling, where they effectively capture the
nonlocal and memory-dependent dynamics15–18 underlying the spread of infectious
diseases.19
Thermoelasticity with a fractional Rabotnov kernel introduces a generalized
heat-conduction framework in which the classical Fourier law is replaced by a
constitutive relation involving a hereditary (memory) integral whose kernel fol-
lows a Rabotnov-type fractional function. In this approach the heat flux q is ex-
pressed as a convolution of the temperature gradient with a fading-memory kernel
K (t) ∼ t−αE (λtα), where 0 < α < 1 and E is the Mittag-Leffler function.

ThefractionalRabotnovkernelofferssignificantadvantagesinheat-conduction
modeling by incorporating memory and nonlocal effects, ensuring that the present
heat flux reflects the complete thermal history of the material. Through its
fractional-order parameter α, the model provides continuous tunability between
classical Fourier diffusion (α = 1) and anomalous sub-diffusion (α < 1), enabling
accurate fitting of experimental data without introducing ad hoc parameters. Un-
like the classical Fourier law, it predicts finite-speed thermal wave propagation,
consistent with second-sound and ballistic heat-transport phenomena observed at
micro- and nanoscales. When embedded within thermoelastic field equations, the
Rabotnov kernel enhances damping, stabilizes thermal stresses, and improves the
fidelity of dynamic analyses, offering a mathematically robust and physically real-
istic framework for advanced thermoelastic applications such as energy-harvesting
composites and microelectronic systems where classical diffusion models fail.
Forsimulatingheterogeneouspiezoelectricsubstances,wherecoupledelectrome-
chanical and thermal forces show considerable memory and nonlocal effects, the
inclusion of the Rabotnov exponential kernel in a fractional thermoelastic frame-
workprovidessubstantialscientificbenefits.Indescribinglong-termrelaxationand
retardation processes, the Rabotnov kernel ensures both mathematical regularity
and physical realism by offering a non-singular, exponentially decaying memory
function. This is especially helpful in heterogeneous piezoelectric materials, where
differences in composition, polarization, and microstructure lead to anomalies in
electrothermalcoupling,rate-dependentstressresponses,andnon-Fourierheatcon-
duction that are not captured by classical models. The current method allows for
the precise prediction of time-delayed thermo-electromechanical interactions and
finite-speed heat wave propagation in graded or composite domains by combining
the fractional-order derivative with the dual-phase-lag idea. An important devel-
opment for the design and performance optimization of smart structures, sensors,
and energy-harvesting devices operating under transient thermal and mechanical
fields is the unified treatment of fractional thermoelasticity and electromechanical
coupling through the Rabotnov kernel, which offers a generalized theoretical basis
for analyzing memory-dependent, heterogeneous piezoelectric media.
Motivated by these considerations, the present study examines thermoelas-
tic interactions in a heterogeneous medium containing a spherical bore, with
particular emphasis on coupled spatiotemporal nonlocal effects. Unlike classi-
cal or singular fractional formulations, the constitutive relations are enriched
by incorporating a nonsingular Rabotnov-type exponential kernel within the
Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) thermoelastic framework, enabling a physically
consistent description of finite-speed heat transport with memory. This formula-
tion bridges the gap between classical diffusion-based models and experimentally
observed non-Fourier thermal responses in heterogeneous solids. To elucidate the
resulting thermomechanical wave propagation, a coupled loading configuration is
imposed by prescribing both mechanical traction and a temporally localized pulse
heat source on the inner surface of the cavity, thereby providing a realistic set-
