electricthermoelasticquasicrystalhalf-spacewithLord–Shulmanheatconduction. Three
phason transport laws are examined. They are inertial, friction-controlled diffusive, and
telegraph-type transport. The electric field decouples for propagation normal to the pe-

1 Introduction
Quasicrystals exhibit long-range orientational order and rotational symmetries that are
incompatible with periodic crystals[1,2]. In the standard description, a quasicrystal is ob-
tainedbyprojectingaperiodicstructureinahigher-dimensionalspaceontophysicalspace[3,4].
The physical-space component of the higher-dimensional displacement defines the conventional
phonon displacement. The complementary component represents local atomic rearrangements
of the quasiperiodic structure and defines the phason field[5–7]. Phasons modify the occupation
of local atomic configurations. They possess their own stiffness and balance law and couple
to the phonon field through the phonon–phason coupling constants. The associated elastic-
ity theory[8,9] is reviewed in Refs. [10,11]. Two-dimensional (2D) hexagonal quasicrystals are
quasiperiodic in a plane and periodic in the normal direction, similar to decagonal phases.
The phason field is a vector in the quasiperiodic plane, and the corresponding phason stress is
generally asymmetric.
Phason dynamics are less well established than phason deformation, particularly with re-
spect to the appropriate transport law. Hydrodynamic analyses describe the phason as a slow
dissipative variable with diffusive relaxation[7,8]. Experiments on icosahedral phases have ob-
servedphasonfluctuationsandphason-assistedatomicdiffusiononlaboratorytimescales[12,13].
At shorter time scales, the phason field may instead be assigned inertia. Elastic-wave analyses
have examined phonon and phason wave parameters under both descriptions[14]. Agiasofitou
andLazar[15] combinedthesedescriptionsinawave-telegraphmodelthatincludesbothphason
inertia and friction. The inertial and diffusive laws are recovered as limiting cases, while the
friction-to-inertia ratio defines a crossover frequency. This framework has subsequently been
appliedtoquasicrystaldislocations[16],theGreentensorofgeneralisedquasicrystalelasticity[17],
and piezoelectric quasicrystals[18].
Heat conduction presents a related problem. Classical coupled thermoelasticity predicts an
infinitethermal-signalspeed, whereasgeneralisedtheoriesallowfinitepropagationspeeds. The
single-relaxation-time theory of Lord and Shulman[19] is widely used for this purpose, together
with the Green–Lindsay theory[20]. Related developments are reviewed in Refs. [21–23]. The
telegraph phason law and the Lord–Shulman law have a similar mathematical structure. In
both cases, a relaxation term converts a diffusion equation into a damped wave equation.
GeneralisedheatconductionhasbeenincorporatedintostudiesofRayleigh-typeandbulkwaves
in smart media with piezoelectric, semiconductor, porous and hygro-thermal couplings[24–27].
Wave propagation in heat-conducting anisotropic media was also studied by Chadwick and
Seet[28]. Surface acoustic waves are particularly useful for examining such transport processes
because they sample a thin subsurface layer where heat transport and phason rearrangements
Phason transport and Lord–Shulman heat conduction effects on surface waves in 2D quasicrystals 3
occur. In an anisotropic half-space, a surface wave exists when the boundary matrix assembled
from decaying partial waves becomes singular[29]. In piezoelectric crystals, electromechanical
coupling can also guide a surface wave[30,31]. Such formulations avoid spatial discretisation of
the half-space and are well suited to parameter studies and verification against exact limits.
Mostsurfaceandguided-wavestudiesofquasicrystalsconcernone-dimensional(1D)hexago-
nalstructureswithasinglephasoncomponent. Rayleighwaveshavebeenstudiedingradedand
layered half-spaces[32–34]. Other studies have considered guided waves in layered and piezoelec-
tric plates and nanoplates[35–37], Love waves with interface cracks[38], anti-plane fracture and
scattering[39–41], and nonlocal plates[42]. Thermoelastic wave problems include a generalised
thermoelastic piezoelectric rod[43] and thermally coupled Lamb waves in a nanoplate with a
fractional-order Lord–Shulman model[44]. Surface and interface effects on shear-horizontal and
anti-plane waves in piezoelectric quasicrystals have also been investigated[45].
Research on 2D hexagonal quasicrystals has focused mainly on static and quasi-static prob-
lems, particularly those involving thermal effects. These include general thermoelastic solu-
tions[46], half-space fundamental solutions[47], thermoelastic fracture and cracks under thermal
loading[48,49], piezoelectric thermo-electric response[50], and piezoelectric electro-elastic crack
problems[51]. For the related decagonal class, meshless analysis under thermal shock has also
been reported[52]. In the dynamic setting, Wang and Schiavone[53] used the Stroh formalism to
show that quasicrystals can support up to three subsonic surface-wave speeds. Their analysis
did not include thermal coupling or dissipative phason dynamics. Results for 1D structures
therefore do not transfer directly to the 2D case. The latter has two phason components, addi-
tional independent constants[46], asymmetric phason stress, and two phason tractions at a free
surface.
To our knowledge, no surface-wave study of the 2D hexagonal class has combined this
constitutive structure with non-Fourier heat conduction or compared inertial, diffusive and
telegraph-typephasontransportwithinacommonformulation. Thisworkaddressesthesegaps
by examining the common surface-wave behaviour of the three transport laws, the differences
between the diffusive and telegraph descriptions across the frequency–friction plane, and the
effects of thermal relaxation and free or clamped phason surfaces. The in-plane problem is
formulatedforpropagationnormaltotheperiodicaxis,forwhichtheelectricpotentialdecouples
exactly. The piezoelectric response is retained in the constitutive structure but does not affect
thewavespeeds. Asinglesemi-analyticalsurface-matrixformulationisusedforallthreephason
laws. Theresultsareverifiedagainstclassical,adiabaticandpure-phasonlimits,theexactlimits
connecting the three transport laws, the complex-wavenumber problem, and an independent
spectral-collocation solution.

7 Conclusions
Surface waves in a two-dimensional hexagonal piezoelectric thermoelastic quasicrystal half-
space were analysed with Lord–Shulman heat conduction and three phason transport laws:
inertial, diffusive and telegraph-type. For propagation normal to the periodic axis, the electric
potential decouples exactly, reducing the problem to a five-field surface matrix without spatial
discretisation. The main findings are as follows.
• Thesemi-analyticalformulationrecoverstheclassicalRayleighratioandthepure-phason
surface-wave limit to about 10−11–10−10. The telegraph model approaches the inertial
and diffusive models in the corresponding limits, and independent spectral-collocation
calculations confirm the computed wave speeds.
• The phason transport law controls the surface-wave branches. For ρ = ρ, the iner-
w
tial model supports a phason-dominated wave with a speed about one third below the
Rayleigh-type wave and scaling as ρ−1/2. The diffusive and telegraph models support
w
onlytheRayleigh-typebranch,whiletheinertialRayleigh-typewaveisweaklyleakywith
a loss of 4.5×10−6 per wavelength.
• Whenρ ≲0.25ρ,thephason-dominatedbranchapproachestheRayleigh-typespeedand
w
lies within 0.6% of it. The long-wavelength offset of the Rayleigh-type speed from the
classical Rayleigh value is instead caused by the adiabatic thermoelastic response.
• At the adopted friction, models B and C remain nearly identical, with ∆ ⩽5.8×10−5
BC
for ρ∗ = 0.1–103 at the selected frequencies. Their difference is controlled mainly by
w
χ = Ω∗/Ω , grows as χ2 for χ ≲ 0.1, and approaches 5×10−3 in the inertia-dominated
c
regime.
• Heat conduction through the thermal boundary layer dominates Rayleigh-type damping
below Ω∗ ≈ 160, while phason friction dominates at higher frequency. Lord–Shulman
relaxation changes the phase speed by less than 7.9 × 10−5, and an insulated surface
substantially reduces low-frequency thermal damping.
• Phason clamping changes the Rayleigh-type speed by at most 8.1×10−6 but removes
the bounded phason-dominated branch of the inertial model. All computed branches
remain surface-localised, with the phason-dominated wave penetrating more deeply than
the Rayleigh-type waves.
The present half-space formulation provides a reference model for surface wave propaga-
tion in quasicrystalline materials and sufficiently thick coatings. The results can be useful for
analysing the effects of phason transport, thermal relaxation and surface boundary conditions
on wave propagation and attenuation. The formulation also provides a benchmark for numeri-
cal models of coupled phonon–phason–thermal wave problems, while the predicted trends can
guide the interpretation of surface-wave behaviour in quasicrystalline media.
