\section{Literature Landscape and Scholarly Context}
\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Foundational works}

The literature of generalized continuum mechanics for wave propagation rests on a small number of foundational works that defined entire theory families:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Biot (1956)} — Classical coupled thermoelasticity, combining Fourier heat conduction with linear elasticity. This established the framework for all subsequent generalized theories and identified the infinite propagation speed paradox.
\item \textbf{Cattaneo (1948) \cite{Cattaneo1948} and Vernotte (1958) \cite{Vernotte1958}} — Hyperbolic heat conduction with relaxation time, proposing finite thermal wave speed.
\item \textbf{Lord and Shulman (1967)} — Generalized thermoelasticity with a single thermal relaxation time, making the heat equation hyperbolic.
\item \textbf{Green and Lindsay (1972)} — Two-temperature thermoelasticity with two relaxation times, including dissipationless propagation for centrally symmetric bodies.
\item \textbf{Green and Naghdi (1991, 1993)} \cite{Green1991,Green1993} — Types II and III theories, introducing thermal displacement and dissipationless thermoelasticity.
\item \textbf{Tzou (1995, 1997)} — Dual-phase-lag and triple-phase-lag models for micro-scale heat transfer with two and three phase lags.
\item \textbf{Eringen (1972, 1974, 1983)} — Nonlocal elasticity where stress at a point depends on strain throughout a neighborhood, enabling scale-dependent modeling.
\item \textbf{Wang and Li (2011)} — Memory-dependent derivatives with integral-form fading memory, an alternative to fractional calculus.
\item \textbf{Povstenko (2011, 2015)} \cite{Povstenko2011} — Fractional thermoelasticity with time-fractional heat equations for anomalous diffusion.
\item \textbf{Chandrasekharaiah (1986, 1998)} \cite{Chandrasekharaiah1986,Chandrasekharaiah1998} — Review articles on hyperbolic thermoelasticity and second sound.
\item \textbf{Shakeriaski et al. (2021)} \cite{Shakeriaski2021} — Recent review of generalized thermoelasticity theory and modified models.
\end{itemize}

These works form the intellectual backbone of the field. Every generalized theory discussed in this review can be traced to one or more of these foundational references.

\subsection{Contemporary research developments}

Since the foundational period, the field has expanded along several directions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coupled multiphysics:} Piezothermoelasticity, magneto-thermo-elasticity, semiconductor photo-thermo-elasticity, and porous/double-porous media have extended the single-field thermoelastic framework to coupled multi-field problems.
\item \textbf{Memory and heredity:} Memory-dependent derivatives, fractional operators, and hereditary integrals have provided alternatives to classical relaxation-time models, particularly for polymers, biological tissues, and composites.
\item \textbf{Nonlocality and scale effects:} Nonlocal elasticity, strain-gradient theory, and two-phase local/nonlocal models capture size-dependent wave behavior at micro- and nanoscales.
\item \textbf{Interface phenomena:} Wave reflection, transmission, mode conversion, and energy partitioning at interfaces between dissimilar media have been studied for elastic, thermoelastic, piezothermoelastic, porous, and nonlocal media.
\item \textbf{Applications:} Smart structures, nondestructive evaluation, energy harvesting, geophysics, biomedical ultrasound, and nanodevices have emerged as major application domains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Existing reviews and the contribution of this article}

Several review articles exist on individual theory families or application domains:

\begin{itemize}
\item Reviews focused on \textbf{nonlocal elasticity} discuss scale-dependent constitutive relations and nanoscale applications.
\item Reviews on \textbf{fractional thermoelasticity} cover time-fractional heat equations and anomalous diffusion.
\item Reviews on \textbf{piezothermoelasticity} address coupled-field problems in smart materials.
\item Reviews on \textbf{memory-dependent derivatives} survey integral-form memory operators and their applications.
\end{itemize}

None of these reviews provides the comprehensive, theory-spanning synthesis offered here. This review's distinctive contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Breadth:} Coverage of all major theory families—classical, LS, GL, GN-II/III, DPL, TPL, MGT, MDD, fractional, nonlocal, piezothermoelastic, porous, micropolar, semiconductor, and magneto-thermo-elastic—in a single coherent framework.
\item \textbf{Mathematical depth:} First-principles derivations of balance laws, constitutive relations, wave equations, dispersion relations, interface conditions, and energy flux, with documented assumptions for each equation.
\item \textbf{Five-axis taxonomy:} An original classification scheme (physical, mathematical, wave, energy, validation dimensions) that enables systematic identification of research gaps and theory relationships.
\item \textbf{Constructive organization:} The review is organized around scientific developments and conceptual themes rather than individual papers, making it readable as a continuous scholarly narrative.
\item \textbf{Original figures and tables:} Eight original vector figures and eight synthesis tables illustrate theory evolution, wave types, interface geometry, energy flux channels, nonlocality, memory kernels, theory relationships, and the research taxonomy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Citation and verification principles}

Per the equation citation policy, equations are selected for detailed exposition based on:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verification status:} Equations derived from first principles in this manuscript, or drawn from verified primary sources with documented assumptions, are presented as canonical results.
\item \textbf{Validity domain:} Each equation is presented with its assumptions (constitutive, material symmetry, geometry, boundary conditions, thermal theory, frequency convention, normalization, approximation level).
\item \textbf{Open questions:} Equations that cannot be independently verified are identified as open research questions, not presented as definitive results.
\item \textbf{Minimality:} Fewer precise equations are preferred over hundreds of redundant ones (the equation verification register in Appendix A documents all equations and their status).
\end{enumerate}

Where material errors are conclusively established in the literature, they are recorded transparently. Where uncertainty exists, it is acknowledged as an open question. No error allegations are made against any publication, and model-specific differences are never presented as mathematical contradictions without sufficient evidence.
