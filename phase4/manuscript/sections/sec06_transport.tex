\section{Generalized Transport and Memory Effects}
\label{sec:transport}

This section presents generalized transport mechanisms and memory effects in continuum media: relaxation mechanisms, fractional operators, hereditary integrals, memory-dependent derivatives, and their implications for wave propagation.

\subsection{Relaxation mechanisms: physical origin and mathematical representation}
\label{sec:6.1}

Relaxation in continuum media arises from microscopic processes that delay the material response to an applied stimulus. The response at time $t$ depends not only on the current stimulus but on its history. Mathematically, relaxation is represented by three principal structures:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Differential relaxation:} Time derivatives of fluxes appear in the constitutive relation (LS, DPL, TPL, MGT). The heat flux $q_i$ satisfies a differential equation involving $\dot{q}_i$ or higher derivatives.
\item \textbf{Integral relaxation:} The response is a convolution of the stimulus with a memory kernel (MDD, fractional). The flux at time $t$ depends on the history of the temperature gradient over a finite or semi-infinite interval.
\item \textbf{Spatial relaxation:} Nonlocal integral relations where stress at a point depends on strain throughout a neighborhood (Eringen \cite{Eringen1972} nonlocal elasticity).
\end{itemize}

The common feature is that the material response has memory: past states influence present behavior. This memory produces finite wave speeds, dispersion, attenuation, and history-dependent dissipation.

\subsection{Fractional operators: Caputo and Riemann--Liouville}
\label{sec:6.2}

Fractional derivatives generalize integer-order differentiation to non-integer orders, providing a natural mathematical language for memory and hereditary effects.

\subsubsection{Caputo fractional derivative}

The Caputo derivative of order $\alpha$ ($n-1 < \alpha < n$, $n = \lceil\alpha\rceil$):

\begin{equation}
{}^C D_t^\alpha f(t) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\int_0^t \frac{f^{(n)}(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1-n}}\,d\tau.
\label{eq:6.1}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Riemann--Liouville fractional derivative}

\begin{equation}
{}^{RL} D_t^\alpha f(t) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\frac{d^n}{dt^n}\int_0^t \frac{f(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1-n}}\,d\tau.
\label{eq:6.2}
\end{equation}

The Caputo derivative is generally preferred in physical applications because it allows classical initial conditions (integer-order derivatives at $t=0$) to be prescribed.

\subsubsection{Fractional heat conduction}

The time-fractional heat conduction equation:

\begin{equation}
{}^C D_t^\alpha T = a\nabla^2 T, \qquad 0 < \alpha \leq 2,
\label{eq:6.3}
\end{equation}

where $a$ is the thermal diffusivity. For $1 < \alpha \leq 2$, this equation describes wave-like heat propagation with memory, interpolating between diffusive behavior ($\alpha = 1$, classical Fourier) and wave-like behavior ($\alpha = 2$, classical wave equation).

\subsection{Hereditary integrals: convolution kernels}
\label{sec:6.3}

Hereditary integrals represent the material response as a superposition of responses to past stimuli, weighted by a memory kernel:

\begin{equation}
f(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} K(t-\xi) g(\xi)\,d\xi,
\label{eq:6.4}
\end{equation}

where $K(t-\xi)$ is the memory kernel satisfying the fading memory condition $K(s) \to 0$ as $s \to \infty$. The kernel weights recent stimuli more heavily than distant ones.

\subsection{Memory-dependent derivative (MDD): operator and kernel}
\label{sec:6.4}

The MDD operator (Wang \& Li, 2011):

\begin{equation}
D_\omega f(t) = \frac{1}{\omega}\int_{t-\omega}^{t} \kappa(t-\xi) f'(\xi)\,d\xi,
\label{eq:6.5}
\end{equation}

where $\omega > 0$ is the time delay (memory horizon) and $\kappa(t-\xi)$ is the kernel function with $0 \leq \kappa(t-\xi) \leq 1$.

\subsubsection{MDD kernel function}

A commonly used kernel:

\begin{equation}
\kappa(t-\xi) = 1 - \frac{2\beta}{\omega}(t-\xi) + \frac{\alpha^2}{\omega^2}(t-\xi)^2,
\label{eq:6.6}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are kernel parameters controlling the weighting shape.

\subsubsection{Limiting behavior}

As $\omega \to 0$, the MDD approaches the classical first derivative:

\begin{equation}
\lim_{\omega \to 0} D_\omega f(t) = f'(t).
\label{eq:6.7}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy flux in memory-dependent media}
\label{sec:6.5}

For memory-dependent media, the energy flux acquires a hereditary character through the heat flux:

\begin{equation}
J_i(t) = \sigma_{ji}(t)v_i(t) + q_i(t),
\label{eq:6.8}
\end{equation}

where $q_i(t)$ satisfies the MDD heat conduction law:

\begin{equation}
q_i(t) + \tau_0 D_\omega q_i(t) = -k T_{,i}(t).
\label{eq:6.9}
\end{equation}

Substituting the MDD definition:

\begin{equation}
q_i(t) + \frac{\tau_0}{\omega}\int_{t-\omega}^{t} \kappa(t-\xi) \dot{q}_i(\xi)\,d\xi = -k T_{,i}(t).
\label{eq:6.10}
\end{equation}

The complete time-domain expression for $J_i$ solely in terms of $T$ requires solving this convolution equation, which depends on the kernel choice. The frequency-domain form can be derived for specific kernels, but the general time-domain hereditary flux remains an open formulation.

\subsection{Well-posedness and causality for memory-dependent models}
\label{sec:6.6}

\subsubsection{Well-posedness conditions}

For MDD models, well-posedness requires:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The kernel $\kappa$ must be positive definite,
\item The time delay $\omega$ must be finite and positive,
\item The relaxation parameter $\tau_0$ must be non-negative.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Causality (finite wave speed)}

All memory-dependent models predict finite thermal wave speeds, addressing the infinite propagation speed paradox of classical Fourier theory. The thermal wave speed depends on the kernel parameters and relaxation time.
