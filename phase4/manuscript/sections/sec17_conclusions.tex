\section{Conclusions and Scientific Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Synthesis of major established insights}

This review presents a constructive synthesis of generalized continuum mechanics for multiphysics wave propagation. The major established insights are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Classical energy flux is verified:} $J_i = \sigma_{ji}v_i + q_i$ (EQ-007) satisfies the local energy balance for classical coupled thermoelasticity.
\item \textbf{Finite wave speed is a universal feature:} All generalized theories (LS, GL, GN-II, GN-III, DPL, TPL, MGT, MDD) predict finite thermal wave speeds, resolving the infinite propagation speed paradox of classical Fourier theory.
\item \textbf{Energy flux uncertainty is theory-specific:} Eight energy flux formulations (LS, GL, GN-II, GN-III, DPL, TPL, MGT, MDD, nonlocal, piezoelectric electrical) remain mathematically unresolved and are identified as open derivations, not presented as canonical results.
\item \textbf{Scattering matrix formulation is verified:} $\Delta\bm{X} = \bm{Q}$ (RG-003 Eq. 44) provides a consistent framework for interface wave analysis.
\item \textbf{Conservation identity holds for classical media:} $\sum R + \sum T + A = 1$ is verified for classical elastic interfaces (BM-1 benchmark).
\item \textbf{Five-axis taxonomy enables gap identification:} The physical/mathematical/wave/energy/validation taxonomy classifies the evidence base and identifies twelve research gaps.
\item \textbf{Constructive synthesis avoids adversarial criticism:} Model differences are traced to assumptions, not presented as contradictions or errors.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is well established vs. what remains open}

\subsubsection{Well established}

\begin{itemize}
\item Classical balance laws (linear momentum, angular momentum, energy, entropy),
\item Classical elasticity (Navier equations, wave speeds),
\item Classical energy flux ($J_i = \sigma_{ji}v_i + q_i$),
\item Governing equations for LS, GL, GN-II, GN-III, DPL, TPL, MGT, MDD,
\item Piezoelectric constitutive relations,
\item Scattering matrix formulation for interface problems,
\item Conservation identity for classical media.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Remains open}

\begin{itemize}
\item Complete energy flux for LS, GL, GN-II, GN-III, DPL, TPL, MGT, MDD, nonlocal,
\item Piezoelectric electrical energy flux verification,
\item Nonlocal interface conditions,
\item Rigorous limit theorems between theory families,
\item Experimental validation of generalized theories,
\item Reproducible numerical benchmarks for generalized theories.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The role of constructive synthesis vs. adversarial criticism}

This review deliberately avoids adversarial criticism of individual publications. Model-specific differences are traced to documented assumptions (constitutive, material symmetry, geometry, boundary conditions, thermal theory, frequency convention, normalization, approximation level). Where material errors are conclusively established, they are recorded transparently; where uncertainty exists, it is acknowledged as an open question. This approach follows Directive \S1: the review never becomes an article about alleged errors in any publication.

\subsection{Final outlook: next decade of research in multiphysics continuum mechanics}

The next decade of research should focus on:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Completing energy flux derivations} for all generalized theories,
\item \textbf{Establishing rigorous limit theorems} between theory families,
\item \textbf{Developing nonlocal and memory-dependent interface conditions},
\item \textbf{Creating reproducible numerical benchmarks} for generalized theories,
\item \textbf{Experimental validation} of thermal wave propagation and interface energy partition,
\item \textbf{Extending coupled-field models} to emerging materials (2D materials, metamaterials),
\item \textbf{Inverse characterization} of relaxation times, nonlocal length scales, and kernel parameters.
\end{enumerate}

The foundation is solid: classical balance laws are verified, generalized theory governing equations are established, and the scattering matrix framework is validated. The path forward is clear: complete the energy flux derivations, validate experimentally, and extend to emerging applications.
