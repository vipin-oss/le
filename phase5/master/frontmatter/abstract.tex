This review examines how extensions of classical continuum mechanics change thermomechanical wave propagation, energy transport and interface phenomena. It organizes generalized thermoelastic, multiphysics, memory-dependent, fractional, gradient and nonlocal models by constitutive mechanism, governing structure, predicted modes and validation. For the exact classical Eulerian balance, the outward-positive total-energy flux is $F_i=q_i-\sigma_{ij}v_j+\mathcal{E}v_i$; the reduced $J_i=q_i-\sigma_{ij}v_j$ is used only for the stated first-order small-amplitude wave balance. Lord--Shulman and Green--Lindsay are distinct from two-temperature thermoelasticity: the former relaxes heat flux, whereas the latter introduces temperature-rate dependence in a single-temperature formulation. The standard Eringen-type nonlocal dispersion relation considered here has bounded frequency at high wavenumber, and quasi-electrostatic piezoelectric models do not contain a propagating electromagnetic branch. The review compares model-specific assumptions and identifies questions for further analytical, numerical and experimental study.
