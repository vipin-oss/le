This review examines how extensions of classical continuum mechanics change thermomechanical wave propagation, energy transport and interface phenomena. It organizes generalized thermoelastic, multiphysics, memory-dependent, fractional, gradient and nonlocal models by constitutive mechanism, governing structure, predicted modes and validation. With total energy per unit volume and outward-positive flux, the classical mechanical contribution is minus stress power, giving $J_i=q_i-\sigma_{ij}v_j$. Lord--Shulman and Green--Lindsay are distinct from two-temperature thermoelasticity: the former relaxes heat flux, whereas the latter introduces temperature-rate dependence in a single-temperature formulation. The standard Eringen-type nonlocal dispersion relation considered here has bounded frequency at high wavenumber, and quasi-electrostatic piezoelectric models do not contain a propagating electromagnetic branch. The review compares model-specific assumptions and identifies questions for further analytical, numerical and experimental study.
