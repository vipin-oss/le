\section{Reflection, Transmission, and Interface Phenomena}
\label{sec:interfaces}

This section presents wave reflection, transmission, and energy partitioning at interfaces between different continuum media. The scattering matrix formulation, interface conditions, and energy ratios are developed for elastic, thermoelastic, and piezothermoelastic media.

\subsection{Interface geometry}
\label{sec:9.1}

Consider two half-spaces in perfect contact at the interface plane $x_3 = 0$. Medium 1 occupies $x_3 < 0$ and medium 2 occupies $x_3 > 0$. A plane harmonic wave is incident from medium 1 onto the interface, generating reflected waves in medium 1 and transmitted waves in medium 2.

The interface normal is $\bm{n} = (0, 0, 1)$. All field variables vary harmonically in time with convention $e^{-i\omega t}$ and propagate along the interface with wave number $k$ in the $x_1$-direction:

\begin{equation}
\text{field}(x_1, x_3, t) = \hat{\text{field}}(x_3) e^{i(kx_1 - \omega t)}.
\label{eq:9.1}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Wave decomposition: incident, reflected, transmitted modes}
\label{sec:9.2}

In each medium, a finite-mode solution is assembled from partial waves selected for the model and geometry. In the sagittal plane of an isotropic elastic medium, the in-plane polarizations are P and SV; an out-of-plane SH polarization is a separate possibility. Thermoelastic and piezothermoelastic formulations may couple additional thermal and electrical field variables. The admissible modes and their labels depend on the constitutive equations and electric-field closure.

The displacement field in medium $m$ ($m = 1, 2$) is decomposed as:

\begin{equation}
u_i^{(m)} = \sum_n A_n^{(m)} \hat{u}_i^{(m,n)} e^{i(kx_1+q_n^{(m)}x_3-\omega t)},
\label{eq:9.2}
\end{equation}

where $A_n^{(m)}$ are complex amplitude coefficients, $q_n^{(m)}$ are the vertical wave numbers, and $\hat{u}_i^{(m,n)}$ are the polarization vectors.

The incident wave comes from medium 1 with known amplitude. Reflected waves propagate back into medium 1, and transmitted waves propagate into medium 2.

\subsection{Interface conditions}
\label{sec:9.3}

For an ideal, perfectly bonded interface with no surface force, the mechanical conditions are displacement and traction continuity. Thermal and electrical conditions below likewise assume the stated perfect-contact and no-surface-source idealizations.

\subsubsection{Mechanical conditions}

\begin{equation}
u_i^{(1)} = u_i^{(2)}, \qquad \sigma_{i3}^{(1)} = \sigma_{i3}^{(2)} \quad \text{at } x_3 = 0.
\label{eq:9.3}
\end{equation}

Yang (2018) p.~38 Eq.~(1.221) prints continuity of displacement and of the traction vector in spatial form; Eq.~(1.225) on p.~39 is the material-form counterpart; the manuscript restricts to the $x_3$ tractions, which is correct only for the flat interface at $x_3=0$ with no in-plane variation of the interface \cite{Yang2018}.

\subsubsection{Thermal conditions}

For thermoelastic media:

\begin{equation}
T^{(1)} = T^{(2)}, \qquad q_3^{(1)} = q_3^{(2)} \quad \text{at } x_3 = 0.
\label{eq:9.4}
\end{equation}

These conditions represent perfect thermal contact with no interfacial heat source or thermal resistance; other interface models require different conditions.

\subsubsection{Electrical conditions (piezoelectric media)}

For piezothermoelastic media:

\begin{equation}
\phi^{(1)} = \phi^{(2)}, \qquad D_3^{(1)} = D_3^{(2)} \quad \text{at } x_3 = 0.
\label{eq:9.5}
\end{equation}

The display is the zero-charge specialization of the general electrical jump Eq.~(1.222) printed on p.~38; Eq.~(1.230) on p.~39 is the plate result D_3=sigma_e for a charged plate \cite{Yang2018}.

For the stated idealization, potential is continuous and normal electric displacement is continuous when no free surface charge is present; a nonzero free surface charge changes the displacement jump. At an insulated interface, the normal displacement condition is instead $D_3=0$.

\subsubsection{Imperfect bonding}

One linear spring-interface model relates traction to the displacement jump:

\begin{equation}
t_i = K_{ij}^u\bigl(u_j^{(2)} - u_j^{(1)}\bigr), \qquad t_i = \sigma_{ij}^{(1)}n_j = \sigma_{ij}^{(2)}n_j \quad \text{at } x_3=0,
\label{eq:9.6}
\end{equation}

where $K_{ij}^u$ is an interfacial stiffness tensor and $\bm{n}$ is the common interface normal. Under this particular law, the large-stiffness limit enforces displacement continuity, while vanishing stiffness gives zero traction; other imperfect-interface models may use different constitutive relations.

\subsection{Scattering matrix formulation}
\label{sec:9.4}

The interface conditions lead to a linear system relating the unknown amplitude coefficients to the known incident wave amplitude. For a thermo-piezoelastic interface problem with $N$ unknown amplitudes and $N$ interface conditions, the system can be written in matrix form:

\begin{equation}
\Delta \bm{X} = \bm{Q},
\label{eq:9.7}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta$ is the $N\times N$ system matrix whose entries depend on material properties, wave numbers, frequency, and incidence angle,
\item $\bm{X} = (A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_N)^T$ is the vector of unknown amplitude coefficients (reflected and transmitted),
\item $\bm{Q} = (Q_1, Q_2, \ldots, Q_N)^T$ is the known vector determined by the incident wave amplitude.
\end{itemize}

The entries of $\Delta$ are determined by substituting the partial wave expansions into the interface conditions \eqref{eq:9.3}--\eqref{eq:9.5}. Each row corresponds to one interface condition; each column corresponds to one unknown amplitude.

The solution is obtained by matrix inversion:

\begin{equation}
\bm{X} = \Delta^{-1}\bm{Q}.
\label{eq:9.8}
\end{equation}

For this square linear system, the condition $\det(\Delta) \neq 0$ is the condition under which the solution is unique. This is a linear-algebra statement, not a physical well-posedness result.

\subsection{Secular equation and mode classification}
\label{sec:9.5}

For a selected finite-field model, substituting the modal ansatz into the governing equations gives a homogeneous algebraic system $\bm{M}(q,\omega,k)\hat{\bm{a}}=\bm{0}$. Non-trivial amplitudes require the secular condition

\begin{equation}
\det \bm{M}(q,\omega,k)=0,
\label{eq:9.9}
\end{equation}

where $\bm{M}$ is assembled from that model's constitutive equations, orientation, and electric-field closure. Its form, polynomial order when polynomial, and admissible mode count are model-specific; no universal degree or mode set is asserted. The roots are classified as:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Propagating modes:} real $q$ --- waves that propagate without spatial attenuation,
\item \textbf{Evanescent modes:} purely imaginary $q$ --- waves that decay exponentially with distance,
\item \textbf{Attenuating modes:} complex $q$ --- waves that both propagate and attenuate.
\end{itemize}

Only outgoing modes (satisfying the radiation condition) are retained in the physical solution.

\subsection{Energy ratios at interfaces}
\label{sec:9.7}

The interface-plane ratios $R_{\Sigma}$ and $T_{\Sigma}$ and the interface-local residual $A_{\mathrm{int}}$ are defined from total-field normal fluxes as:

\begin{equation}
R_{\Sigma} = \frac{J_{\mathrm{inc},n}-J_{1,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}}{J_{\mathrm{inc},n}},
\qquad
T_{\Sigma} = \frac{J_{2,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}}{J_{\mathrm{inc},n}},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{int}} = \frac{J_{1,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}-J_{2,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}}{J_{\mathrm{inc},n}},
\label{eq:9.10}
\end{equation}

Here $J_{\mathrm{inc},n}\equiv P_{i,\Sigma}>0$ is the net, time-averaged normal power flux carried by the incident field alone, evaluated at the interface reference plane used for this normalization; it is not the incident-plus-reflected total-field flux $J_{1,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}$. The latter is the complete incident-side flux, and $J_{2,n}^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the complete transmitted-side flux at that same plane. All fluxes use one common normal and the total-field fluxes include cross terms. With no interfacial storage or other surface power, $A_{\mathrm{int}}=\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}/P_{i,\Sigma}$ for this same-plane normalization. This does not identify $P_{i,\Sigma}$ with the remote-plane incident power $P_i$ when bulk attenuation separates the planes; see Section~\ref{sec:10.5}. $R_{\Sigma}$ and $T_{\Sigma}$ are interface-plane bookkeeping ratios, not remote-plane reflectance and transmittance, and $A_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the interface-local residual, not bulk attenuation.

For a passive interface with no interfacial storage or dissipation ($A_{\mathrm{int}}=0$), total-field accounting gives:

\begin{equation}
\sum R_{\Sigma} + \sum T_{\Sigma} = 1.
\label{eq:9.11}
\end{equation}

With interfacial dissipation or storage represented by a nonzero local residual:

\begin{equation}
\sum R_{\Sigma} + \sum T_{\Sigma} + A_{\mathrm{int}} = 1.
\label{eq:9.12}
\end{equation}

The identity \eqref{eq:9.12} is the interface balance when the theory-specific flux is evaluated from total fields on both sides and all modes and cross terms are retained. It does not add bulk dissipation integrated over either half-space as a third interface channel.

For passive perfect contact with no interfacial storage or dissipation, continuity of total-field flux gives zero interface-local residual. Mode-diagonal outgoing fluxes are not interchangeable with the total-field flux unless the relevant cross terms have been shown to vanish under the selected model and reference-plane convention. Bulk dissipation is a volume integral and must be reported separately; adding it to an interface residual would double-count energy loss.

The one-dimensional, lossless longitudinal benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:10.5} derives the incident--reflected cross-term cancellation for that specified configuration. No coupled-system numerical example is used here to support a general flux-ratio identity.

\subsubsection{What the interface partition reveals}

The interface-plane ratios defined above are model-dependent observables. Their interpretation requires the stated constitutive flux and boundary conditions; the closure identity alone does not distinguish two theories that predict the same total-field flux balance. Comparisons of reflected and transmitted fractions require a common normalization and source-specific modal conventions.



\subsubsection{The pattern and its limits}

No universal claim that the conservation identity has been verified across generalized-media papers is made here. For the present total-field definitions, the local interface balance follows from flux continuity only when the specified interface has no storage or dissipation; mode-projected literature ratios require source-specific reconstruction and independent checking. The configurations discussed here idealize flat interfaces between homogeneous half-spaces. Whether, and at what rate, a sharp-interface solution approximates a graded transition in a selected nonlocal, memory-dependent, or gradient model is a model-specific open question; the identities above do not resolve it.

\subsection{Interface conditions under memory and nonlocality}
\label{sec:9.8}

The local interface conditions of Section~\ref{sec:9.3} cannot be transferred automatically to every nonlocal or history-dependent constitutive law; the appropriate matching variables and data depend on the selected model.

For a model whose constitutive flux is defined by a history integral, equality of instantaneous flux values alone may not specify the interface law or its history data. Any matching condition should be derived for the selected memory model and initial-history assumptions.

For an integral nonlocal model whose kernel crosses the interface, fields on both sides may enter the constitutive response near it. The interface law and any local approximation must be derived for the selected kernel, boundary treatment, and length scales; no universal order estimate is asserted here.

These issues motivate open questions about how constitutive variables and history data are assigned across material discontinuities. The appropriate formulation remains model-specific.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{OQ9.\arabic*}]
\item What are the interface conditions for nonlocal media where stress depends on strain in a neighborhood? \\ \label{oq:9.1}
\item How are energy ratios computed for memory-dependent media with hereditary flux? \\ \label{oq:9.2}
\item What is the correct interface energy balance for piezothermoelastic media with electrical coupling? \\ \label{oq:9.3}
\item Is the sharp-interface result the limit of a graded-interface result for nonlocal, gradient and memory-dependent media, and at what rate does the difference vanish as the interfacial layer is thickened? \\ \label{oq:9.4}
\item Can the constitutive variables of a nonlocal or memory-dependent theory be continued across a material discontinuity at all, and what does that imply for the meaning of an interface in such a theory? \\ \label{oq:9.5}
\end{enumerate}
