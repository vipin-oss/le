## Green & Lindsay (1972), linear thermoelasticity

**Status:** targeted page-image review completed for the GL equation-level gap in OCL-481. This is limited to the paper's own homogeneous linear theory; no broad GL-family or later-variant survey was made.

### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Green and Lindsay, “Thermoelasticity,” *Journal of Elasticity* 2 (1972), pp. 1–7. The p. 1 abstract had previously been text-extracted for OCL-474. For the equation check, only printed pp. 4–6 were rendered and reviewed visually: the homogeneous-body constitutive development and symmetry result in Eqs. (3.12)–(3.19) on p. 4; the small-field linearization, constitutive/heat-flux equations, coefficient restrictions, and linearized field equation in Eqs. (4.1)–(4.13) on p. 5; and the additional positivity conditions and uniqueness identity in Eqs. (4.14)–(4.17) on p. 6.

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-496

The paper's selected linear theory assumes small temperature departure from a constant basic temperature, small displacement, and small time/spatial derivatives; the initial body is taken stress-free with zero heat flux. For a homogeneous, possibly anisotropic body, the authors give a linearized stress, heat-flux, entropy, and field-equation system (Eqs. (4.5)–(4.13)) and impose coefficient restrictions from the entropy inequality (Eqs. (4.8)–(4.10), (4.14)). In a centrosymmetric body the vector coefficient multiplying the temperature rate vanishes; this is a special case, not a universal closure.

The authors state uniqueness for the homogeneous initial mixed-boundary-value problem under suitable conditions, including the entropy restriction and additional positive-coefficient conditions (printed p. 6, Eq. (4.16)); Eq. (4.17) supplies the identity used in the proof for the homogeneous equations. This is a source-specific uniqueness result, not an existence or continuous-dependence proof. The source's coefficient notation has not been mapped to the manuscript's deleted `\tau_1 \ge \tau_0` claim or to later modified GL laws; OCL-143–OCL-149 and OCL-481 remain unresolved for those broader claims.

### Limitations and remaining open work

The page images make the selected equations and stated sufficient conditions legible, but the article's proof details are expressly omitted; no full proof was independently reconstructed. No source-specific wave-speed/stability classification, parameter mapping to later relaxation-time notation, or family-wide GL template is inferred. The existing table should use only the bounded model-level facts above and retain those broader questions as B/open.

## Green & Naghdi (1993), type-II thermoelastic model

**Status:** targeted page-image review completed for the equation/theorem gap in OCL-491. Printed pp. 200–204 were rendered and visually checked; this is not a whole-paper transcription. No Drive identifiers, links, or source-folder names are recorded here.

### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Green and Naghdi, “Thermoelasticity without energy dissipation,” *Journal of Elasticity* 31 (1993), pp. 189–208. Only the relevant displayed passages on printed pp. 200–204 were consulted as images: the linearized constitutive development and Eqs. (3.12)–(3.17) on pp. 201–202, followed by the mixed initial-boundary-value uniqueness result and Eqs. (4.1)–(4.9) on pp. 203–204.

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-493

The selected type-II equations are for an isotropic linearization about an equilibrium state. The paper assumes small temperature departure, thermal displacement, relative displacement, and their time/space derivatives; the free-energy density is taken quadratic in temperature, thermal-displacement gradient, and infinitesimal strain (Eq. 3.12). The resulting constitutive relations and coupled field equations are model-specific (Eqs. 3.13–3.16), not a family-wide GN-II template.

For the displayed coupled system, the authors state that, with body force and heat supply absent and the printed restrictions $c>0$, $\kappa^*>0$, and $\lambda+2\mu\ge 0$ in Eq. (3.17), harmonic waves propagate without damping (printed p. 202). The image check confirms these coefficients and signs in this source's notation. This is a source-specific wave result under those assumptions; it is not a general stability or admissibility theorem for every GN-II formulation.

The uniqueness theorem is for the linear type-II problem and its stated mixed boundary data: displacement or traction on complementary boundary portions, and temperature or entropy flux on complementary portions, together with the specified initial data (Eqs. 4.2–4.4). It requires positive reference density and temperature, the specific-heat condition referenced from Eq. (3.17), and the stated solution regularity; it concludes at most one solution (printed pp. 203–204). Uniqueness alone does not establish existence or full well-posedness.

### Limitations and remaining open work

The page images resolve the displayed coefficients and signs on pp. 200–204, but this check did not reconstruct the full uniqueness proof or map every source term to the manuscript's displayed candidate. The paper states the theorem's result but omits proof details. It does not derive the selected system's complete boundary power or interface flux in this audit. OCL-491 therefore remains Class B for exact equation-to-manuscript reconciliation and model-specific energy/interface derivations; the bounded wave/uniqueness statements are recorded in OCL-493 (Class A). Do not infer existence, generic well-posedness, universal GN-II dissipation, or a cross-family constitutive identity from these pages.

## Green & Naghdi (1992), type-III thermoelastic model

**Status:** targeted page-image review completed for the type-III equations and conductivity-regime split in OCL-492. Printed pp. 259–262 were rendered and reviewed; the separate introductory distinction on p. 255 remains based on the earlier targeted text extraction. No whole-paper transcription was prepared.

### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Green and Naghdi, “On Undamped Heat Waves in an Elastic Solid,” *Journal of Thermal Stresses* 15(2) (1992), pp. 253–264. The first-page abstract describes two distinct one-dimensional conductivity regimes. The introduction and footnote on printed p. 255 distinguish type-II nondissipation from type-III finite-speed thermal-wave behavior (text extraction). Page images were reviewed for printed pp. 259–262: Eqs. (3.14)–(3.20) give the linearized model and parameter restrictions; the one-dimensional specialization is in Eqs. (4.1)–(4.10), with the conductivity-regime split stated on p. 262.

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-494

The selected type-III development is a linearized isotropic thermoelastic model about a zero-stress equilibrium. Temperature departure and displacement, together with their time and spatial derivatives, are treated as small; the free energy is selected quadratic in temperature, infinitesimal strain, and thermal-displacement gradient (printed p. 260, Eqs. (3.11)–(3.16)). The authors identify the resulting displacement/temperature pair as a coupled PDE system (Eqs. (3.19)–(3.20)) and describe finite-speed thermal waves for this linearized type-III formulation.

The one-dimensional reduction is parameter-regime-specific. Page images of Eqs. (4.3)–(4.10) confirm the paper's dimensionless comparison: for $R_2\gg R_1$, Fourier conductivity dominates and Eq. (4.10) reduces to the classical Fourier heat equation, which does not support undamped thermal waves; for $R_2\ll R_1$, the selected small-conductivity case admits undamped thermal-wave solutions. This comparison does not make the full type-III family nondissipative, and the paper's p. 255 footnote explicitly reserves the type-II no-dissipation distinction.

### Limitations and remaining open work

The page images resolve the selected linearized coefficients, Eq. (3.18) restrictions, and the printed $R_2/R_1$ regime split; no separate energy/free-energy/dissipation derivation was made. The p. 255 introductory distinction was not image-checked. OCL-492 remains Class B for mapping the exact source equations to any broader comparison, and for energy/admissibility and boundary/interface flux. OCL-494 (Class A) records only the bounded source result; no type-II property is transferred, and no family-wide type-III stability or well-posedness claim is inferred.

### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Lord and Shulman, “A Generalized Dynamical Theory of Thermoelasticity,” *Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids* 15 (1967), pp. 299–309. The source's isotropic heat-flux reduction is printed p. 300, Eq. (8); its linearized isotropic constitutive and coupled field equations are printed p. 302, Eqs. (22)–(24).

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-495

The source starts from a general linear relation between heat flux and temperature gradient that includes heat-flux acceleration (Eq. 7); for an isotropic elastic solid it reduces this to the one-relaxation-time heat-flux law (Eq. 8), with the parameter described as the relaxation time for establishing steady conduction after a gradient is imposed. The later linearized isotropic system (Eqs. 22–24) is derived for the paper's small-strain/small-temperature setting and constant-specific-heat approximation. This verifies the source-specific equation convention used in the matrix; it does not verify every later LS variant or prove a universal parameter bound, stability theorem, or well-posedness result.

### Limitations and remaining open work

No positivity or admissible-range condition for the relaxation parameter is asserted by this bounded extraction. The displayed coupled equations do not by themselves establish existence, uniqueness, stability, or an energy-flux identity under arbitrary initial and boundary data. Page images were not reviewed, and no exact tensor-coefficient transcription is claimed. The source equation anchor is recorded in OCL-495; OCL-011/OCL-483 and the LS open-question cell remain for parameter/admissibility and model-wide energy/interface analysis.
