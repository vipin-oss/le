### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Green and Naghdi, “Thermoelasticity without energy dissipation,” *Journal of Elasticity* 31 (1993), pp. 189–208. Only the relevant displayed passages on printed pp. 199–204 were consulted: the linearized constitutive development and Eqs. (3.12)–(3.17) on pp. 200–202, followed by the mixed initial-boundary-value uniqueness result and Eqs. (4.1)–(4.5) on pp. 203–204.

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-493

The selected type-II equations are for an isotropic linearization about an equilibrium state. The paper assumes small temperature departure, thermal displacement, relative displacement, and their time/space derivatives; the free-energy density is taken quadratic in temperature, thermal-displacement gradient, and infinitesimal strain (Eq. 3.12). The resulting constitutive relations and coupled field equations are model-specific (Eqs. 3.13–3.16), not a family-wide GN-II template.

For the displayed coupled system, the authors state that, with body force and heat supply absent and the coefficient restrictions in Eq. (3.17), harmonic waves propagate without damping (printed p. 202). This is a source-specific wave result under those assumptions; it is not a general stability or admissibility theorem for every GN-II formulation.

The uniqueness theorem is for the linear type-II problem and its stated mixed boundary data: displacement or traction on complementary boundary portions, and temperature or entropy flux on complementary portions, together with the specified initial data (Eqs. 4.2–4.4). It requires positive reference density and temperature, the specific-heat condition referenced from Eq. (3.17), and the stated solution regularity; it concludes at most one solution (printed pp. 203–204). Uniqueness alone does not establish existence or full well-posedness.

## Green & Naghdi (1992), type-III thermoelastic model

**Status:** targeted primary-text extraction completed for the equation/scope gap in OCL-492. Only the passages that identify and bound the selected type-III formulation were consulted; no page-image audit was made and no whole-paper transcription was prepared.

### Scope and anchors

The checked article is Green and Naghdi, “On Undamped Heat Waves in an Elastic Solid,” *Journal of Thermal Stresses* 15(2) (1992), pp. 253–264. The first-page abstract describes two distinct one-dimensional conductivity regimes. The introduction and footnote on printed p. 255 distinguish type-II nondissipation from type-III finite-speed thermal-wave behavior. The type-III discussion identifies Eqs. (3.19)–(3.20) as its coupled linearized field system (printed pp. 259–261); the one-dimensional specialization is developed in Eqs. (4.1)–(4.10) on the following pages.

### Source-bounded finding recorded in OCL-494

The selected type-III development is a linearized isotropic thermoelastic model about a zero-stress equilibrium. Temperature departure and displacement, together with their time and spatial derivatives, are treated as small; the free energy is selected quadratic in temperature, infinitesimal strain, and thermal-displacement gradient (printed p. 260, Eqs. (3.11)–(3.16)). The authors identify the resulting displacement/temperature pair as a coupled PDE system (Eqs. (3.19)–(3.20)) and describe finite-speed thermal waves for this linearized type-III formulation.

The one-dimensional reduction is parameter-regime-specific. The authors state that a Fourier-conductivity-dominant case returns the classical Fourier heat equation, which does not support undamped thermal waves; in the negligible-conductivity case, the reduced equation admits undamped thermal-wave solutions. This comparison does not make the full type-III family nondissipative, and the paper's p. 255 footnote explicitly reserves the type-II no-dissipation distinction. The exact dimensionless threshold symbols in the extracted Eqs. (4.3)–(4.10) are not transcribed because OCR is unreliable.

### Limitations and remaining open work

The extraction supports source identity, the type-III system designation, the isotropic linearization assumptions, and the stated qualitative one-dimensional regimes. It does not certify exact coefficient signs, threshold normalization, or a complete energy/free-energy/dissipation identity; Eqs. (3.19)–(3.20) are OCR-garbled, and no source-page image was reviewed.
