## Title

Multiphysics Wave Mechanics in Generalized Continua

## Abstract (draft, 230 words or fewer)

This review examines how extensions of classical continuum mechanics change thermomechanical wave propagation, energy transport, and interface phenomena. It organizes generalized thermoelastic, multiphysics, memory-dependent, fractional, gradient, and nonlocal models by constitutive mechanism, governing structure, predicted modes, and validation. For the classical Eulerian balance, the outward total-energy flux is stated explicitly, and the reduced wave flux is used only for a stated first-order small-amplitude balance. Lord–Shulman and Green–Lindsay are single-temperature formulations, distinct from two-temperature thermoelasticity. For the displayed nonlocal dispersion relation, the frequency saturates at high wavenumber, and quasi-electrostatic piezoelectric potential models carry no propagating electromagnetic branch. Symbolic checks support selected reductions, interface energy partitions, and limiting behaviour; they do not establish general stability, finite-speed propagation, or dissipation for any family. Several primary sources remain unverified, and each such limitation is stated where it applies. The review identifies questions for analytical, numerical, and experimental study.
