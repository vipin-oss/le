   Drive-wide query was issued.
2. Text read through the Drive connector's PDF text layer, in slices of ≤17 000 characters. Above
   roughly 20 000 characters the connector returns the head and tail of the document with the middle
   elided, so each paper was read from the beginning in increasing slice sizes and the point at which
   the window closed was recorded.
3. **No page-render/OCR fallback exists in this environment** (no PDF rasteriser, no image channel to
   a Drive page). Where the text window closed before the governing equations, the row is tagged
   PARTIAL with the blocker named rather than filled from inference. One paper (Megahid 2023) is in

4. Section mapping taken from the manuscript's own section files: §4.3 LS, §4.7 DPL, §4.8 TPL, §4.9

5. Every equation number and inequality below was transcribed from the printed text of the read copy,
   not from a secondary summary. Where a fact is quoted from `control/FULLTEXT_FINDINGS_01/02.md`
   instead, the row says SECONDARY-SOURCE (supervisor-read) — no B01 row needed that tag except as
   cross-check, because all ten B01 files opened.
6. Prior own work checked before making any negative statement (`V-B2h`, `V-B2p`, `V-B8a`,

### Findings that bear on the manuscript

**B01-1 (supports §4.8).** Roy Choudhuri 2007 prints a **first-order** expansion in all three lags
and contains **no `τ_q²/2` term anywhere in eqs (1)–(10)**. The manuscript's §4.8 text — first-order
form, ordering `0≤τ_v<τ_T<τ_q`, "no τ_ν/2 term", second-order forms attributed to Barak/Gupta — is
consistent with the primary source. This discharges the standing item in `V-B2h`
("do not print a TPL equation without the 2007 paper in hand"): the 2007 paper is now in hand.

**B01-2 (new source for the second-order truncation).** Two independent primary papers print the
second-order law **with a first-order `τ_q` term as well**: Aldawody eq (8) and Yang eq (5)/(7),
coefficient `½τ_q²`, acting on the whole heat operator. This is the "T2" truncation already analysed
in `V-B2p`. It also settles the factor question flagged in `V-B2h` R3 for that truncation: **the
coefficient is ½ and it multiplies the entire heat operator**, as printed in Quintanilla 2009
eq (1.1) as well. Three independent sources agree; the factor is not a free choice.

**B01-3 (ordering is not universal).** Printed orderings differ by source: Roy Choudhuri 2007 states
`0≤τ_v<τ_T<τ_q`; Aldawody et al. justify second-order-in-`τ_q` truncation "since τ_θ≫τ_q≫0 (citing
Roy Choudhuri 2007)" — the opposite sense for `τ_q` vs `τ_θ`; Yang et al. print no ordering at all and
carry `(τ_q−τ_T)` and `(τ_Tτ_q−½τ_q²)` only inside their `a₁,a₂` (eq 26); Quintanilla 2009 replaces
ordering by an explicit stable window (below). The manuscript's §4.7 refusal to assert `τ_q≥τ_T` as a
universal admissibility condition is therefore **supported by the sources**, not merely prudent.

**B01-4 (a citable stability window for TPL — currently unused).** Quintanilla 2009 prints, for the
second-order TPL truncation, `(k/k*)τ_q < τ*_m < 2kτ_T/τ_q` (eq 1.2) with semigroup/exponential
stability when it holds, and instability demonstrated by exact solutions when it fails ([25] therein).
§4.8 and §6.6 currently leave TPL well-posedness "model-specific"; that remains defensible for the
*first-order* law, but the second-order truncation now has a printed, citable window and is
under-cited. (Note the window is a **different** inequality from the DPL result `τ_T>τ_q/2` recorded in
`V-B2p`; they must not be conflated.)

**B01-5 (scope of "nonlocal").** Sarkar et al. 2019 and Yang et al. 2021 both nonlocalise the
**stress only**; the heat-conduction law stays local (GN-II and DPL respectively). §7.1–7.6, §9.7 and
§11 must not describe this literature as "nonlocal heat conduction". Eq (3) of Sarkar et al. matches
manuscript eq (7.2) with `ε↔ξ`; Yang uses `μ=(e₀a)²` (a squared length) where the manuscript uses
`ξ=e₀a` — a notation collision to avoid, not an error.

**B01-6 (two-temperature ≠ Green–Lindsay, confirmed at source).** Youssef 2006 prints the relaxation
`τ₀` multiplying **both** the thermal-inertia term and the coupling term (eq 2.24), i.e. LS-type
transport in the *conductive* temperature `φ`, with `φ−T=aφ,_ii` (a>0) as the two-temperature
relation. §11.3's insistence that two-temperature theory is a distinct class from GL is consistent
with the primary source.

**B01-7 (Megahid 2023, PARTIAL — and one printed defect).** Only the abstract, symbol list and
introduction were reachable; the governing equations were not, so this paper does **not** discharge
§4.9's "unverified source lead". Two notes: (a) the text assigns **GN-II to 1993 and GN-III to 1992**,
the reverse of the primary record — Roy Choudhuri 2007's own reference list, read in this batch,
gives GN-II as `J. Thermal Stresses` 15:253–264 **(1992)** and GN-III as `J. Elasticity` 31:189–208
**(1993)**. Recorded as a labelling defect in that paper (CONDITIONAL wording, not WRONG): the
manuscript must simply not copy it. (b) §4.9's lead can be closed from a source already read FULL in
this project — Quintanilla 2019 (LIT-0041) — rather than from this bioheat application.
