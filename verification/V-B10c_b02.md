### Findings

**B02-1 (fractional-Taylor conventions differ — a trap for §11.1).** Ezzat & El Karamany (2011)
print `τ₀^α/α!` (eq 18); their own 2012 paper prints `τ_q^α/α!` **and** `τ_q^{2α}/(2α)!` (eq 17).
Neither is the `τ^α/Γ(1+α)` form that appears in other fractional-thermoelasticity work. A review
that writes "the fractional relaxation is `τ^α/Γ(1+α)`" and cites these papers is citing the wrong
normalisation. The manuscript must name the convention it uses.

**B02-2 (truncation order is a declared choice, not a property of "TPL").** Deswal & Kalkal (2015)
eq (4) is an independent printed instance of the T2 truncation — **½τ_q² multiplying the whole heat
operator** — matching B01's Roy Choudhuri 2007 / Aldawody / Yang / Quintanilla pattern. Abouelregal
(2020) eq (10) makes the point formally: the expansion keeps *independent* orders `(l,m,n,h)` on the
four lags. This is direct textual support for the manuscript's position that truncation order must be
stated model by model.

**B02-3 (MDD is applied to a single lag, not to DPL, in the primary modelling paper).** Guo, Li & Tian
apply the memory-dependent derivative to the **CV** (one-lag) flux law, eq (9), with the kernel
`[(p−t)/t_d+1]^b`. Nothing there licenses a claim that MDD "reproduces" or "generalises" DPL/TPL; the
manuscript's §4.10/§6.4 caution is the correct reading.

**B02-4 (§9.7 has a fractional precedent, but its law cannot be quoted).** Kumar & Sharma (2017)
compute reflection/transmission **energy ratios** at an elastic/piezothermoelastic interface with a
fractional flux-rate and verify energy conservation numerically. That establishes precedence for
§9.7's "energy ratios can be computed for fractional models"; it does **not** provide a quotable law
from this read (math-font failure) and the paper's numbers were not adopted.

**B02-5 (own-group papers — CONDITIONAL, and one coordination point).** Both own-group items are
recorded with neutral wording and no numbers (B11 rule 1). One substantive coordination point: the
2025 EJMSOL introduction states that the Tzou (1995) and Choudhuri (2007) models "have been found to
be ill-posed according to the Hadamard criterion". The primary sources read in B01/B02 do not support
that as a blanket statement — Quintanilla (2009) prints a stable window for the T2 TPL truncation
(and this batch's Routh–Hurwitz check confirms the window, §4 below). Recommendation: the review
should carry the model-specific qualifier rather than the blanket claim.

**B02-6 (Megahid 2023 resolved; §4.9 lead now corroborated).** Full text obtained locally. Its
eq (7) is `q_i + τ₀∂q_i/∂t = −Kθ_,i − K*v_,i` — **identical in form to the manuscript's §4.9
candidate eq (4.18)**, with `τ₀=τ`, `K=k`, `K*=k*`. §4.9 can therefore be upgraded from "unverified
source lead" to "form corroborated by a printed secondary source", with Quintanilla (2019, LIT-0041,
FULL) still the authority. The paper's printed GN-II/GN-III year inversion (noted in B01) stands as a
labelling defect not to be copied.

**B02-7 (printed defects found, recorded but not fixed).** Abouelregal (2020) eq (8) is printed as
`q(x,t+τ_q) = −K∇(x, t+τ_θ)` — the field is missing from the gradient argument (intended: `∇θ`).
Ezzat & El Karamany (2011) print the equation number (18) twice. Neither is a model ambiguity; both
should not be reproduced verbatim in the review.

## 4. Quintanilla (2009) window — independent check (ordered this batch)

A separate file, `V-B10c_quintanilla_rh.md` (+ script `V-B10c_quintanilla_rh.py`, transcript
`V-B10c_quintanilla_rh.out.txt`), reports an exact symbolic Routh–Hurwitz analysis of the quartic of
the **rigid** TPL conductor. Result: the exact stability region for all wavenumbers is
`τ*_m > τ_q·C` and `τ*_m ≤ 2kτ_T/τ_q`, where `C` is the coefficient of `∇²T`; with `C = k/k*` this
**is** eq (1.2) verbatim, and the upper bound is normalisation-independent. A numerical root sweep
flips the sign of `max Re(s)` exactly at both edges. **So the window is confirmed and the row keeps
PRIMARY-SOURCE-CHECKED, now annotated ROUTH–HURWITZ-CONFIRMED.** The instability *counterexample*
beyond linear dispersion still rests on the paper's ref. [25], which remains unopened.
