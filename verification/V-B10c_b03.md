## Result

### B03-1 — GN labelling: the literature does not fix a year→type mapping, so the manuscript must print structure

* **DRV-0363, B. Singh 2020** (*Int. J. Thermophys.* 41:97) formulates two-temperature
  porothermoelasticity "in accordance with Green and Naghdi theory of thermoelasticity without
  energy dissipation" (abstract). Printed heat equations **(6), (7)**: a single conductivity per
  phase multiplies the Laplacian of that phase's conductive temperature on the left, and the whole
  coupling group — both temperatures and both dilatations, with `F11, F12, F21, F22` and
  `T0 R11, T0 R21, T0 R12, T0 R22` — sits under a **second** time derivative on the right. The
  two-temperature relations are **(8)**, printed as conductive temperature minus thermodynamic
  temperature equals the two-temperature parameter times the Laplacian of the conductive
  temperature, separately for solid and fluid. Its single GN reference is Green & Naghdi,
  *J. Elast.* 31, 189 (1993).
* **DRV-0014, Aldandani et al. 2026** cites GN three times in one sentence — 1991, 1993, 1992 —
  with **no** type-to-year mapping attached.
* **DRV-0039, Gupta & Barak 2023** cites DPL and TPL but no GN paper at all.

*Consequence for the manuscript.* Absence of a first-order term does **not** separate GN-II from
GN-III: both are undamped. The printed discriminator is whether a second, independent
conductivity-like coefficient multiplies the Laplacian of the temperature itself (GN-III) or whether
only one conductivity per phase appears with the entire coupling group under the second time
derivative (GN-II). Singh's (6), (7) carry one conductivity per phase and therefore print the
undamped structure while citing the 1993 paper by its own label. A manuscript sentence of the form
"GN-II is the 1992 paper and GN-III the 1993 paper" is defensible only as a statement about
Green & Naghdi's own publication sequence; it is **not** how the citing literature uses the labels,
and it must not be used to classify a cited paper. The inversion already recorded in
`V-B10c_b01.md` (Megahid 2023, DRV-0089) remains a citation defect of that one paper and is not
evidence that the inversion is widespread.

### B03-2 — the `τ_q²/2` second-order coefficient is a family, not a form

Four independent printed instances of a second-order lag coefficient, all on the **flux** side:

| paper | eq | printed second-order factor | operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRV-0054 Sur et al. 2019 | (6) | `τ_q²/2!` | memory-dependent, three delay times |
| DRV-0039 Gupta & Barak 2023 | (2.8) | `τ_q²/2` | memory-dependent |
| DRV-0009 Gupta et al. 2026 | (2.15) | `τ_q²/2!` | memory-dependent, under the KG operator |
| DRV-0024 Sur et al. 2025 | (12) | `ω^q/q!`, order `N` arbitrary | memory-dependent, Taylor of order `N` |

DRV-0014 supplies the fractional twin: **(16)** carries the flux-side and both gradient-side factors
as finite sums over `m` of `τ^{mα}/Γ(mα+1)` times the `mα`-order derivative, and §2.1 states the
explicit degenerations — Biot at zero lags, LS at `n = 1`, DPL at `n = 2, p = 1`, TPL at
`n = 2, p = l = 1`, all at `α = 1`. So "second-order TPL" is one point in a two-parameter family
(truncation order, fractional order). This supports the manuscript's §4.8 position that the
`τ_q²/2` term is a **declared truncation** and not a theorem; the sentence should also say that the
truncation order is a free integer in the printed sources, which is stronger and more accurate than
"second order is a modelling choice".

### B03-3 — nonlocal operator placement: two printed classes, and the manuscript's §7 wording is too narrow

* **Stress only.** DRV-0039 on the manuscript side (`(1 − ξ²∇²)σ_ij = σ^L_ij`, manuscript
  eq. 7.2), and in the batch the same placement appears in the KG papers' constitutive step
  (DRV-0009 eq. (2.8), DRV-0014 eq. (7)).
* **Whole law.** DRV-0009 **eq. (2.15)** multiplies the *entire* memory-dependent TPL flux law by
  the full operator `1 − ξ²∇² + τ²∂²/∂t²`: the operator applies to the flux side at once, with both
  gradient groups on the right; result **(2.16)**. DRV-0014 does the same to its higher-order
  fractional TPL law at **eq. (16)** and carries the operator into the motion equation at
  **(13)**.
* **Inertia only.** DRV-0222, Lata & Singh 2020, eq. **(1)** and its plane-wave form **(10), (11)**:
  the nonlocal factor multiplies the acceleration, while the stress **(4)** is local.

A blanket statement in §7 that "the nonlocal operator acts on the stress" is contradicted by two
own-group papers in this batch. §7 and §9.7 must either restrict the claim to the Eringen
differential form used in the manuscript, or enumerate the three placements. This is also a
correction to the framing recorded in B02, which assumed the stress-only placement was generic.

### B03-4 — memory-dependent derivative: the normalisation split is confirmed and widened

* `1/ω` normalisation: DRV-0227 Li et al. 2019 **eq. (5)**; DRV-0024 Sur et al. 2025 **eq. (13)**.
* `1/R`, `R = ∫₀^τ K(s)ds`: DRV-0226 Wang & Li 2021 **Definition 1, eq. (1)**, presented by the
  operator's own authors as an improvement on the earlier form.
* Manuscript **eq. (6.7)** uses `1/ω`.

Both conventions are current, and the difference is not a typo in either source. The manuscript
sentence in §6.4 that the normalisation "must be taken from the exact model definition" is correct
and should name the two sources rather than one. DRV-0227 additionally asserts that the
memory-dependent and fractional-order models are "equivalent in theory" in a degenerate limit;
DRV-0226 argues the opposite (that the memory-dependent derivative is not a fractional derivative
and is preferable to it). The literature disagrees, so §4.10/§6.4 should report the disagreement
instead of asserting non-equivalence as settled — the manuscript's present cautious wording ("no
exact exponential-kernel reduction … is asserted") is compatible with both positions and can stay.

### B03-5 — fractional truncation conventions in the batch are Γ-normalised

DRV-0039 eq. **(2.7)** carries `τ^α/Γ(α+1)` and `τ^{2α}/Γ(2α+1)`; DRV-0006 eq. **(15)** carries
`τ₀^α/Γ(α+1)`; DRV-0014 eq. **(16)** carries `τ^{mα}/Γ(mα+1)`. This is the Sherief/Ezzat
convention, not bare powers. Consistent with B02's finding that the fractional-Taylor convention is
source-specific; a comparison table in §4.11 that normalises to bare `τ^α` would misrepresent all
three.

### B03-6 — one printed defect not to be inherited

DRV-0006 eq. **(15)** defines the fractional derivative by a three-branch rule whose `α → 0` branch
is stated as `f(x,z,t) − f(x,z,0)`. Substituting that branch into (15) does not return the Fourier
law, so the degenerate limit claimed in the surrounding text is not realised by the printed
definition. The manuscript must not repeat this branch. (Recorded as a printed defect of the cited
paper, not as a verdict on the paper's results; the branch is not used anywhere in the manuscript.)

### B03-7 — one printed admissibility condition exists in this batch

**DRV-0054, Sur et al. 2019**, quotes the thermodynamic compatibility requirement of the underlying
TPL law: the tensors `K_ij + (τ_v − τ_q)K*_ij` and `τ_T K_ij − (τ_q/2)(K_ij + τ_v K*_ij)` are
**positive semi-definite**. For isotropic `K > 0` this yields an inequality of the same qualitative
type as the DPL condition `τ_T > τ_q/2`. It also states that its memory-dependent flux law reduces
to Roy Choudhuri (2007) as the delay times vanish, matching that paper's printed first-order form.

This matters for §6.6: the manuscript must not say that admissibility conditions for TPL are never
stated in the literature. One is printed here. The four own-group papers in the batch print **no**
ordering or admissibility condition at all — DRV-0039, DRV-0006, DRV-0009 and DRV-0014 each state
their model without an inequality, DRV-0014 merely asserting in the numerical section that its
chosen lag values ensure stability. That asymmetry is the honest basis for the CONDITIONAL tags.

### B03-8 — Lata & Singh 2020 is not a generalized-thermoelasticity source

DRV-0222 heat equations **(2)** and **(8)** are the classical Fourier/Biot law (conductivity times
Laplacian equals heat capacity times the time derivative plus the coupling term); there is no
relaxation time, no phase lag and no GN term anywhere in the paper, and the nonlocal factor sits on
the inertia. It belongs to §7/§11.3 (nonlocality, two-temperature), not to §4.6 or §4.12. Citing it
as a precursor for generalized heat conduction would overstate the literature.
