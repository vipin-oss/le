## Result

### B05-1 — the Klein–Gordon operator now has its primary source, and one B04 criticism must be withdrawn

**Lazar & Agiasofitou 2022**, *Wave Motion* **114**, 103038, doi:10.1016/j.wavemoti.2022.103038,
prints the operator the own-group papers use:

* **(32)** `L = 1 − ℓ²Δ + τ²∂²_t`, with ℓ the internal length and τ the internal time;
* **(33)** characteristic velocity of nonlocality `c = ℓ/τ`;
* **(34)** the equivalent form that names it the Klein–Gordon operator;
* **(35)** `ℓ = ℓ₀ a` (a = lattice constant);
* **(36)–(38)** kernel as the retarded Green function, and its Fourier transform;
* **(39)** **constitutive law**: `L t_ij = σ_ij`;
* **(40)–(42)** equations of motion in which the operator is applied **to the inertia as well**, and
  the resulting generalized Navier equation with fourth-order time and mixed space-time derivatives.

Two consequences. First, sec.7 can cite the operator's origin directly instead of a paper that merely
uses it. Second, **B04's criticism of "inertia-side placement" needs qualifying**: in the KG
formulation, applying `L` to the inertia is part of the printed construction (eq. 40), not a misuse.
What remains a genuine inconsistency is applying the *Helmholtz* operator `1 − ℓ²Δ` to the inertia
while leaving the stress local (Tung 2019, Zang 2014 in this batch), because there `L` is not
hyperbolic and no `τ` exists. That distinction should replace the blunter wording.

### B05-2 — placement census, continued: the "mixed" class is not an own-group artefact

**Kalkal, Sheoran & Deswal 2020** (*Acta Mechanica*, doi:10.1007/s00707-020-02676-w) apply the
operator to **four** different objects in one model: nonlocal stress (1), nonlocal **couple stress**
(2), the inertia-plus-rotation group of the force equation (3) and the microrotation inertia (4) —
and then, at (5), put it outside the relaxation bracket of a Lord–Shulman flux law,
`(1 − ϵ²∇²)(1 + τ₀∂/∂t) q = K*∇θ`. This is a non-own-group paper doing what B04 recorded only from
own-group work, so the §7 taxonomy is a property of the literature, not of one group's style. It is
also the only source in B05 with a **couple stress**, which bears on §7's framing of Eringen
nonlocality as a single-field construction.

**Lotfy et al. 2025** (*Int. Commun. Heat Mass Transf.*, doi:10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2025.108692)
likewise carries the operator on both the inertia and the constitutive law, in a hydro-poroelastic
semiconductor with variable conductivity and mass diffusion — the natural §5.4–5.7 citation.

**Tung 2019** (*Vietnam J. Mech.* **41**(4), 363–371) and **Zang et al. 2014** (*Physica E*,
doi:10.1016/j.physe.2014.05.019) are the inertia-only, local-stress class, and the second is
one-dimensional (plate operator) rather than a full Laplacian. Both are §7 material only: neither has
a heat law, so neither may be cited in §4 or §6.

### B05-3 — EQ-10.12 now has a printed identity behind it

**Barak, R. Kumar, R. Kumar & Gupta 2023** (*J. Thermal Stresses*, doi:10.1080/01495739.2023.2253879)
define energy ratios as ratios of **average energy fluxes** (6.7)–(6.9) for the reflected and the four
transmitted waves, per incident wave type, at (6.10)–(6.12); then define **interaction** energy ratios
`E_st` between distinct wave fields; and then print the closure

* **(6.14)** sum of the reflected ratios + sum of the transmitted ratios + the interaction term = 1.

This is exactly the manuscript's `ΣR + ΣT + A_int = 1` in printed form, with the cross-term visible
rather than absorbed. §9.7 and EQ-10.12 can be written against this equation instead of against a
graphical sum. Own-group: neutral wording, CONDITIONAL, no numerical values taken from figures.

**Kumar, Barak, Kumari, Gupta & Ahmad 2024** (*Mech. Time-Depend. Mater.*,
doi:10.1007/s11043-023-09657-1) supplies the same average-flux definition at (6.5)–(6.7) for the
elastic/piezo-visco-thermoelastic pair and states that energy balance is analysed — a second,
independent printing of the definition.

### B05-4 — one own-group paper states unity as "close to", and that wording must not be upgraded

**Kumar & Kumari 2024** (*Int. J. Appl. Mech. Eng.* **29**(1), 54–72,
doi:10.59441/ijame/174992) reports that the sum of the energy ratios for its eight models is
**close to** unity across the range examined, at an **imperfect (stiffness) interface**. A stiffness
interface stores energy, so exact unity is not expected there; the printed claim is a numerical
observation, not an identity. §9.7/§10.5 must not cite this as proof of conservation. (Authorship is
the group's collaborators without Barak/Gupta, so the row is tagged CONDITIONAL group-adjacent.)
