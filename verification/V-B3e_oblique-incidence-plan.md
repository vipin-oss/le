## 3. Result

### 3.1 Geometry and notation

Interface at \(x=0\), normal \(\mathbf n=\hat{\mathbf x}\), tangential direction \(z\). Sagittal plane
\((x,z)\); all fields \(\propto e^{i(k_x x+k_z z-\omega t)}\). Incidence angle \(\Theta\) from the
normal; slowness \(k=\omega/c\). Conventions as in `V-B3d` (tensile-positive stress, \(s=-i\omega\)).

### 3.2 Which branches exist, and how many

**(a) Decoupling first — this is the key structural fact.** The SH mode (displacement
\(\mathbf u=u_y\hat{\mathbf y}\), strain \(e_{xy},e_{yz}\) only, \(e_{kk}=0\)) produces **no volumetric
strain and hence no thermoelastic coupling**: it satisfies \(\rho\ddot u_y=\mu\nabla^2u_y\) **exactly**,
with no \(\theta\) anywhere. Therefore:

- SH is **elastically decoupled from heat** at **any** angle, not just at normal incidence.
  *(Corollary: a thermoelastic interface problem in which SH enters the thermal boundary conditions
  is wrong.)*
- P and SV **do** couple to \(\theta\), because both have \(e_{kk}\ne0\).

**(b) Mode count for an incident P wave on one interface.**

| side | P-type (quasi-longitudinal) | SV-type (quasi-transverse) | thermal | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reflected (medium 1, outgoing \(-x\)) | 1 | 1 | 1 | **3** |
| transmitted (medium 2, outgoing \(+x\)) | 1 | 1 | 1 | **3** |

So **6 unknown amplitudes**, and the problem is square against 6 conditions (§3.3). This is the
oblique counterpart of the 4-unknown normal-incidence problem solved in `V-B3d`; the extra pair is
the SV mode on each side, which at normal incidence is uncoupled and carries no flux.

For an incident **SV** wave the count is the same (P and thermal are generated by mode conversion).
For an incident **SH** wave the count is 2 (one reflected, one transmitted SH) and no thermal mode
appears at all.

**(c) Why the thermal branch is usually negligible but must not be dropped.** The thermal wavenumber
is \(|k_t|\sim\sqrt{\omega/\kappa}\), which at \(10^{8}\) Hz exceeds the elastic wavenumber by a factor
\(\sim67\) (measured in `V-B3d` §3.2). Because \(k_z\) is conserved by Snell's law, a thermal branch
with \(|k_t|\gg k_z\) necessarily has \(k_x\approx ik_z\cdot(\ldots)\) — strongly evanescent, decaying
within a fraction of a wavelength, contributing nothing to the energy partition. **This is why
"thermal mode ≈ negligible" is a defensible approximation at ordinary frequencies — and why it fails
at the strong-coupling frequency found in `V-B3d` (4.46×10¹¹ Hz for steel), where
\(|k_t|\approx|k_e|\) and the thermal branch is a genuine propagating participant.**

### 3.3 Interface conditions (6, and why exactly 6)

Perfect contact between two solids:

| # | condition | source of the condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | \([u_x]=0\) | welded contact |
| 2 | \([u_z]=0\) | welded contact |
| 3 | \([\sigma_{xx}]=0\) | traction continuity (no surface force) |
| 4 | \([\sigma_{xz}]=0\) | traction continuity (no surface couple) |
| 5 | \([\theta]=0\) | perfect thermal contact |
| 6 | \([q_x]=0\) | no interfacial heat source; **must use the model's own flux** (`V-B3a` §3.7: LS carries \(1/(1-i\omega\tau_0)\); GN-II uses \(\partial_x\alpha\), not \(\partial_x\theta\)) |

6 conditions / 6 unknowns ✓. Two checks on the count, both falsifiable:

- If a model needs more conditions than 6 in the thermal sector (MGT: see `V-B3a` §3.6 — a third-order
  thermal operator needs 3 thermal conditions instead of 2), the system becomes 6+1 = 7 conditions
  for 6 unknowns ⇒ **over-determined**, which is the correct diagnosis for an MGT interface, not a
  reason to drop a condition silently.
- If a paper writes 5 conditions (forgetting \([\sigma_{xz}]=0\)) it will still get a number, and that
  number will be wrong; the symptom is that the energy check (§3.5) fails.

### 3.4 Conserved quantities — and what is not

**Exactly conserved across the interface:**

1. **Frequency \(\omega\)** — the interface is time-independent (fails for moving interfaces).
2. **Tangential wavenumber \(k_z=\omega\sin\Theta/c_1\)** — translational invariance along the
   interface (Snell's law). This fixes \(k_z\) for *all six* branches on both sides.
3. **Normal energy flux \(J_x\)** — the quantity that must balance (§3.5).

**Not conserved / must be computed per branch:**

- \(k_x\) for each branch: obtained from that branch's own dispersion relation with \(k_z\) fixed.
  For an isotropic thermoelastic medium this is a **polynomial in \(k_x^2\) of degree 2** (same
  determinantal structure as `V-B3d` §2.2, with \(k^2\to k_x^2+k_z^2\)), so each side has two
  \(\pm k_x\) pairs; branch selection by \(\operatorname{Im}k_x>0\) for outgoing.
- The **energy flux direction** is not generally along \(\mathbf k\) for anisotropic media (group
  velocity \(\partial\omega/\partial\mathbf k\)); for the isotropic case here it is, and \(J_x\) is the
  projection on the normal.
- **Critical angles.** A transmitted branch becomes evanescent when \(k_z\) exceeds its \(k\); its
  \(J_x\to0\) and it must be **excluded from \(T\)** but **kept in the field expansion** (it still
  contributes to traction and temperature continuity, hence to \(R\)). Omitting it from the field
  while omitting it from \(T\) is the classic inconsistency; keeping it in the field is what makes
  the 6×6 system correct beyond the critical angle.

### 3.5 Energy accounting (the acceptance criterion)

Normal energy flux, from the identity in `V-B3d` §2.4:
$$J_x=-\sigma_{xx}\dot u_x-\sigma_{xz}\dot u_z+\frac{\theta q_x}{T_0},
\qquad \langle J_x\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\big[\hat\sigma_{xx}\hat v_x^*+\hat\sigma_{xz}\hat v_z^*\big]
+\frac{1}{2T_0}\operatorname{Re}[\hat\theta\hat q_x^*].$$
\(R=(\langle J_x\rangle_i-\langle J_x\rangle_{\text{side1,total}})/\langle J_x\rangle_i\),
\(T=\langle J_x\rangle_{\text{side2,total}}/\langle J_x\rangle_i\), \(A=1-R-T\).

**Note the added cross term:** at oblique incidence the total-field flux contains
\(\sigma_{xx}\dot u_x\) **and** \(\sigma_{xz}\dot u_z\), so there are now *three* modes per side whose
mutual interference contributes — the effect quantified in `V-B3d` §3.2 grows and is no longer
optional. This is the main reason B3e is a plan and not a computation: doing it properly requires the
three-R bookkeeping of `V-B3d` §2.5 extended to 3 modes per side (9 cross terms per side).

### 3.6 What the implementation would have to check (acceptance criteria)

1. **Normal-incidence limit:** at \(\Theta=0\) the SV amplitudes must vanish identically and all
   numbers must reproduce `V-B3d`'s table to \(10^{-10}\). *This is the primary regression test.*
2. **Identical media:** \(R=0\), \(T=1\), \(A=0\), residual at machine precision.
3. **SH decoupling:** an SH incident wave must give \(\theta\equiv0\) identically and reduce to the
   classical elastic SH problem.
4. **Dissipation integrals** as in `V-B3d` §3.4, now with the \(k_x\) projection:
   \(\int\langle D\rangle dx\) against \(T\langle J_x\rangle_i\) — the \(x\)-integral of a mode
   propagating at angle \(\Theta\) involves \(k_x\), and its convergence needs
   \(\operatorname{Im}k_x>0\) per branch.
5. **Critical angle:** continuity of \(R\) and \(T\) as \(\Theta\) passes the critical angle for the
   transmitted SV branch — a jump here means the evanescent branch was mishandled.
6. **Energy partition vs angle:** \(T\) must not exceed 1 and \(A_{\rm flux}\) must stay at machine
   zero; a plot of \(R_{\rm ref},R_{\rm mode},T\) vs \(\Theta\) is the deliverable.

## 7. NOT verified / open issues

1. **No literature was opened.** The plan's formulas are self-contained, but the review will need the
   papers' conventions, and those are unknown to me.
2. **No numbers at all** — no \(R(\Theta)\) curve, no critical angles, no energy partition. Nothing in
   this file may be quoted as a result.
3. **Anisotropic (transversely isotropic, 6mm) media** are not planned in detail: the group velocity
   departs from \(\mathbf k\), so "the" angle of a branch is ambiguous and the flux must be computed
   from \(\sigma_{ij}n_j\dot u_i\) with the full tensor. This is where most published oblique-incidence
   work lives, and it is **out of scope** of this plan.
4. **Rayleigh-type surface waves** (free surface, no transmission) are a different problem and are not
   covered.
5. **Interface with a thin layer or Kapitza conductance at oblique incidence** is not planned; the
   normal-incidence version is `V-B3b` §3.3 (M1).
6. **Numerical conditioning at grazing incidence** (both branches nearly tangential) is the likely
   practical difficulty; no strategy is proposed here.
