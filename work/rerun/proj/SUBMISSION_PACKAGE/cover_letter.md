Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We submit our manuscript **"Crystal orientation versus cavity shape in transient thermoelasticity of monoclinic β-Ga₂O₃: a covariance-verified computational study with thermal-memory sensitivity"** for consideration as a full-length research
paper in the International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer.

Thermal-shock stress-concentration around holes is classical, yet the interaction of
*material* anisotropy with *geometric* symmetry in coupled transient thermoelasticity
has remained unclear. Combining a symmetry classification with a conservative
flux-form mapped-grid solver for monoclinic β-Ga₂O₃ (plane strain, a-c plane, full
13-component Cij with off-diagonal conductivity K13), we establish: (i) for a circular
cavity the peak thermoelastic hoop stress is rotation-invariant (machine-checked
discrete-rotation identity) while the field is non-axisymmetric; (ii) an equal-area
elliptical cavity converts crystal orientation into a resolvable 33–37% peak-stress
modulation, robust to grid and to numerical damping; (iii) the thermal-memory
deviation of the stress response obeys a two-parameter (relaxation, size) map — the
classical one-parameter collapse fails the pre-registered tolerance, an honest
negative result backed by post-hoc scaling tests; and (iv) thermoelastic feedback on
the temperature field is bounded and negligible (≈0.2δ). Verification (V0–V7) and
uncertainty accounting (including a partial grid-convergence outcome) are reported in
full.

To our knowledge this is the first cavity-thermoelasticity study for the ultrawide-
bandgap semiconductor β-Ga₂O₃ with crystal-orientation resolution in the finite-domain
thermal-wave setting. The work sits at the intersection of heat conduction and thermal
stress within the journal's scope.
